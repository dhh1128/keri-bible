## 1. What a Dossier fundamentally IS / IS NOT (worldview and design intent)

- **A dossier IS itself an ACDC.** "A dossier MUST be a valid Authentic Chained Data Container (ACDC)" (`spec/dossier-spec-body.md` §"Core Structure: The Dossier as an ACDC", L5). It is a usage pattern of ACDC, not a new container format.
- **A dossier is a curated bundle of evidence, not a set of direct claims.** "The primary payload of a dossier is not a set of direct claims, but rather a graph of references to external" evidence (§"The Edges Attribute: Linking to Evidence", L87; the source word "evidence" is a `[[ref: evidence]]` glossary link). The substance of the assertion enters exclusively through edges.
- **A dossier has an issuer and no issuee, and is an affidavit rather than a credential.** "It functions as a cryptographically verifiable affidavit about the composition and integrity of that collection rather than as a credential about a subject" (`spec/terms-definitions/dossier.md`, L3). The front matter: "A dossier, by contrast, has no issuee. It has only an issuer—the entity that curates the collection" (`spec/dossier-spec-head.md` §"Introducing the Dossier: An Issuer-Centric Evidence Container", L56).
- **The dossier's `a` (attributes) section is inverted relative to a normal credential.** "The `a` section MUST NOT be used to carry primary evidence" (§"The Attributes Section: Proximate Metadata", L478); it is reserved for proximate metadata, facts about the dossier itself. The spec's illustration: crash photos, a diagram and witness statements are edges; the adjuster's name, case number, assembly date and governance framework are `a`-section metadata (L482-487).
- **What the issuer's anchor attests is composition and integrity, not veracity.** There is no issuer signature on a dossier. The issuer "attests to its composition by anchoring the container in the manner just described" (§"The Role of the Issuer", L83), and "The issuer does not necessarily attest to the veracity of the claims within the evidence, but rather to the integrity and composition of the collection itself" (L83). See section 1A.
- **A dossier is intentionally heavy, designed for reuse.** "A dossier is intentionally heavy" and "is designed for reuse: curation happens once, and the resulting artifact serves as an authoritative reference for many later transactions, verifiers, and decisions" (§"Dossiers and Derivative References", L649).
- **Dossiers are persistent, evolving artifacts** with a lifecycle of "curation, iterative assembly, state management, citation, and verification" (§"The Operational Lifecycle: Creation, Evolution, and Verification", L295).
- **Design purpose:** aggregate "an arbitrary quantity and variety of evidence" (§"The Edges Attribute: Linking to Evidence", L89); a dossier MAY contain an unbounded number of edges.
- **A dossier may attest to its own negative space.** "The [[ref: manifest]] field exists because some dossiers must attest to their own negative space" (§"Attesting to Completeness", L580). In regulated filing, audit and discovery, "The consequential failure is rarely a forged document. It is an item that was required, was not supplied, and was never mentioned" (L580). See section 12A.

## 1A. Anchoring, not attached signatures

The spec states the issuance mechanism once and enforces the vocabulary through the rest of the document. This is a standardizing-line statement of the anchored-versus-paired-signature distinction; see section 15.

- **The flat denial.** "A dossier is not a signed document. The issuer does not compute a signature over the dossier ACDC and attach that signature to it as proof of authenticity" (§"How a Dossier Is Issued: Anchoring, Not Attached Signatures", L13-14). Restated for the VC audience: "A dossier is not signed; nothing is attached to it. The issuer's commitment lives in an append-only log" (`spec/dossier-spec-head.md` §"Relationship to W3C Verifiable Presentations", L95).
- **The four-step mechanism** (same section, L18-25): the assembled dossier is saidified; the issuer constructs a seal containing that SAID; the issuer anchors the seal "either directly in the key event log (KEL) of the issuer's AID, or in a transaction event log (TEL) … anchored in that KEL" (L20-23); and "The key event carrying the anchor is signed with the keys authoritative for the issuer's AID at that point in the log" (L24-25).
- **Where the signature lives.** "The signature exists on the anchoring key event, not on the dossier. What binds the issuer to the dossier is the anchor: a commitment to the dossier's SAID" (L27-28), placed in a log "that is append-only, duplicity-evident, and witnessed" (L29).
- **Why: the retrograde problem, without the word.** "An attached signature can be checked only against the key that produced it, and nothing in the signature itself establishes when it was made" (L32-34); "once that key is rotated or compromised, a verifier has no way to distinguish a signature made while the key was authoritative from one made afterward" (L34-36). An anchor "is evaluated against the key state the KEL shows was authoritative at the position where the anchoring event sits" (L36-38).
- **Framed as a requirement.** "Because a dossier is expected to be verified years or decades after issuance by parties who cannot reach the issuer, anchoring is the only mechanism that satisfies the requirement" (L39-41).
- **Three defined terms** (L43-55). To anchor is "to commit its SAID into an append-only verifiable log by placing a seal containing that SAID in an event of that log" (L45-47). Signed "means bearing an attached digital signature computed over an artifact's own bytes" (L49-50), and the spec partitions its artifacts: "key events are signed, and so are the short-lived transactional objects described under" Dossiers and Derivative References, while "Dossiers, endorsements, and other ACDCs are anchored" (L50-52). Authenticated "is used where an obligation is satisfied by either mechanism" (L53-54).
- **The one narrow exception, and its price** (§"Ephemeral Dossiers With Attached Signatures", L57-79). A dossier that "lives only for the duration of a single interaction, and that will never be verified after the issuer's next key rotation, MAY be authenticated by an attached signature" (L59-62). The consequences: it ceases to be verifiable once the key leaves the current key state; "The issuer's commitment becomes repudiable in practice" (L69); and verification at a referenceTime other than the present "is not possible" (L71-72). So an attached signature is "NOT RECOMMENDED for any dossier that is published, cited, cached, or otherwise expected to outlive the transaction that produced it" (L75-77), and "A verifier MUST NOT treat an attached signature as equivalent to an anchor" for any non-present referenceTime (L77-79).
- **Redaction is downstream of anchoring.** "Because what the issuer anchored is that top-level SAID, redaction leaves the issuer's commitment intact" and "had the issuer signed the container's bytes, any redaction would have invalidated that signature" (§"Graduated Disclosure Mechanism", L721). See section 8.
- **Terminology hygiene is the stated motivation.** "Terminology in this area is easy to get wrong, and the wrong reading has real consequences for implementers" (L9-10).

## 1B. The dossier as the root of what is presented with it

§"The Dossier as Graph Root" (L92-102) is a structural rule about a presentation, not about dossiers in general.

- **Acyclicity is derived, not imposed.** "An ACDC can therefore point only at ACDCs that already existed when it was made, and edges can never form a cycle" (L94), because an edge names its target by SAID and a SAID needs final content.
- **The gap the rule fills.** A citation names the dossier's SAID, but a verifier "that is handed a package ... may not" know which ACDC is the dossier, since "the package may hold dozens of ACDCs" (L96).
- **The rule.** Within any set of ACDCs presented together for evaluation, "the dossier under evaluation MUST be the only ACDC that no other ACDC in the set points to" (L98). A set with more than one such ACDC "is malformed, and the verifier can reject it before doing any cryptographic work" (L98).
- **Grounded in ACDC.** The ACDC specification, the body says, "already requires that the graph disclosed in any one issuance or presentation exchange (IPEX) have a single source node, its origin node" (L100), with several unchained ACDCs gathered "under a bespoke ACDC whose edges reach all of them" (L100). See section 16, item 8: on ACDC `main` that requirement is not present.
- **It does not ban other ACDCs from pointing at a dossier.** Later versions point back through `prev` or annotation edges, and other dossiers cite it; "They cannot be presented alongside a dossier they point at, unless one of them is itself the subject" (L102). If versions 1 and 2 are presented together, version 2 is the root and is the dossier under evaluation (L102).

## 2. Invariants and "never do X" rules (dossier spec)

Normative MUSTs, MUST NOTs and SHOULDs, all at `spec/dossier-spec-body.md` `6fcf84127` unless noted:
- Dossier MUST be a valid ACDC.
- Schema MUST be an `allOf` array with one member a `$ref` to the base dossier schema by SAID. The `$ref` "signals that the schema is for a dossier and should be processed using the dossier semantics defined in this spec" (§"Base JSON-Schema Definition", L110). The base dossier schema SAID is `ENroDvI_lXRUa3p1UCxU6Pxp0DWDS1yZNexCq_1TQlcj` (`schemas/dossier-base.json`, `$id`, L3; the body's example `$ref` names the same SAID, L121). The `$id` changed because the schema gained the `attest` and `manifest` properties (section 12), so a schema whose `allOf` `$ref` names an earlier base SAID (`EKuLzS_oN_mIao5og1NOujtF2QMXloiMCZP7xuAR5tY7` at `b9227753c`, or `ECpZgR2Ybj2QCfFZrnNQUqhbLnZuMl5cjL2MtgDaxMbY` before that) does not resolve to this base. The schema's own IS-A rule: "A schema S IS-A dossier iff S's top-level allOf array contains a $ref to this schema's SAID, or recursively to another schema that IS-A dossier" (`schemas/dossier-base.json`, `description`, L5).
- **Recognition may fall back to an allowlist** (§"Recognizing a Dossier", L141-149). The `allOf`/`$ref` construction is how a schema "*declares*" it is a dossier (L143), and recognizing it needs a validator that resolves `$ref` by SAID, "which is a capability rather than a given" (L143). A verifier that can resolve SHOULD recognize that way; one that cannot MAY match the schema SAID against its governed set, which is weaker because "it silently fails to recognize a conforming dossier of an unfamiliar type" (L147). Such a verifier "MUST NOT treat the allowlist as a substitute for schema validation" and SHOULD state which method it implements (L147).
- The spec states, about keripy, that "`$ref` resolution is not currently available in the most widely deployed KERI implementation" and that a draft 2020-12 schema with an `allOf`/`$ref` to the base "cannot currently be issued or verified by that stack" (L149). This is the spec's own statement about code, not `[code]` evidence; see section 16, item 10.
- MUST use the ACDC `e` (edges) section to bind the dossier to all evidenta, and MUST NOT place any evidenta in the `a` section (L134).
- MUST define `a`-section fields for proximate metadata (L133).
- SHOULD NOT include an issuee field (L136).
- SHOULD set `additionalProperties: true` at root and for the edges object (L137).
- MAY include ordinary (non-evidenta) ACDC-relationship edges (L135).
- **Default edge operators apply on evidence edges** (§"Edge Operators on Evidence Edges", L174-182). A target with no issuee (every Foreign Artifact wrapper, every Observation Attestation) "defaults to NI2I, which asks only whether the target was issued" (L178); a target with an issuee defaults to I2I. "Issuers therefore SHOULD omit `o` on evidence edges and let the defaults apply" (L182), and "A verifier MUST apply the ACDC default when no operator is present rather than treating an absent operator as an absent constraint" (L182). This agrees with the targeted/untargeted default inference on ACDC `main` recorded in `raw/03-acdc-spec.md` (§Edge, L1197-1201 at `f0bd097de`).
- **Authority binding** (§"Binding the Issuer's Authority", L184-192). A dossier SHOULD carry an edge to the credential conferring the authority it was issued under, and "The edge SHOULD be named `authority`" (L188). How much of the chain the dossier spells out is a schema choice. "A verifier MUST follow an authority chain transitively to its root" and "MUST NOT require that every hop appear as a direct edge of the dossier" (L192); a chain not terminating at a root in the acceptance policy is INVALID (L192).
- Foreign Artifact wrapper MUST be a valid ACDC with no issuee; its `a` section MUST contain `content_digest` (CESR-encoded hash) and `content_type` (IANA MIME type); `content_digest` SHOULD be a bSAID or xSAID; and the `a` section "MUST contain a `filename` field when `content_digest` holds an xSAID, and SHOULD contain one otherwise" (§"Foreign Artifact Wrappers", L251-257). The reason: an xSAID is carried in the filename, "so the filename is an input to the identifier rather than incidental packaging" (L261).
- Derivative MUST cryptographically bind to the dossier, "minimally by including the dossier SAID under whichever mechanism authenticates the derivative: the signature over a token's payload, or the resolvable identifier that constitutes a citation" (§"Dossiers and Derivative References", L672). A derivative MUST NOT be treated as independent evidence: "its trust value collapses to that of the dossier alone if the binding cannot be checked" (L672).
- When evaluating a derivative, a verifier MUST consult the dossier's revocation state "effective at the verification time, not at the time the derivative was issued" (L674). "A token issued before its referenced dossier was revoked is not valid after the revocation event" even if its own `exp` has not passed (L674). See section 16, item 12.
- Any protocol citing a dossier MUST incorporate ephemeral, context-specific signed data (timestamps, originator and destination IDs, nonces) to defeat replay (§"Replay Attack Mitigation in Citation Protocols", L697).
- To manage evidence state transitions, dossiers MUST use Annotation Edges, and verifiers MUST traverse the graph to resolve each item's effective state, "applying the latest annotations found in the chain" (§"State Management and Metadata Overlays", L319).
- To include dynamic data, issuers MUST use Temporal Pinning; "Direct links to live APIs are unverifiable in a static context" (§"Temporal Pinning", L323).
- An active "no" in joint issuance: "An active 'no' MUST therefore be expressed as an anchored [[ref: declination]], never as a null or unanchored slot" (§"Slot dispositions", L621).
- A package's ACDCs and log events "MUST be serialized as a CESR stream" (§"Presentation as a Self-Contained Package", L342), and a resource serving a dossier "MUST return a CESR stream" and "MUST NOT wrap the stream in another structure" (§"What a Citation Returns", L359). See section 4A.
- Where a manifest entry has `status` `provided`, "its `edge` field MUST name an edge that is present in the dossier's `e` section" (§"Attesting to Completeness", L586).

**Anti-pattern explicitly corrected: direct reference to non-ACDC material.** "Direct reference to non-ACDC material without a wrapper is NOT RECOMMENDED, as it places an untenable burden on the verifier" to parse and validate a foreign format (§"Referencing Non-ACDC Evidence", L202-205). The recommended approach is to wrap foreign material in a new ACDC before linking.

## 3. Incorporating evidence: three evidence categories

1. **ACDC-native evidence** (§"Referencing ACDC-Native Evidence", L157-172). The edge value MUST be a JSON object with at least `n` (SAID of the referenced ACDC, "a direct, tamper-evident link to the evidence artifact", L167-168) and `s` (SAID of its schema). Exemplified by VVP.
2. **Opaque file artifacts** (§"Foreign Artifact Wrappers", L207-271): photos, audio and video, PDFs, genomic data, spreadsheets, binaries "cannot participate in authenticated data graphs using the standard ACDC saidification algorithm, because that algorithm assumes JSON content that can be canonicalized and rewritten" (L211-213). The artifact gets a cryptographic identity through a bytewise or externalized SAID algorithm and is wrapped in a Foreign Artifact ACDC.
   - **bSAID (bytewise SAID):** for artifacts whose bytes can be rewritten (JPEG Exif, a Markdown comment). The SAID sits at a `SAID:` insertion point and is recoverable by scanning raw bytes (L223-229).
   - **xSAID (externalized SAID):** for artifacts that cannot safely be rewritten (compressed archive, encrypted file, PDF with a cross-reference table). The SAID is carried in the filename under a constraint expressed by an `XSAID:` delimiter inside the file (L231-236). The wrapper MUST then record the `filename` (section 2).
   - Fallback: a plain CESR-encoded hash in `content_digest`, where "the hash cannot be discovered by inspecting the artifact itself, only by consulting the wrapper" (L240-242).
   - "the primitive code identifies the hash algorithm, so no separate algorithm field is required" (L245-246).
   - Recording the filename is worthwhile even when it is not load-bearing, because it "is what lets a verifier report *which* artifact failed when a digest does not match" (L264-265).
3. **Foreign credentials** (W3C VC, ISO mDL; §"Bridging from Foreign Credential Ecosystems", L273-291). A bridging party verifies the foreign credential natively and issues a bridge wrapper ACDC attesting "I, the bridging party, successfully verified the attached foreign credential on date X according to policy Y" (L279-281). Two caveats: trust depends on the bridging party, and "the revocation lifecycles of the original foreign credential and of the bridge wrapper are decoupled unless a specific governance framework explicitly links them" (L289-291). The pattern "transforms the problem of verifying a foreign format into the problem of trusting the attestation of the bridging party" (L284-285).

## 4. Operational lifecycle (curation, citation, verification)

**Curation steps (normative)**, §"Curation: Assembling and Issuing the Dossier", L297-313. (1) Evidence acquisition from authoritative sources; (2) Assembly: build the edges block of named links; (3) Iterative assembly and versioning: a new version links to the previous via a `prev` edge or schema-specific equivalent, which "creates a verifiable chain of the dossier's history" (L307); (4) Issuance initiation: "For single-issuer dossiers, the [[ref: collector]] issues the fully assembled dossier ACDC by anchoring it" (L309), while for joint issuance a coordinator "coordinates the endorsement and anchoring process among the designated members" (L309); (5) Anchoring: the collector or finalizer "saidifies the dossier, places a seal containing that SAID in an event, and anchors the event in the key event log (KEL) of a KERI AID they control" (L311), directly or through a TEL that KEL anchors, and "The private keys authoritative for that AID sign the anchoring key event, not the dossier itself" (L311); in joint issuance "the anchor may be distributed across several KELs or consolidated in a finalization event" (L311); (6) Publication at a stable, publicly resolvable location, typically HTTP URLs (L313).

**Roles (leader-follower joint issuance**, §"Leader-Follower Roles", L602-607):
- **Collector:** assembles evidence artifacts, defines the initial dossier structure.
- **Coordinator:** once collection is finished, initiates issuance and distributes the candidate dossier for endorsement.
- **Finalizer:** any entity that, observing the threshold met, submits a finalization event to a KEL.
- Also **assembler** (curator; may differ from issuer), **oracle/observer** (temporal pinning), **bridging party**.

**State management, Annotation Edges** (§"State Management and Metadata Overlays", L315-319): an edge in a new dossier version pointing to an artifact (or edge) in a previous version, carrying the new state or ruling. Example: "Court Case Dossier v2" with edge `ruling_101` pointing to the SAID of `exhibit_A` (v1) with `status: "admitted"` (L319).

**Temporal Pinning** (§"Temporal Pinning", L321-330): an assembler or oracle observes dynamic state at Time T, wraps the observation in an Observation Attestation ACDC, and anchors that ACDC in a KEL. The dossier links to the static attestation, asserting that "The borrower had $50,000 in this account at the exact moment this dossier was assembled" rather than now (L330). The glossary: "An ACDC, anchored by its issuer, produced during" temporal pinning "that records a dynamic value observed at a specific time" (`spec/terms-definitions/observation-attestation.md`, L3).

**Citation** (§"Citation: Referencing the Dossier in Protocols", L332-336): dossiers "are generally not transmitted in their entirety within real-time communication protocols. Instead, they are cited" (L334). A citation MUST be a resolvable identifier enabling fetch of the complete, unmodified dossier ACDC; the canonical form is an OOBI URL in the `evd` claim of a VVP passport (L336).

**Verification outcomes** (§"Verification Outcomes", L420-430). Verification yields one of three outcomes:
- VALID: every check passed "and every credential reachable from the dossier carries a schema the verifier governs" (L424).
- INVALID: a check failed definitively, including a SAID that does not recompute, an anchor absent or made under non-authoritative keys, a chain not reaching a trusted root, revocation as of the referenceTime, an artifact whose bytes do not match its digest, or a package breaking the graph-root rule (L425). "The verifier MUST NOT act on the dossier" (L425).
- INDETERMINATE: "The structure and the cryptography are sound, but the verifier encountered something it is not competent to judge" (L426), most commonly a schema outside the governed set. The verifier "MUST NOT treat it as though it could" say the dossier is good (L426).
- The reason for the third outcome: a two-outcome verifier "must either reject those dossiers, which makes the extensibility the model depends on unusable, or accept them, which silently confers trust on credentials nobody evaluated" (L428). Precedence: "an established failure is not made uncertain by the presence of an unknown" (L430), and a verifier "MUST NOT return VALID for a dossier containing any node it could not evaluate" (L430).

**Verifier acceptance policy** (§"Verifier Acceptance Policy", L432-440). A conforming verifier operates under an explicit policy, configured in advance, that MUST state at least: governed schema SAIDs (outside them, INDETERMINATE); trusted root AIDs (a chain ending elsewhere "is not an unknown, it is a chain to the wrong root", L437, so INVALID); and a reference time rule, which states "the rule for choosing it, not a particular time" (L438). An explicit policy "is what makes two verifiers' verdicts comparable" (L440).

**Verification algorithm** (§"The Algorithm", L442-470). Inputs: a citation, a referenceTime, and the acceptance policy (L444). Ten ordered steps:
1. Fetch the dossier and its accompanying stream (section 4A), and "Record its capture time and source" (L446).
2. Validate integrity: recompute the SAID and match the citation's SAID (L448).
3. Check the graph root: where the dossier came as a package rather than by citation, confirm it is the only presented ACDC nothing points to; otherwise INVALID (L450).
4. Check governance: the dossier's schema and every credential reached in traversal must be in the governed set; otherwise INDETERMINATE (L452).
5. Determine the issuance model: `fi` in the attributes block and a threshold operator (`MxN`, `RMxN`, `MxQ`, `RMxQ`) in an edge group's `o` (L454).
6. Validate anchors (L456-460): (a) if `fi` is present and non-null, find the finalization event in that AID's KEL and verify it "carries the threshold-satisfying endorsements for the relevant operator" (L457); (b) if `fi` is absent but a threshold operator is present, a slot is Endorsed only when it references an Endorsement ACDC with `disp` `"endorse"` and an appropriate `act`, "issued by the expected endorser and anchored in that endorser's KEL" (L458), the Endorsed weights must sum to at least unity, and qualified operators also require a valid `e.qp` against `qs`; (c) single issuer: locate the event anchoring a seal containing the dossier's SAID, directly or through a TEL, and verify its signatures "against the key state the KEL establishes as authoritative *at that event's position in the log*, not against the key state current at the referenceTime" (L459), because requiring the referenceTime key state "would defeat that property" (L459); then confirm the anchoring event precedes the referenceTime; (d) only for the ephemeral attached-signature exception, verify against current key state; "A verifier MUST reject such a dossier when the referenceTime is not the present" (L460).
7. Recursive traversal: locate each referenced artifact "in the retrieved stream or presented package, fetching it separately only if it is absent" (L462), validate recursively, and follow any `authority` edge to a root named in the policy.
8. Revocation: for the dossier and every node, consult KELs or status registries for revocation "effective at the referenceTime" (L464).
9. Artifact digests: for each Foreign Artifact wrapper whose artifact is available, recompute the digest by the CESR code of `content_digest`; "A mismatch MUST fail verification" (L466). Where the artifact is unavailable, "a verifier MUST NOT treat a valid wrapper as evidence that the bytes it describes are intact" (L466) and SHOULD report it as unchecked.
10. Apply semantic rules once cryptographic validation is complete (L468).

The verifier SHOULD short-circuit on the first failing step and SHOULD retain per-step results, because "the question asked later is usually not whether verification succeeded but which checks were performed and against what state" (L470).

**Layered verification doctrine** (§"Base JSON-Schema Definition", L139): Layer 1 is cryptographic validation, universal and defined by the spec, "confirming anchors, SAIDs, and KEL consistency"; Layer 2 is semantic validation, "necessarily application-specific and requires context-dependent business logic." A generic verifier "can verify that an edge labeled 'lunarPropertyDeed' is cryptographically linked, but it cannot know what that means or how to process it" (L139). The INDETERMINATE outcome is the layered doctrine given a verdict: "the third exists because of the layered verification described under" Base JSON-Schema Definition (L422).

## 4A. Presentation, serving and the three times

**Presentation as a self-contained package** (§"Presentation as a Self-Contained Package", L338-351). Some dossiers are delivered rather than cited: a regulatory filing, a disclosure to opposing counsel, an audit package (L340). A dossier MAY be presented as "a single transferable unit carrying the dossier, every ACDC reachable from it" plus the KELs and TELs needed for key state and revocation, plus any wrapped opaque artifacts (L342). The ACDCs and log events MUST be a CESR stream; opaque artifacts travel alongside "in whatever container the parties agree on" (L342). "IPEX provides a natural exchange protocol for a package, but this specification does not mandate it" (L342).
- "A conforming package MUST be verifiable from its own contents alone, with no network access at the time of verification" (L344). A verifier SHOULD ingest a package "into a fresh, empty datastore" (L344), because "A package that verifies only against a populated datastore has not demonstrated what it appears to demonstrate" (L344). See section 16, item 11.
- "The act of delivery SHOULD itself be authenticated, separately from the dossier's own anchor" (L349): the anchor "says nothing about who transmitted it, to whom, or under what obligation" (L349).
- Durability: "The same bytes can be replayed years later, against the same algorithm and the same referenceTime" (L351).

**What a citation returns** (§"What a Citation Returns", L357-373). What a citation returns "is a package in the sense of" Presentation as a Self-Contained Package, "fetched by the verifier instead of delivered to it" (L355). The stream needs no wrapper "because every element of the stream carries its own proof" (L361). It MUST contain: (1) the dossier and every ACDC reachable from it, except deliberately withheld ones, each with the attachment locating its anchoring event (L365); (2) for TEL-anchored ACDCs, the registry inception and every event about that ACDC up to the capture time, so a revocation is included (L366); (3) the complete KEL of each AID whose anchors are checked, and of every delegator up the chain, each key event with controller signatures and enough witness receipts to meet its threshold (L367). Dependency order and no duplicates are SHOULDs that a verifier MUST NOT enforce (L369). "Including a KEL does not make its AID trusted; the verifier's acceptance policy decides that" (L371). A withheld ACDC yields INDETERMINATE for that part of the graph (L373).

**Three named times** (§"Capture Time and Reference Time", L375-383). "The capture time is when the stream was produced" (L379), for a fetcher the moment of retrieval; "The referenceTime is the moment as of which the verifier asks whether the dossier was valid" (L380); "The verification time is when the verifier runs the algorithm" (L381). The worked example: a call on 3 March, a stream fetched on 10 September, an evaluation in 2028 (L383). The glossary now adds that "The reference time is neither the moment verification runs nor an expiry date" (`spec/terms-definitions/reference-time.md`, L3) and defines capture time as "the latest moment for which the stream can show key state and revocation status" (`spec/terms-definitions/capture-time.md`, L3).

**What a stream proves** (§"What a Stream Proves", L385-392). "It cannot show that nothing happened afterward" (L387). If the referenceTime is later than the capture time, the verifier "MUST obtain the events recorded in that interval for every AID and registry in the graph, from witnesses or watchers", and if it cannot, "the outcome is INDETERMINATE" (L391). If the referenceTime is at or before the capture time, the stream holds everything the algorithm checks, but the verifier SHOULD still consult witnesses or watchers, because "a recovery rotation can supersede interaction events recorded before the capture time, and duplicity in an issuer's history can come to light later" (L392).

**Archiving** (§"Archiving a Stream", L394-398). "A stream does not record its own capture time in any verifiable form" (L396); first-seen timestamps "are unsigned assertions by the publisher" (L396). An archiving verifier MUST record the capture time and source URL and SHOULD keep signed, dated key state notices. An archived stream lets an auditor reach the same verdict on the same evidence; "It does not show whether the verdict would survive evidence that appeared later" (L398).

**Locating witnesses** (§"Locating Witnesses", L400-406). Establishment events name witnesses by AID, not address, so the stream SHOULD include `/loc/scheme` and `/end/role` reply messages (L404). A verifier that can reach none of the listed witnesses has a problem "not specific to dossiers, and this specification does not define a solution to it" (L406).

**HTTP guidance (informative)** (§"HTTP Guidance (Informative)", L408-416): path contains the SAID; 200 with `Content-Type: application/cesr`; 404 for an unknown SAID; permissive CORS for public dossiers; a strong `ETag`; and not immutable, because "its stream grows when an issuer rotates keys or a credential in the graph is revoked" (L416).

## 5. Joint issuance (the doctrinal heart of multi-party dossiers)

- **Framing** (§"Logic", L591-600): joint issuance is best understood "not as a single, uniform approach to approval, but as a family or style of approval strategies" (L592), mapping onto coordinated control in multi-agent systems. Three cooperative-control variants: leader-follower, behavior-based control, virtual structures (L594-596).
- **Contrast with group multisig** (L598): "Unlike group multisig, which requires synchronous agreement on key event log (KEL) sequence numbers, joint issuance relies on logic within the ACDC layer." "This lets members anchor their endorsements of a dossier at different times and via different channels, each in their own KEL" (L598). Members are not adding signatures to a shared object; each writes to a log it alone controls.
- **Threshold mechanics** (§"Threshold Mechanics", L609-612): a weighted threshold operator in the `o` field of an edge group; member edges are slots; each slot carries a weight in reserved `w`; the group is satisfied when weights of Endorsed slots sum to at least unity. "the same fractionally weighted threshold KERI uses for key-event signing thresholds (`kt`): the threshold itself is the fixed constant 1" (L610). For m-of-n equal endorsers each slot gets `1/m`, "so that any *m* of them sum to unity while any *m*−1 fall short" (L612). Unequal weights express weighted governance; grouped weights express nested AND/OR rules "exactly as KERI's nested `kt` lists do" (L612).
- **Weighted unity is identity with KERI, not analogy.** "how many endorsements are enough, and how much each is worth, lives entirely in the weights" (L610). A reader who understands KERI's fractionally weighted signing thresholds (`raw/01-keri-spec.md`, `bible/03-key-management-and-identifier-lifecycle.md`) needs no new mechanism, only a new placement: weights on edge slots in the ACDC layer instead of on keys in the KEL. The glossary: "the operator is satisfied when the weights of its endorsed slots sum to at least unity (1) — the same fractionally weighted threshold KERI uses for `kt`" (`spec/terms-definitions/threshold-operator.md`, L3).
- **A schema may fix the rules or defer them:** "A schema MAY fix the operator, the weights, and the set of candidate endorsers, or MAY defer some or all of these" to the instance (L612).

**Slot dispositions: only an anchor authenticates a decision** (§"Slot dispositions", L614-621). A slot points via `n` to an endorsement ACDC, names the required schema via `s`, assigns a weight via `w`, and the expected endorser "is identified by the issuer (`i`) of the ACDC the slot references" (L615).
- **Pending:** "references a placeholder meta ACDC that names the candidate endorser but that the candidate has not anchored (or the slot is null)" (L617). Contributes nothing.
- **Endorsed:** references an endorsement ACDC "issued by the candidate and anchored in the candidate's KEL, with a `disp` (disposition) of" `"endorse"` and a `said` equal to the dossier's SAID (L618). Weight added.
- **Declined:** the same ACDC, anchored, with `disp` `"decline"`. Weight not added, but it "records attributable dissent — distinguishing a candidate who was asked and refused from one who has not yet acted" (L619).
- Doctrine (L621): "a pending slot and an absent slot are equivalent in trust terms: neither attributes any act to the candidate."
- **The schema is blunter than the prose.** An endorsement "is authenticated by anchoring" and "It is not signed and carries no attached signature" (`schemas/endorsement.json`, `description`, L5). An unanchored instance "is only a placeholder reserving a slot for a named candidate; its field values are not authoritative until anchored" (same).

**Four threshold operators** (§"Threshold Operators", L623-633), distinguished by `disp`, `act` and the optional qualification-proof edge `e.qp`. All four use one endorsement schema, SAID `ECRjgun8t3cay_YBiVxEY-qXKCz37oUrvE5GvmySwxCh` (L626; matches `schemas/endorsement.json` `$id`, L3). "The operator's own name, together with the `act` of the endorsements it counts, distinguishes issuance from revocation" (L626). The `act` field stops cross-replay: "Binding the act prevents a disposition toward issuance from being replayed as one toward revocation, or vice versa" (`schemas/endorsement.json`, `a.act` description, L66).
- **`MxN`** ("M of N"): issuance, exactly N slots; each counted endorsement carries `act` `"issue"`, `disp` `"endorse"`, no `e.qp`. Candidates are enumerated structurally as slots, so the operator "embodies an *m of n* pattern without any separate enumeration of potential endorsers" (L628). Example: a judicial decision by m of n named justices.
- **`RMxN`** ("Revocation M of N"): same mechanics, `act` `"revoke"` (L629). The revocation slot set MAY be identical, overlapping or disjoint, and weights MAY differ, "so the authority to revoke can be configured independently of the authority to issue."
- **`MxQ`** ("M of Qualified"): issuance for an open-ended set of qualified endorsers; slots are added as endorsers act (L630). Declares a uniform member weight in its own `w`; satisfied "when those weights sum to unity (equivalently, when at least `1/w` qualified endorsers have endorsed)". Each counted endorsement carries `act` `"issue"`, `disp` `"endorse"` and `e.qp`. The edge group MUST carry a `qs` field naming the proof schema, chosen by the dossier creator "since the proof of qualification differs from one context to another". Models "any licensed physician in good standing."
- **`RMxQ`** ("Revocation M of Qualified"): same, `act` `"revoke"` plus `e.qp`; the qualified revoker set and weights are configurable independently (L631).
- **Declination under any of the four** (L633): the same endorsement ACDC, "anchored with `disp` `"decline"` and the matching `act`." Whether a declination under a qualified operator must carry a qualification proof "is a policy choice left to the governing schema"; a declination never adds weight.

**Finalization (`fi` field)** (§"Finalization", L635-639): an optional aid to verifiers that do not traverse, "signaled by the `fi` ('finalization identifier') field in the dossier's attributes (`a`) section, rather than by an edge operator" (L636). When present and non-null it holds the AID (typically a group AID) whose KEL is expected to carry the finalization event, where a finalizer anchors the threshold-satisfying proofs "so that the aggregate evidence is collected in one predictable place" (L638); a verifier SHOULD use it as definitive proof. When absent or null, "no finalization event is promised. A verifier MUST instead gather the endorsements from the participants' individual KELs and confirm directly that the threshold is met" (L639).

**Revocation** (§"Revocation", L641-645): defined independently of issuance, "using the `RMxN` or `RMxQ` operator" (L642). Default: "if no revocation operator is present, the threshold required to revoke a dossier is identical to the threshold required to issue it" (L644). Asymmetric thresholds are allowed, for example majority issuance with a single administrative AID able to revoke "(that slot weighted at unity on its own)" (L645).

## 6. Derivatives: Citation vs. Token (separating two conflated questions)

Two derivative forms (§"Dossiers and Derivative References", L653-670):
- **Citation:** a resolvable identifier (canonically an OOBI URL) to fetch the full dossier and run the algorithm (L655).
- **Token:** a short-lived signed object carrying enough context for an immediate decision and embedding the dossier SAID as `evd`. Sample fields `iss`, `iat`, `exp`, `aud`, `nonce`, `evd`. "The token asserts that, between `iat` and `exp`, its bearer is acting under the authority of the dossier whose SAID is given in `evd`" (L670). A verifier with a cached validated dossier MAY accept without re-traversing; one requiring fresh assurance dereferences `evd`, runs the full algorithm and caches the result.
  - A token is the exception to anchoring: "A token is one of the few artifacts in this specification that genuinely bears an attached signature" and "it can afford one because its lifetime is measured in seconds and it is never expected to outlive a key rotation" (L657). The signed/anchored partition (section 1A) is drawn on lifespan, not artifact kind.

The split separates two questions "conflated in conventional bearer credentials" (L676):
- What was attested, and by whom? This lives in the dossier, curated once, amortized.
- Who is presenting it now, in what session, under what immediate constraints? This lives in the derivative, bound through ephemeral fields (`iat`, `exp`, `aud`, `nonce`).

Derivatives inherit the dossier's revocation lifecycle, evaluated at verification time (L674; see section 16, item 12). Implementers MAY define further forms, such as a redacted summary or a blinded validity proof, subject to the same binding and revocation rules (L681).

## 7. Security and threat-model positions

**Provenance warning:** the bullets marked "(primer …)" come from `.ref/keri-primer.md`, which is untracked and gitignored (section 0, Warning 2). They cannot be pinned to any commit and are not `[spec]`. The bullets marked "(dossier …)" are from the spec body at `6fcf84127`.

- **Detection over prevention, duplicity-evident (primer §2.6, unpinnable):** "Duplicity is KERI's term for the double-spend problem in identity", a controller signing two different events with the same sequence number. Witnesses provide availability and duplicity detection; watchers poll witnesses and, on conflicting head hashes, can broadcast the conflict as proof of fraud. "Detection (rather than prevention) allows KERI to operate with low latency while ensuring that any dishonesty is provable."
- **Adversarial trust model:** "The trust model is adversarial: witnesses are assumed to be potentially malicious" (primer §2.6). Witnesses are not CAs: "A witness makes no such assertion. A witness simply stores and serves events."
- **Decentralized, plural root of trust (dossier §"Verifier Trust and Root of Trust Management", L699-703):** "The dossier model operates on a decentralized root of trust" and the verifier "makes explicit trust decisions about a plurality of evidence issuers" (L701). Verifiers SHOULD consult multiple witnesses "to ensure they have a consistent and complete view of an issuer's KEL, thereby protecting against duplicity and compromise" (L703).
- **When the verifier is the root of trust (dossier, same section, L705-707):** where a dossier goes to "the same authority that roots the issuer's credential chain", such as a regulator receiving a filing from an entity it licensed, "the verifier and the root of trust are the same party" (L705). This "removes most of the judgment from trust configuration, but it does not remove the configuration" (L707): the authority must still state its root AIDs and governed schemas, and still "depends on witnessed KELs to detect duplicity in the submitter's key history" and SHOULD apply the same multi-witness discipline (L707).
- **Distributed root of trust in VVP (Compositional Dossier, L756):** "Trust is derived from the leaf nodes (the authorities), not merely from the dossier issuer." The assembler bundles credentials from distinct domain-specific roots (LEI issuer, carrier or regulator, trademark steward).
- **Integrity via SAID (dossier §"Integrity and Non-Repudiation via KERI", L689):** "A SAID is a cryptographic hash of an object's canonical content. Any modification to the data results in a different SAID, making tampering immediately evident."
- **Non-repudiation via anchoring (same section, L691):** "Anchoring makes the act of issuing a dossier non-repudiable." "The signature is on that key event rather than on the dossier; what a verifier relies on is the presence of the commitment in a log the issuer cannot rewrite and witnesses can attest to." In joint issuance, "the collective anchors of all participating members in their respective KELs provide non-repudiation."
- **Pre-rotation firewall (primer §2.3, unpinnable; quoted with the source's `<sub>` markup):** "Pre-rotation establishes a firewall between day-to-day use and occasional governance." "If the server is breached, the attacker steals K<sub>1</sub> but cannot rotate to assume control of the identity" (`.ref/keri-primer.md`, L79). Security "comes from the asymmetry in knowledge: the controller knows or has access to K<sub>2</sub>, while the world only sees its hash" (L77).
- **Point-in-time verifiability (dossier §"Long-Term Auditability and Historical Analysis", L709-713):** "a verifier can perform validation for any arbitrary point in the past" (L711), and "An auditor can determine if a dossier and its entire evidence graph were valid at the time of a transaction, based on the key states and revocation information known at that moment" (L713). The serving section bounds this: what a verifier knows is bounded by the capture time of the stream it holds (section 4A).
- **Anchored, not paired, signatures and retrograde attacks (primer §3.3, unpinnable; section 1A carries the same argument from the spec body, pinnable):** "The credential was issued *during* the active window of a specific key... This eliminates timestamp ambiguity." Cites NIST SP 800-102: a signed message "provides no assurance that the private key was used to sign the message at that time."
- **Replay mitigation (dossier §"Replay Attack Mitigation in Citation Protocols", L695):** the dossier is stable and long-lived, so "the primary risk of replay attacks exists at the level of the protocol that cites it."

## 8. Privacy doctrine

- **Graduated disclosure (dossier §"Graduated Disclosure Mechanism", L717-721):** the ACDC SAID is computed recursively, "the hash of a parent object is derived from its scalar values and the SAIDs of its child objects" (L719), so any child "can be replaced by its SAID without altering the SAID of the parent object" (L721). Redaction is a consequence of anchoring: "redaction leaves the issuer's commitment intact: the redacted form still hashes to the SAID sealed in the issuer's KEL" (L721). Joint issuance: "redaction does not affect the validity of member seals anchored in KELs, as those seals point to the immutable SAID of the root dossier" (L721).
- **Withheld ACDCs in a served stream:** the stream may omit ACDCs the dossier deliberately withholds, and the verifier returns INDETERMINATE for that part of the graph (§"What a Citation Returns", L365, L373). Graduated disclosure therefore costs a VALID verdict on the withheld subgraph.
- **Selective disclosure (primer §3.5, unpinnable):** payload sections each with a random salt and digest; the holder reveals only chosen sections.
- **Right to be forgotten (primer §3.5, unpinnable):** "the KEL stores only the anchors (hashes) of the credentials"; deleting the payload leaves an irreversible hash. Contrasted with blockchain, where "'forgetting' an identifier requires a hard fork of the entire chain."
- **Correlation vectors (dossier §"Analysis of Data Correlation Vectors", L723-729):** the dossier SAID (persistent unique identifier); the citation signer AID (links all messages it signs); explicit brand information (intentional correlator).
- **Mitigations (dossier §"Mitigation Strategies for Unwanted Correlation", L731-737, and §"Contractually Protected Disclosure", L739-741):** rotate the citation-signing AID independently of the issuer AID; maintain a pool of AIDs for herd privacy; use a blinding service that issues a short-lived derivative attesting validity "without revealing its SAID to the end verifier" (L737); serve a redacted version to anonymous requests and require a signed request for the expanded version, creating an audit trail (L741).

## 9. Anti-patterns and outsider-tells the material explicitly corrects (from primer)

**Provenance warning: this section is sourced from `.ref/keri-primer.md`, untracked and gitignored (section 0, Warning 2). None of it is pinnable and none of it is `[spec]`.** It is a Hardman working paper, and the same arguments appear in the tracked papers corpus (`/home/daniel/code/me/papers/x509-prob.md`, `was.md`), which synthesis should prefer. The VC/VP contrast has a pinnable `[spec]` source in the spec's front matter; see section 14A. Not re-read this pass.

These are the doctrinal reframings against PKI, blockchain and VC priors:

- **PKI/X.509 root-of-trust flaw:** "The fundamental flaw... is the separation of the identifier from the cryptographic keys that control it." In X.509 an identifier "is a lease entry in a database"; binding is "merely an assertion — a digital certificate — signed by a third party," creating "a root of trust that is external to the identity itself." KERI reframing: "The identifier is the root of trust", and binding must be "mathematical (and therefore, objectively provable and permanent), not administrative."
- **CA fragility:** "If a CA is compromised, coerced, or negligent, it can issue valid certificates that assert bindings that are not true" (DigiNotar, Symantec distrust, TrustCor). "the security of the leaf (the user) is entirely dependent on the security of the root (the administrator)."
- **Jurisdictional non-universality of CA trust:** "a CA-based ecosystem has different trust profiles in different jurisdictions" (SHAKEN not accepted in Europe/Asia). The CA/Browser Forum is "a new centralization."
- **Revocation-list critique (CRL/OCSP):** "X.509 standard also lacks a native, low-latency, enforceable mechanism for revocation." CRLs "heavy, bandwidth-intensive... scale poorly." OCSP "requires the client to query a central responder for every validation, creating a privacy leak (the CA knows every site you visit)." Fail-open: "browsers often 'fail open', meaning they accept the unverifiable certificate as valid." KERI requirement: "Revocation is immediate and safe... without privacy leaks or 'fail open' risks."
- **CT is detection, not prevention, and re-centralizes:** "CT is a detection mechanism, not a prevention mechanism; fraudulent certificates may be usable for a time." And "the implementation of CT has introduced a new form of centralization."
- **The persistence and identity-continuity problem:** certificates rotate to a "mathematically unrelated" new cert; "The only link between them is the administrative procedure of the CA." ACME continuity "exists only in the eyes of the CA." Requirement: "Identity survives key rotation... without having to trust a party who claims it's safe to ignore a gap."
- **KERI is not a blockchain (rejection of global consensus):** "Traditional blockchains rely on a global consensus model, where every node must agree on the total ordering of all transactions." "KERI rejects the need for global consensus regarding identity. Instead, it uses microledgers called key event logs (KELs)." "The ordering of events matters only relative to that specific identifier." "KERI naturally scales horizontally because there is no central choke point." "Hacking incentives decay because any breach is likely to be limited in scope to a single identifier."
- **An AID is not a domain name or UUID:** "Unlike a domain name, which is rent-seeking text, or a UUID, which is arbitrary entropy, an AID is a self-certifying identifier (SCID)."
- **SAID vs. UUID:** "The problem with a UUID is that it has no relationship to the data." A SAID is content-addressable: "An ACDC is not a mutable document; it is a crystallized fact."
- **Multisig: KERI's innovation is exposing, not inventing.** In X.509/PKI multisig governance "is not knowable or verifiable by the public... This makes it impossible to distinguish between sloppy and robust management of secrets... Hackers thrive on opacity." KERI "requires a definition of the identifier's fractionally weighted thresholds directly in the public KEL." "KERI's real innovation in multisig is less about inventing and more about exposing: multisig is optional and infinitely variable, but defining and following policy about it is required."
- **JSON canonicalization and JSON-LD critique (CESR rationale):** `{"a":1,"b":2}` vs `{"b":2,"a":1}` "are semantically identical but have different cryptographic hashes." JCS "normalization logic is brittle." "In complex environments like JSON-LD, this fragility has led to 'term redefinition' vulnerabilities, where the meaning of a signed credential can be altered without invalidating the signature."
- **W3C VC / JWT / X.509 use paired signatures**, against ACDC anchored signatures. Foreign VCs belong as wrapped or bridged evidence, not native roots of trust. A pinnable `[spec]` source exists for this in the spec's VP comparison table, section 14A.

## 10. Precise terminology and definitions

**Provenance warning: the definitions below are from the untracked `.ref/` documents (section 0, Warning 2) and are not pinnable. Not re-read this pass.** The Dossier spec has its own tracked glossary, `spec/terms-definitions/*.md` (29 term files at `6fcf84127`), which is pinnable. It covers the dossier-specific vocabulary (annotation-edge, assembler, bridge-wrapper, bridging-party, capture-time, citation, collector, coordinator, curator, declination, derivative, dossier, edge, endorsement, evidentum, finalization-identifier, finalizer, foreign-artifact, foreign-artifact-wrapper, manifest, observation-attestation, operator, oracle, predicate-edge, proximate-metadata, reference-time, revocation-operator, temporal-pinning, threshold-operator) but not the KERI/ACDC/CESR base vocabulary below. Quotes from the tracked glossary appear in sections 1, 4, 4A, 5 and 12A.

From primer and refs (unpinnable):
- **AID (Autonomic Identifier):** a self-certifying identifier (SCID) derived cryptographically from initial key(s). Two derivation modes:
  - Basic derivation: identifier = digest of a single public key; non-rotatable, "suitable only for ephemeral, short-lived use cases."
  - Transferable derivation: identifier derived from the entire inception event (initial keys plus pre-rotation commitment); "The identifier string remains constant even as the keys change."
- **KEL (Key Event Log):** "the authoritative source of truth for an AID," an append-only signed chain of events; a microledger, verified by replay from inception. Event types: inception (`icp`), rotation (`rot`), interaction (`ixn`).
- **Establishment events:** icp and rot; carry `k` (public keys) and `kt` (signing threshold).
- **Pre-rotation:** committing now (via hash of K2) to the next key; revealing K2 only at rotation and re-committing to K3.
- **`kt` fractionally weighted threshold:** list(s) of fractional weights; a clause is satisfied when the sum of signing weights is at least 1.0; lists of lists give AND/OR. Example `[["1/2","1/2","1/2"], ["1/3","1/3","1/3","1/3"]]`.
- **Signature indexing (CESR):** each signature attachment carries a code naming which key in `k` produced it.
- **Witness:** "a server designated by the controller to store and serve the KEL"; signs receipts. A witness threshold (e.g. 2 of 3) makes an event stable.
- **Watcher:** run by verifiers or auditors to poll witnesses and detect duplicity.
- **SAID (Self-Addressing Identifier):** content-addressable hash embedded in the `d` field by the dummy-character derivation.
- **ACDC (Authentic Chained Data Container):** chained, so a verifiable DAG; fixes the "copy-paste vulnerability."
- **Anchored signature:** a provable KEL association (issuer digest sealed in a KEL event), against a paired signature.
- **TEL (Transaction Event Log):** a log for credential status and revocation; "ACDCs anchored in TELs allow for real-time status checks without privacy leaks."
- **IPEX (Issuance and Presentation Exchange):** `exn` messages `apply`, `offer`, `agree`, `grant`. Mutual anchoring "prevents spam (you can't force a credential into someone's wallet) and provides non-repudiation of receipt."
- **Endorsement (issuance-ref §4):** "a signature provided by an AID that is not the controller of the KEL", external and non-mutating. "You can add 100 endorsements to a single KERI event... These endorsements do not change the digest of the event." This is a different sense from the spec's endorsement ACDC (section 5), which is anchored, not signed. A reader should not carry the issuance-ref sense into the spec.
- **CESR (Composable Event Streaming Representation):** self-framing via a framing-code prefix; concatenation composability across Base64 and binary; pipelining and crypto agility. Sample codes: `A` = Ed25519 seed (44), `E` = Blake3-256 digest (44), `0B` = Ed25519 signature (88), `-A##` = pipeline group counter.
- **DKMI (Decentralized Key Management Infrastructure):** what the stack enables.
- **OOBI (Out-of-Band Invitation):** resolvable URL to a resource serving an ACDC and its KERI proofs; the canonical dossier citation. The spec now says what that resource serves (section 4A).

**ACDC edge operators (operators-ref, unpinnable; the ACDC specification itself is the citable source, see `raw/03-acdc-spec.md`):**
- Reserved edge field labels: `d`, `u`, `n`, `s`, `o`, `w`.
- **Unary (edge-level):** `I2I` Issuer-To-Issuee, `NI2I` Not-Issuer-To-Issuee, `DI2I` Delegated-Issuer-To-Issuee, `NOT`. The operators-ref calls `I2I` the default; the spec body (section 2) and ACDC `main` say the default depends on whether the far node has an issuee: `I2I` if targeted, `NI2I` if not.
- **M-ary (edge-group level):** `AND` (default), `OR`, `NAND`, `NOR`, `AVG`, `WAVG`.
- Doctrine: "ACDCs act as fragments of a distributed property graph. Operators allow the graph to perform 'reasoning'."

## 11. Worked examples and use-case architectural patterns (dossier §"Use Cases and Architectural Patterns", L743-812)

All seven are non-normative: the Scope section lists "non-normative implementation guidance through use case profiles" among what is in scope (`spec/dossier-spec-head.md` §"Scope", L131). The abstract still names only three domains, "telecommunications, law enforcement, and investigative journalism" (`spec/dossier-spec-head.md` §"Abstract", L25).

- **VVP, Compositional Dossier** (§"Verifiable Voice Protocol (VVP): The Compositional Dossier", L747-756): assemble a "permission slip" from independent authorities; distributed root of trust; the verifier recursively checks issuers of edge credentials.
- **Law enforcement and adjudication, Procedural Dossier** (L758-766): Phase 1 (Investigation) an immutable "bag" of artifacts; Phase 2 (Adjudication) state changes (`Marked`/`Offered`/`Admitted`/`Stricken`) through Annotation Edges. To strike, the Clerk issues a new version with an edge targeting the original SAID and `status: "stricken"`, preserving the original for appeals. The text still points at "Section 10.2" for annotation edges (L766), a numbered cross-reference that does not match the current heading structure.
- **Investigative journalism, Redacted Dossier** (L768-776): private graph (unredacted Source Asset) and public graph (Redacted Asset) linked by a "blinded" edge, using Cross-File Association "precursor" relationships.
- **Mortgage qualification, Snapshot Dossier** (L778-784): Temporal Pinning with an oracle or observer; the Observation Attestation, "which the observer anchors in its own KEL" (L784), says "I observed Account X having Balance Y at Block Height Z."
- **Clinical trials, Predicate Dossier** (L786-794): a Predicate Edge pointing to a ZKP or derived claim; the verifier validates the proof, not the document.
- **Petition, Open-Endorsement Dossier** (§"The Petition: The Open-Endorsement Dossier", L796-803): uses `MxQ`, "enough qualified, unique endorsements that their weights sum to unity (with a uniform per-member weight `w`, that means at least `1/w` endorsers)" (L802); participants issue a qualified Endorsement ACDC and anchor it in their own KELs; the coordinator may set `fi` and finalize.
- **Regulatory filing, Attested Submission Dossier** (§"Regulatory Filing: The Attested Submission Dossier", L805-812). The shape: a checklist fixed by the authority, a named individual who assembles and certifies, and "the authority that receives it is the same body that authorized the filer to act" (L807). The distinguishing feature is "not the evidence but the enumeration" (L810): a `manifest` and an `attest` declaration (section 12A). Documents are wrapped, typically by xSAID "since filings are dominated by PDFs whose bytes cannot be rewritten without breaking them" (L811); an `authority` edge binds the filer's role credential; the filing travels as a self-contained package with the submission separately authenticated (L811). The authority is both root of trust and relying party, so "a credential minted under a schema it never defined is correctly INDETERMINATE rather than refused" (L812). Self-containment "is what makes the filing auditable rather than merely received" (L812). This profile ties together sections 1B, 2 (authority binding, filename), 4 (outcomes, policy), 4A (package) and 7 (verifier as root).

## 12. Proximate metadata fields (dossier `a` section, all optional unless a schema requires)

§"Standard Proximate Metadata Fields", L494-576. All thirteen listed fields are also carried in `schemas/dossier-base.json` under `a.properties` (L61-185), together with `fi`.

`assembly_dt` (ISO 8601 assembly time; distinct from envelope issuance date), `assembler` (AID or name of the curator, useful when it differs from the issuer), `purpose` (human-readable, not machine-interpreted), `ref` (external case, docket or transaction ID, untyped), `gov` (SAID or URI of the governance framework), `evt_dt` (ISO 8601 time of the documented event, distinct from `assembly_dt`), `evt_loc` (where the event occurred; no mandated format; SHOULD follow domain standards such as ISO 6709), `jur` (ISO 3166-1 alpha-2, optionally with 3166-2; MAY be an array), `cls` (type or category of matter; domain controlled vocabulary), `phase` (procedural maturity; a new version SHOULD be issued on phase transition), `gov_rules` (SAID or URI of the specific evidence-collection ruleset; `gov` says who oversees, `gov_rules` what procedural constraints applied), `attest` and `manifest` (section 12A). ## 12A. Attestation and completeness: `attest` and `manifest`

- **`attest`** (L569): "A declaration the issuer makes in their own voice about the collection, as part of the act of issuing it", an object with `statement` and a boolean `confirmed`. "The boolean is not redundant with the issuer's anchor" (L569): the anchor says the issuer issued the dossier, while `confirmed` says whether the issuer adopted the declaration, "so a dossier can carry a required declaration and record that it was withheld" (L569). Where it has legal effect, `gov` or `gov_rules` SHOULD identify the framework that supplies the wording.
- **`manifest`** (L571-576): "An enumeration of the components the dossier is expected to contain, keyed by an identifier drawn from the governing framework" (L571). Each entry has a `status` and optionally `name`, `reason`, `due` and `edge`. Defined statuses: `provided` (with `edge`), `pending` (with `due`), `not_applicable` (with `reason`), `withheld` ("required, is not supplied, and is not promised", L576). Implementers MAY define others. The glossary: an enumeration of expected components "including those that are absent — so that an omission is an attributable claim by the issuer rather than an inference from silence" (`spec/terms-definitions/manifest.md`, L3).
- **Why it matters** (§"Attesting to Completeness", L578-586). Without a manifest a dossier "cannot distinguish the three cases a supervising authority most needs to tell apart" (L582): absent because inapplicable, still coming, or withheld by choice. Because the manifest is inside the dossier, "the issuer's anchor commits to it exactly as it commits to the evidence graph" (L582). "An issuer who omits a required item and marks it `not_applicable` has made a false statement that survives in a duplicity-evident log" (L582).
- **It does not break the evidence-in-edges rule.** "The entry carries no evidence; it carries the issuer's account of what the collection was supposed to contain" (L584). A verifier ignoring the manifest still recovers the full graph and loses only the completeness claim.
- **Manifest processing is semantic, not cryptographic.** Verifiers "SHOULD treat manifest processing as semantic validation rather than cryptographic validation" (L586); whether a `withheld` item is acceptable is policy. The one hard rule is the `provided`/`edge` consistency MUST (section 2).
- **Schema encoding.** In `schemas/dossier-base.json` the manifest entry requires only `status` (L147-185); the `edge` property's description says it is "Required when status is provided, and must name an edge present in the dossier", but no conditional schema encodes that, so the MUST is enforced by verifier logic, not by schema validation. `due` is typed `"format": "date-time"` while its description says "ISO 8601 date by which a pending component is expected"; a validator asserting formats would reject a bare date.

## 13. Escrow model and why joint issuance is an evolution (issuance-ref)

**Provenance warning: sourced from `.ref/issuance-ref.md`, untracked and gitignored (section 0, Warning 2); not pinnable, not `[spec]`. Not re-read this pass.** The claims about keripy's escrow machinery are `[code]`-shaped claims with no `[code]` citation and should be re-sourced against keripy at a named commit (`raw/09-keripy-code.md`) before any chapter rests on them. The final bullet's doctrine, that issuance is anchoring, is stated in the Dossier spec itself and should be cited there; see section 1A.

- **Group AID:** a multisig entity whose control keys are member AIDs; every Group KEL event must be signed per the group's `kt`.
- **Wait-for-Signature (WFS) Escrow:** partial-signature events held in a "Partial Signature Escrow," "effectively 'invisible' to the authoritative state of the KEL," until weights reach threshold, then "promoted" to the KEL.
- **Core limitation, synchronous serialization:** "Because KERI events are chained by digests, the group must coordinate on the *content* of the event before signing." "This 'tight' coordination creates high friction in distributed environments." This motivates the loose, ACDC-layer, endorsement-based joint issuance where the artifact is fixed and members endorse its SAID asynchronously.
- **Issuance is anchoring** (issuance-ref §3.1): "an 'Issuance' is not just a signature on a document. It is the act of Anchoring or Sealing the credential's digest (SAID) into the Issuer's KEL."

## 14. Engineering-mindset doctrine (primer §5)

**Provenance warning: sourced from `.ref/keri-primer.md`, untracked and gitignored (section 0, Warning 2); not pinnable, not `[spec]`. Not re-read this pass.**

- Resilience via pre-rotation and weighted multisig makes identity "antifragile. It gets stronger with more keys, not more complex."
- Verification cost is O(n) in history but O(Δn) in practice via incremental verification and cached state.
- Mental-model shift: "We stop thinking about certificates (static assertions) and start thinking about event streams (dynamic histories). We stop building admin panels for identity and start building agents that manage keys. Trust is not granted by a corporation or a government, but established by the mathematical consistency of the identifier itself."
- Quantum posture: rotation authority stays hidden behind a hash until used and discarded; hybrid governance (ECC and post-quantum signatures in one threshold): "The ecosystem has a straightforward, calm response to any quantum apocalypse."

## 14A. Spec front matter: scope, evidence lifespans, and the W3C VP comparison

`spec/dossier-spec-head.md` is tracked and pinnable, and carries three things the body does not.

**Scope** (§"Scope", L127-133). In scope: "normative requirements for dossier structure and schema; joint issuance, operator semantics, and revocation; privacy-preserving and graduated disclosure; and non-normative implementation guidance through use case profiles" (L131). Out of scope: "transport or citation protocols for presenting dossiers at transaction time; storage and hosting requirements for dossier artifacts" (L133), among others. The replay requirement is therefore phrased as an obligation on the citing protocol rather than on the dossier. The body now specifies what a serving resource must return; see section 16, item 9.

**Evidence lifespans, the premise behind anchoring** (§"Evidence Lifespans and Verification Timing", L35-51). Three classes: temporary ("A movie ticket, a JSON Web Token, a browser session cookie, or a transient network message carries authority for seconds or minutes", L39); changed occasionally ("A PIN, a password, a credit-card number, an X.509 certificate, or a magnetic key card", L41); and effectively permanent ("A birth certificate, a passport, articles of incorporation, a fingerprint or iris template, or a chain-of-custody record", L43). "A dossier inhabits the permanent end of this spectrum" (L45), verified "in front of audiences and against questions the issuer cannot anticipate at issuance time" (L45). Three consequences (L49-51): "The artifact must stand alone"; freshness is handled "through per-edge metadata and" temporal pinning, "not through a single global expiry"; and "State must be reconstructible at an arbitrary historical point", "supported by KERI's historical-query capability over its KELs". This premise is what makes the body's "anchoring is the only mechanism that satisfies the requirement" (section 1A) an argument rather than an assertion.

**Dossier vs. W3C Verifiable Presentation** (§"Relationship to W3C Verifiable Presentations", L77-99). A nine-row comparison table. The authentication row: "Issuer anchors the dossier's SAID in a KEL; nothing is attached to the container itself" against, for a VP, "Holder attaches a digital signature or other proof to the presentation" (L91). The lifecycle gloss: a VP is "created in response to a verifier's request, signed by the holder, presented once, and discarded", while a dossier is "assembled in advance, issued by the curator (not the subject) through an anchor in the curator's KEL, published at a stable location, and referenced repeatedly" (L93). The spec names this as the most likely reader error: "The row on authentication deserves emphasis, because it is the single easiest thing for a reader arriving from the VC world to get wrong. A dossier is not signed; nothing is attached to it" (L95). The fair-minded half: "These differences do not put the two models in opposition. A dossier MAY reference a W3C VC as one of its evidence items through the bridging mechanism" (L99). The lifecycle row's "published at a stable location" describes the citation case; the body also allows delivery as a package (section 4A).

**Subject model** (L97): "A VP is fundamentally a statement by a subject about themselves: 'I hold these credentials.' A dossier has no issuee." Many dossier use cases "do not have a single subject in the VC sense at all" (L97).
