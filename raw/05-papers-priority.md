## 1. What KERI/ACDC/CESR fundamentally IS (worldview, design intent, root of trust)

- **The root-of-trust move.** KERI shifts the root of trust "from administrators to self-certifying identifiers" (wtbo abstract, L6 @`181569b64`). "KERI's foundational move is to make the identifier self-certifying." (wtbo §4, L91 @`181569b64`). Trust is not vested in a network or service provider "but in the immutable, cryptographic proof contained within the data structure itself." (kspqs §A New Foundation for Trust, L52 @`181569b64`).
- **CT bottoms out in administrators; KERI bottoms out in mathematics.** "CT's chain of assurance bottoms out in administrators… KERI's bottoms out in mathematics: the self-certifying identifier derives from the initial public key, and the KEL's validity can be checked by anyone who replays its hash chain, without querying any authority." (wtbo §3, L72 @`181569b64`).
- **Self-certifying = self-contained verification.** "the identifier itself contains all the information needed to verify its authenticity. It is cryptographically derived from its controlling public keys" (kspqs). An AID is a **self-certifying identifier (SCID)** — "derives cryptographically from the initial public key (or set of keys) that controls it." (primer §2.1). Contrast: a domain name is "rent-seeking text," a UUID is "arbitrary entropy" (primer §2.1).
- **Ledgerless / no global consensus.** "KERI adopts a more direct and efficient ledgerless approach. Its trust basis is rooted in data structures made verifiable with straightforward cryptographic primitives, rather than the social and algorithmic consensus of a distributed network." (kspqs). "KERI rejects the need for global consensus regarding identity." (primer §2).
- **Minimal sufficiency.** KERI is "a decentralized key management infrastructure (DKMI) designed around the principle of minimal sufficiency." (kspqs). Uses "building blocks that have already been rigorously audited, formally verified, and accepted by governments and industry for years" rather than "exotic cryptography."
- **Portability of trust / ambient infrastructure.** "Because KELs are self-validating, they can be stored and served by any 'ambient infrastructure' — a simple web server, a distributed hash table, or even an ordinary USB drive… The underlying infrastructure becomes a replaceable commodity." (kspqs). This "decouples the root of trust from any single point of failure."
- **Identity = sameness, not privilege.** "identity means *sameness*; it's what holds constant across contexts. On the other hand, privileges are dynamic." (x509-prob §Lifespans). "Identity is different. It means *sameness*: the thing that holds constant across contexts." (wtbo §1). Core doctrinal wedge: certs are *dynamic privilege mechanisms*, identity is *stable sameness*.
- **The stack division of labor.** KERI = identity/"Who"; ACDC = data/"What"; CESR = "the language they speak," a serialization format "designed specifically for the constraints of cryptographic transport." (primer §3, §4). "They replace administrative trust with cryptographic trust, enabling a Decentralized Key Management Infrastructure (DKMI)." (primer §1.3).
- **ACDC is a methodology, not just a format.** "ACDCs are not just a format — they're a *methodology* for creating verifiable evidence." (x509-prob §Much better alternatives). ACDCs are *issued by* and *issued to* AIDs.
- **Identity 3.0 framing.** DIDs/VCs = "second-generation" (identity evidence 2.0); ACDCs = "identity evidence 3.0 since December 2022, when the first global-scale systems went to production." (x509-prob). PKI's "issue to keys" paradigm is the 40-year-old baseline being upgraded.
- **Crypto-agility as the ultimate defense.** "the ultimate defense is not a premature bet on a single algorithm, but an architecture built for adaptation. KERI's true quantum-readiness lies in its foundational cryptographic agility." (kspqs conclusion). "an abstract state machine for managing keys; the signature and hashing schemes are simply the tools it uses."
- **Mental-model shift (the doctrine's summary imperative).** "We stop thinking about certificates (static assertions) and start thinking about event streams (dynamic histories). We stop building admin panels for identity and start building agents that manage keys." "Trust is not granted by a corporation or a government, but established by the mathematical consistency of the identifier itself." (primer §5).

## 2. What KERI/ACDC/CESR IS NOT (explicit disclaimers of scope)

- **NOT a blockchain / not global consensus.** "KERI is distinct. Traditional blockchains rely on a global consensus model, where every node must agree on the total ordering of all transactions." (primer §2). De-emphasizing consensus "deprives hackers of a single, easy-to-find, globally relevant target and eliminates entire classes of blockain [sic] vulnerabilities, such as 51% attacks or smart contract exploits." (kspqs §A New Foundation for Trust, L38 @`181569b64`). *[Fidelity correction 2026-09-15: the source reads "blockain"; earlier revisions of this note silently corrected it to "blockchain". The typo is present at both `f94f50f` and `181569b64` and is the author's, not a transcription error here.]*
- **NOT an encryption protocol.** "KERI is a protocol for managing identity, not for encrypting communications. This deliberate separation of concerns…" (kspqs §Securing the Conversation). The separation is what lets applications adopt the best confidentiality solution independently.
- **NOT duplicity-*resistant* — duplicity-*evident*.** "KERI's security posture is best described as *duplicity-evident* rather than *duplicity-resistant*. It doesn't try to make duplicity impossible — that would require global consensus… It makes duplicity *provable and attributable*." (wtbo §4, L113 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin — this is the quote under `keri-doctrine.md` load-bearing claim 3.**
- **NOT prevention — detection.** "Detection (rather than prevention) allows KERI to operate with low latency while ensuring that any dishonesty is provable." (primer §2.6). Both CT and KERI "are fundamentally detection-oriented. Neither prevents a bad actor." (wtbo §3).
- **Witnesses are NOT CAs.** "A witness makes no such assertion… A witness simply stores and serves events." (primer §2.6). "Witnesses make no assertion about identity — they don't claim 'this key belongs to this entity.'… That is a fundamentally different role than a CA." (wtbo §4, L105 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin — this is the quote under the `keri-doctrine.md` shibboleth row "A witness is a CA / a blockchain validator / a notary."**
- **NOT a drop-in replacement for all PKI today.** "This is not an argument that KERI's decentralized PKI is a drop-in replacement for classical PKI everywhere, today. It's not that simple." (wtbo §6, L155 @`181569b64`). Constraints are "of *youth*, not of *architecture*." (same paragraph).

## 3. Security & threat-model positions

### Survivability, not invulnerability; detection & recovery as first-class
- "no system can guarantee resistance to all intrusions, and that detection and recovery are therefore first-class design requirements, not fallbacks." (wtbo §3, citing CMU SEI survivable-systems work [25]).
- KERI is "designed for resilience. It anticipates the possibility of key compromise and provides an elegant, built-in mechanism for active, verifiable recovery." (kspqs). Pre-rotation makes control "fundamentally resilient, not just resistant, to quantum attack." (kspqs — key phrase: **resilient, not resistant**).
- CAP-theorem stance: "KERI explicitly chooses availability and partition tolerance, and handles the consistency gaps through detection." (wtbo §5). Guaranteeing prevention "requires global consensus at a cost in latency and scalability that both systems deliberately decline to pay." (wtbo §3).

### Zero-trust / malicious-controller / adversarial-witness stance
- "The trust model is adversarial: witnesses are assumed to be potentially malicious." (primer §2.6).
- "KERI doesn't prevent a malicious controller from trying to fork their identity history. This is explicit in the specification, and it is the right tradeoff given KERI's scale and availability goals. The watcher layer is the designed response." (wtbo §5). The real question is "not 'can duplicity occur?' — it can — but 'how quickly is it detected, and what happens when it is?'… a deployment question, not a protocol question."

### Local / observer-dependent validity (watchers as observation, not enforcement)
- **Watcher = observation; browser root program = enforcement.** "A browser vendor that distrusts a CA is making an enforcement decision — exercising judgment with immediate global consequences… A KERI watcher that detects duplicity is making an observation — surfacing cryptographic evidence that the relying party confirms independently." (wtbo §4, L111 @`181569b64`).
- "Watchers cannot unilaterally 'distrust' an identifier the way a browser root program can distrust a CA. The worst a dishonest watcher can do is stay silent about duplicity it has seen — a risk addressed by running your own watcher or consulting multiple independent ones." (wtbo §4, L111 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin — this is the quote under the `keri-doctrine.md` shibboleth row "A watcher enforces / can distrust an identifier."**
- **Local-resolver pattern is the default and the recommendation.** A watcher "can be (and by default usually is) as simple as an in-process component in a verifier's own software stack that queries multiple witnesses directly before reporting any result. In this model, the verifier trusts its own code… KERI's architects explicitly recommend this local-resolver pattern." (wtbo §5, L131 @`181569b64`). A "super watcher" (shared archival service) is a *supplementary* pattern; "Depending only on superwatchers is undesirable, as it encourages centralization", but "having some widely deployed superwatchers immunizes any verifier who consults one against eclipse and dead attacks" (wtbo §5, L131 @`181569b64`). *[Fidelity correction 2026-09-15: earlier revisions of this note rendered the last clause as "immunize any verifier…", grammatically adapted to fit a sentence this note built around it. The source verb is "immunizes". The claim is unchanged.]*

### Duplicity = the identity double-spend
- "Duplicity is KERI's term for the double-spend problem in identity: a controller signing two different events with the same sequence number." (primer §2.6). Watcher gets differing log-heads (Hash X vs Hash Y) from two witnesses → duplicity detected → broadcasts conflicting events as "cryptographic proof of fraud." "In the KERI ecosystem, this proof is fatal to the reputation of the identifier."
- "duplicity is cryptographically provable" (wtbo table). Forked events "remain in the record as permanent, cryptographically attributable evidence of mischief." (wtbo §5).

### Pre-rotation firewall (the central security innovation)
- "Pre-rotation establishes a firewall between day-to-day use and occasional governance." (primer §2.3). Keep K1 on a production server; generate K2, hash it, store in "air-gapped, cold wallet." Server breach steals K1 but attacker "cannot rotate to assume control."
- Mechanics: event includes public K1 (to sign) but only the **hash/digest of K2**, not K2's public key. "The security comes from the asymmetry in knowledge: the controller knows or has access to K2, while the world only sees its hash." (primer §2.3).
- Rotation event: (1) reveal K2 public key (proving knowledge of the pre-committed secret), (2) sign with K2 private key, (3) commit to K3 via its hash. Verifier checks: "Does the hash of this newly revealed key K2 match the commitment `n` recorded in the previous event?"
- **Royal-succession analogy** (kspqs): current signing key = "reigning monarch" (signature is law for interaction events); rotation key = "the authority that determines the line of succession… cannot act as a monarch, but… has the power to 'crown' a new monarch." The published digest is "an unbreakable royal seal on a secret decree of succession" — "a verifiable, forward cryptographic commitment to the identity of the next in line… without revealing who that heir is."
- **The genius is timing:** "commitment precede [sic] exposure. The commitment to the next key is made far in advance, when the system is secure, using a quantum-safe primitive (the hash). The quantum-vulnerable public rotation key is only revealed at the exact moment it is used, after which authority has already passed to a new, secret successor. The attacker is always one step behind." (kspqs §Recovery and Invalidation, L88 @`181569b64`). *[Fidelity correction 2026-09-15: the source reads "commitment precede exposure" at both pins; earlier revisions of this note silently corrected the verb. Quoting a paper's typo is ugly, and paraphrasing past it is worse — it makes the quote unfindable in the source, which is the failure standards §7 exists to prevent.]* Note the tension recorded in §9.1: this "always one step behind" framing is **stronger than the same author's own qualification in `wtbo.md` §4**, which says the hash "protects the *next* key, never the one currently exposed".

### The forgery/detection-race guarantee stated relative to assumptions (HONEST framing)
- "The guarantee is therefore a detection-and-rotation race, not an absolute wall: the hash protects the *next* key, never the one currently exposed, so safety rests on the controller detecting compromise and rotating before an adversary can exploit what the rotation reveals." (wtbo §4, L99 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin.** This is the most important sentence in the whole source set for keeping `keri-doctrine.md` claim 7 honest, and the KERI specification has independently arrived at the same concession this cycle — see §9.1.
- "The recovery key revealed in that rotation is itself classical — a quantum adversary who observes it could break it too — but the same event that reveals it also retires it, handing control forward to a fresh key sealed behind a new, post-quantum hash." (wtbo §4, L99 @`181569b64`).
- Interaction-event forgery window "is real and should not be minimized. But KERI provides mechanisms to shrink it that have no X.509 equivalent" — high-stakes ops anchored to KEL are "detectable almost immediately"; weighted multisig raises the bar. "The forgery window in X.509 is not just open — it is invisible to the party most affected." (wtbo §4).
- KAWA formal guarantees hold only under stated conditions: "the controller is honest, witness faults are bounded below the threshold, and communication is reliable." (wtbo §5). KERI's formal literature "remains thin" — most rigorous is a 2025 ETH Zürich Master's thesis (Del Giudice), "not peer-reviewed."

### The retrograde attack (was.md — the reason simple signatures are weak)
- Core claim: "By themselves, digital signatures are much weaker evidence than casual thinkers might imagine." because "simple signatures are difficult to sequence relative to a compromise or revocation event." (was intro).
- Attack timeline: Alice creates key K (Jan 1); Malfoy steals K (Feb 1); Alice rotates/revokes (Feb 8). Malfoy can backdate to *any* time K was valid (window C), and — critically — "Nothing magic happens on Feb 8… Malfoy can keep generating new evidence that looks like it originated in the past, *forever*!" (window D, no endpoint).
- Worst case: "Alice remains at risk *even if she rotates or revokes K before Malfoy steals it*. If K *ever* comes into his possession, even a year after it was revoked, a retrograde attack is possible."
- **Three flawed mitigations:** (1) contextual clues — "human attention and wisdom are both unreliable"; (2) retroactive revocation — "dangerous and foolish," lets Alice cheat/repudiate her own actions, can't reach all stakeholders (Christie's Basquiat-auction example); (3) "pyrrhic victory" — restrict all analysis to "*Does Alice's signature validate this evidence in the current moment?*" — "After-the-fact audits are impossible."
- **"X509 certificates embrace this limitation — you can ask whether one is valid *now*… but you can't ask whether it was valid a week ago."** (was Mitigation 3). Same limitation infects JWTs, OAuth2, OIDC, SD-JWTs, and DIDs "that can only be resolved to the current key state" (e.g., did:web).
- **Solution = anchored signatures.** Formula: "Keep tamper-evident records that can prove how a given signing event relates in time to changes in the associated key state." (was §Solution). Then "Malfoy can't get away with backdating evidence… once Alice rotates her key, Malfoy's attack window closes forever."
- **Why it's simpler than blockchain:** "We don't need to know the relative order of two signing events — only how any one event relates to its key state. That means no total ordering, and no cumbersome consensus algorithm… no permissioning problem, no big central *anything* in the sky, no scale or performance bottlenecks, and no regulatory problems with data locality, privacy, or erasure." (was §Solution). Alt anchoring mechanisms: blockchain, KEL/TEL, randomness beacons + newspaper ad / IPFS CID (provably-after + provably-before = anchored).
- KERI implements via "tiny, identifier-specific files called KELs (key event logs) and TELs (transaction event logs)… KERI anchors (records the hashes of) signed TEL events in the KEL to make sequence unambiguous." (was §Solution).
- NIST grounding: NIST SP 800-102 — a signed message "provides no assurance that the private key was used to sign the message at that time." (primer §3.3).

### Post-quantum threat model (two-front war)
- "defending against a two-front war. The first front is the HNDL attack on confidentiality… The second is a 'surprise quantum attack' on control and authenticity." (kspqs intro).
- **HNDL / "quantum debt":** "every file you encrypt with mainstream cryptography today is an IOU to a future attacker with a quantum computer." (kspqs). "Your adversaries don't need a quantum computer yet. They just need patience and a hard drive." Weak point is *not* symmetric encryption (AES-256 ~quantum-safe) but the Diffie-Hellman **handshake**.
- **Surprise attack on control:** Shor's algorithm derives a private signing key from a visible public key "in a matter of hours or minutes." Most systems "brittle": "If an identity's root key is compromised, control is lost catastrophically and irrevocably." KERI's answer is pre-rotation.
- **Why hashes save you:** "Modern cryptographic hashes like SHA-256 are considered post-quantum safe; while Grover's algorithm provides a theoretical speedup… simply using a 256-bit hash provides an effective 128 bits of post-quantum security." (kspqs §The Pre-Rotation Mechanism, L72 @`181569b64`). Grover = quadratic speedup only; Shor = exponential against RSA/ECC (wtbo §3, L68 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin — cited by `keri-doctrine.md` load-bearing claim 7.**

- **NEW at `181569b64` — the 128-bit figure now carries the author's own caveat.** kspqs 1.2 adds, in the same paragraph as the 128-bit claim: "Its draft revision does ask for 384-bit hash outputs where quantum security is sought, on the ground that a 256-bit output's quantum security level could arguably drop below 128 bits" (kspqs §The Pre-Rotation Mechanism, L72 @`181569b64`). Hardman immediately bounds the concession — "a parameter choice, not a change of primitive, and one KERI's agility accommodates by design" (same sentence, L72) — which is his assessment, not a surveyed result. The hedge that matters for the corpus: the "effective 128 bits" number that `keri-doctrine.md` claim 7 leans on is now **contested by a standards body the author himself cites**, and the paper's answer is an agility argument rather than a defence of the number. See §9.2.

- **NEW at `181569b64` — European certification guidance splits mature from novel primitives.** "the new lattice-based schemes must be combined with a classically secure mechanism before they carry security on their own, while hash-based mechanisms may be used standalone" (kspqs §The Pre-Rotation Mechanism, L72 @`181569b64`, reporting ENISA's *Agreed Cryptographic Mechanisms* v2.0). This is a **third-party rule, not Hardman's claim**, and it is the strongest external support in the source set for KERI's hash-first pre-rotation bet: a regulator independently treats hashes as standalone-safe and lattice PQC as not-yet. wtbo 1.8 adds the matching sentence on the other side: "ENISA's certification guidance concurs — the circuit depth a Grover-style attack requires limits the achievable speedup enough that 256-bit hashes remain agreed even where quantum resistance is sought — while recommending 384-bit outputs in those contexts." (wtbo §3, L68 @`181569b64`).

- **NEW at `181569b64` — hybrid is now a European directive, not just industry practice.** "the EU's agreed-mechanisms catalog directs that post-quantum cryptography be rolled out in hybrid mode alongside classically secure asymmetric cryptography, with every component of the hybrid required to break before the hybrid does." (kspqs §Solution 2, L112 @`181569b64`). Strengthens the existing hybrid-KEM claim from "industry standard" to "regulatory expectation in one major jurisdiction."

- **NEW at `181569b64` — "the clock has become a calendar."** kspqs 1.2 opens a new paragraph in the introduction: "The NIS Cooperation Group's coordinated roadmap sets dated milestones — national roadmaps by the end of 2026, high-risk use cases migrated by the end of 2030, medium-risk by 2035" (kspqs §Introduction, L28 @`181569b64`), and the EU catalog's draft v3 "demotes every classical signature scheme it used to recommend to a time-limited *admissible* class, leaving only post-quantum schemes recommended" (same paragraph, L28). wtbo 1.8 names the four survivors: "leaving only ML-DSA, XMSS, LMS and SLH-DSA recommended" (wtbo §6, L147 @`181569b64`), and is careful that this is **not a ban** — "new systems *should* use recommended mechanisms, and using an admissible one must be justified — so staying classical becomes a rationale owed for every new system rather than a wall met on a date" (wtbo §6, L147). That hedge is the author's, and the corpus should carry it: the deadline is a justification burden, not a prohibition.

- **NEW at `181569b64` — the agility argument, restated as a measurable distance.** This is the sharpest new doctrinal turn in either revision. wtbo 1.8 observes that EU Implementing Regulation 2026/1731 "obliges every European Digital Identity Wallet provider to use only the mechanisms referred to in Annex Ia, and Annex Ia is a single undated link to the document ENISA edits… When the file at that URL changes, the obligation changes." (wtbo §6, L147 @`181569b64`). Set against "a decade of coordinated migration across every CA, every root store, every embedded trust anchor" (same paragraph), the conclusion is: **"A trust architecture's real agility is the distance between those two costs."** (wtbo §6, L147 @`181569b64`). kspqs 1.2 lands the same point independently — "A regulator can now revise its cryptographic requirements by editing a file; whether the systems underneath can follow is an architectural question." (kspqs §Conclusion, L122 @`181569b64`) — and adds the detail that makes it bite: the same regulation "pins an IETF draft to an exact revision" while leaving the crypto catalog undated (kspqs §Conclusion, L122). **Hedge:** this is a rhetorical framing of a regulatory drafting choice, and neither paper argues that the EU *intended* the asymmetry. Repeat it as Hardman's reading of the mechanism, not as a finding about EU intent.
- **Two HNDL solutions on KERI:** (1) Out-of-band key exchange — shared salt via OOB channel, no public asymmetric exchange, "nothing for an HNDL adversary to capture"; (2) Hybrid KEM — classical (X25519) + PQC (ML-KEM), "belt and suspenders," already default in OpenSSH 9.0 (April 2022), IETF-documented, GitHub-adopted.
- **Crypto-agility proof point:** integrating an OQS NIST-finalist algorithm into keripy "took only a couple of hours to complete" (personal communication, Sam Smith, Oct 7 2025). Avoids "twin perils" of premature PQC: performance overhead of larger keys/signatures, and reliance on un-hardened algorithms.
- Hybrid multisig hedge (primer §2.5): threshold requiring *both* an ECC signature and a PQC signature — "Even if the ECC key is broken, the attacker cannot satisfy the full threshold without also breaking the post-quantum key."

## 4. Invariants and "never do X" rules

- **Never use a single opaquely-managed secret as the sole gate on org identity.** Missile-silo analogy: a silo "safe" only because builders set one secret at construction, no guards/fences/surveillance, secret replaced every few months — "Certs are only guarded by one opaquely managed secret, just like the dangerous missile silo." (x509-prob §Governance). Multi-attestation wisdom is ancient: Code of Hammurabi law 7, Deuteronomy 19:15, double-entry accounting.
- **Never renew/rotate an identity.** "renewing an *identity* is a non-sequitur. A renewed credential has to be tied to a *sameness* that spans the before and after." (x509-prob §Lifespans). Birth certificate vs. driver's license framing.
- **The next (rotation) key must be managed differently/independently from the current key** — offline, air-gapped, cold storage, different device. This independence is what makes pre-rotation a real firewall. (primer §2.3, x509-prob §Prerotation).
- **Never treat a signature as evidence of *when* it was made without anchoring.** (was, whole paper).
- **A SAID must equal the hash of its own structure; changing one payload character invalidates it.** SAID derivation: dummy-fill the `d` field (e.g., 44 `#` chars for Base64 256-bit), hash whole structure, overwrite dummies. "An ACDC is not a mutable document; it is a crystallized fact." (primer §3.2). "If a single bit of a past event is altered, the hash links break, and the log is rejected." (primer §2.2).
- **Basic (non-transferable) AIDs cannot be rotated** — identifier *is* the key digest; changing key = abandon identifier and all reputation. "Basic AIDs are suitable only for ephemeral, short-lived use cases." (primer §2.1).
- **Multisig is optional; declaring & following the policy is REQUIRED.** "multisig is optional and infinitely variable, but defining and following policy about it is required." (primer §2.4). "KERI's real innovation in multisig is less about *inventing* and more about *exposing*."
- **Mutual anchoring on issuance** (issuer AND holder each anchor) — "prevents spam (you can't force a credential into someone's wallet) and provides non-repudiation of receipt." (primer §3.1).
- **Catastrophic loss requires TWO independent compromises** (current keys AND pre-committed rotation keys); if it happens "no cryptographic mechanism can restore control. The identifier must be abandoned." (wtbo §5, L127 @`181569b64`). **Verified intact at the new pin — cited by `keri-doctrine.md` load-bearing claim 7.**

## 5. Anti-patterns / outsider-tells / misconceptions explicitly corrected

- **"To a hammer everything looks like a nail" — PKI experts.** "to PKI experts, every verification problem looks like a place for certs." Screws-vs-nails: "ACDCs are drastically better than certs, as primary proof of organizational identity." (x509-prob intro).
- **Cert = privilege, misused as identity.** Certs have "a dynamic sweet spot and lifespans measured in weeks or months" — wrong for "something with a lifespan measured in decades." (x509-prob).
- **Focusing on CA vetting is the wrong locus.** "*Certificate authorities aren't the ones that choose or manage the keys in certs.* Holders of certs do that. It's the key management of *holders* that's the ubiquitous locus of the risk." Master-electrician-inspects-wiring-but-any-employee-inputs-the-secret analogy. (x509-prob §Governance).
- **"Typically" is the tell.** Cert-defenders cite protections IT departments "*typically*" deploy — meaning "it's left to each party to devise and implement (or not implement)… nobody can audit their decisions or competence." (x509-prob).
- **Shorter cert lifespans are a false cure.** Trades 4× insecure-window reduction for 4× maintenance burden; 45 days (or even 3.5 days) of compromised operation "is still *terrible*." Worse, it "created a strong incentive to take the insecure and easy path: if there's a risk of a compromised key, just wait until the cert expires." (x509-prob §Revocation is disincented). Revocation is disincented by cost/effort.
- **"Renewal proves less than it appears."** "certificate renewal demonstrates that a CA is willing to re-assert a binding, not that the underlying key was rotated, that the old key was retired, or that any meaningful change in key management occurred." Holder "may reuse the same keypair across CAs… nothing in the protocol makes this visible." (wtbo §1).
- **DID/VC "decentralized" drift — the issuer-rotation hitch.** Indirection (issue to identifier not key) solves *holder* rotation, "But there's a hitch. Evidence is also *issued by* an identifier… If the issuer updates their identifier, old evidence is invalidated." (x509-prob §Much better alternatives). "This risk is poorly understood in SSI circles." A key rotation "*invalidates all the evidence they have ever created*." (was) — "What would you think of a driver's license bureau whose recover-from-breach plan required all current license holders to come back to the office?"
- **Most DIDs only resolve to current key state.** "*most DIDs, including crowd favorites like did:web, can only be resolved to the current key state*" → historical actions unprovable. (was Mitigation 3). Requires `versionId`/`versionTime` params that most methods lack.
- **Blockchain is not the answer to anchoring.** SSI community "wrestling with the centralization, scale, and regulatory compliance challenges of blockchains for almost a decade… Anybody who tells you the problems are happily solved is either uninformed or disingenuous." (x509-prob). Blockchain *can* anchor but is overkill — you don't need total ordering.
- **Trust registries are a band-aid.** "they just move the centralization back to a different level of the architecture… immature and fragmented… primarily address governance for issuers, not issuees." (x509-prob). ACDC chaining "eliminates the need for trust registries."
- **CT is detection not prevention, and re-centralizes.** "CT is a detection mechanism, not a prevention mechanism; fraudulent certificates may be usable for a time." + "effectively replacing one set of suboptimal gatekeepers with another." (primer §1.1). CT "redistributes administrative trust rather than eliminating it." (wtbo abstract). MMD latency window (~24h); split-view attacks; gossip protocol leaks browsing behavior; 2018 draft RFC abandoned.
- **The ultimate CA arbiter is 3 browser vendors.** "the ultimate arbiter of CA trustworthiness is a handful of browser vendors." (wtbo §1, L30 @`181569b64`). Root programs (Google, Mozilla, Apple) "decide which CAs and which CT logs are trusted, with immediate global effect and no formal appeals process. No government, no standards body, and no external adjudicator sits above them." (wtbo Note [b], L179 @`181569b64`). *[Fidelity correction 2026-09-15: earlier revisions of this note attributed "immediate, global effect" (with the comma) to Note [b]. That comma'd phrase is from wtbo §2, L50 — "These programs wield immediate, global effect over certificate validity" — and the note had spliced two passages under one citation. Both passages survive at the new pin; the quote above is now Note [b] verbatim.]*
- **"Fail open."** OCSP responder offline → browsers "accept the unverifiable certificate as valid because blocking traffic is considered too disruptive." "The system is most vulnerable precisely when it is most needed." (primer §1.1, wtbo §2).
- **JSON canonicalization fragility / JSON-LD term-redefinition.** `{"a":1,"b":2}` vs `{"b":2,"a":1}` — "semantically identical but have different cryptographic hashes." JCS (RFC 8785) normalization "is brittle." JSON-LD "term redefinition" vulnerabilities: "the meaning of a signed credential can be altered without invalidating the signature." (primer §4.1, cites W3C vc-data-integrity issue #272).
- **Paired vs anchored signatures.** *Paired* (W3C VC `proof`, JWT bundle, X509 `tbsCertificate`+`signature`) — "the two go together because the format says so." *Anchored* (ACDC) — signature may travel as sidecar "but independent of any container context, there's a provable association… because of an anchoring association in the KEL." (primer §3.3).
- **Pinning is discouraged by X.509 — a tell.** Google Cloud 2026 advised NOT to pin certs because pinning "will cause your application to break during routine certificate rotations." Doctrine reframing: "there is no cryptographic thread joining the old key to the new one, so a relying party that pinned the old key pinned nothing durable — the only continuity on offer is the CA's say-so. Prerotation is exactly what would let a relying party commit to a durable cryptographic anchor *and* survive the rotation." (x509-prob §Prerotation; wtbo §1 — "trust *more* administrative anchors, not fewer").
- **CA-ecosystem trust is jurisdiction-bound.** "assertions rooted in the SHAKEN ecosystem mandated by US regulators are not accepted in Europe or Asia." (primer §1.1). KERI crosses jurisdictions because no CA / no central storage.
- **STIR/SHAKEN wrong-signer anti-pattern.** Signature "affixed by an originating service provider, not by the enterprise that makes the call." "Signing must be done not by phone companies, but by holders of telephone numbers." (x509-prob §Scale). Delegate certs to fix this create a 10,000× scaling explosion.
- **RFC 9448 self-own:** a telco cert ecosystem "purpose-built to achieve high assurance of 'identity'" abdicates a foundational protection to "undefined local judgment" ("MUST identify the Token Authority based on locally configured information or local policies"). (x509-prob §Governance).

## 6. Precise terminology / definitions

- **AID (Autonomic Identifier):** "a persistent, self-certifying identifier that serves as a control mechanism for an entity engaged in cryptographic interactions." (kspqs). Self-certifying + persistent (endures across key changes). = an SCID.
- **SCID (Self-Certifying Identifier):** identifier derived cryptographically from the initial controlling public key(s). (primer §2.1).
- **Basic vs Transferable derivation:** basic = digest of a single public key (non-rotatable, ephemeral); transferable = derived from the *entire inception event* including the pre-rotation commitment (survives rotation; identifier constant). (primer §2.1).
- **KEL (Key Event Log):** "the complete, verifiable history of an AID's evolving key state… simple, append-only… each entry, or 'event,' is cryptographically linked to the previous one, forming a hash chain." (kspqs). "microledgers" (primer §2). Three event types: **inception (`icp`)**, **rotation (`rot`)**, **interaction (`ixn`)** (anchors data/seals without changing keys). "The head of the log *is* the current state." (wtbo §4).
- **KERL:** (implied by KEL/receipts; not separately defined in these sources — see gaps).
- **TEL (Transaction Event Log):** "identifier-specific files" for transaction (e.g., credential status) events; ACDC status anchored in TELs allows "real-time status checks without privacy leaks." KERI "anchors (records the hashes of) signed TEL events in the KEL to make sequence unambiguous." (was; primer §5).
- **Pre-rotation:** forward cryptographic commitment (hash) to the next key set, published without revealing the next public key. (all sources).
- **Witness:** "a lightweight server the controller designates to store and serve the KEL"; validates events and returns signed receipts; makes no identity assertion. Threshold of receipts → event "stable." (wtbo §4, primer §2.6).
- **Watcher:** detection layer; polls multiple witnesses for each KEL's head, detects duplicity by divergent heads; "an in-proc buffer between untrusted external parties and code that consumes key state." Two forms: in-process local resolver (default/recommended) or shared remote service; archival variant = **"super watcher."** (wtbo §4-5, primer §2.6).
- **KAWA (KERI's Algorithm for Witness Agreement):** "the in-protocol rule by which the witnesses a single controller has designated agree on that controller's own key events." Single-phase agreement; fault tolerance without PBFT multi-phase overhead. Three properties (technical distributed-systems sense): **validity** (accepted events genuinely authorized), **consistency** (no two correct witnesses accept conflicting events at same seq number), **reliability** (progress while faults < threshold). Not a global consensus network. (wtbo §4).
- **Duplicity:** controller signing two conflicting events at the same sequence number; the identity double-spend. (primer §2.6).
- **Weighted multisig / thresholds:** fractionally weighted thresholds declared in the public KEL. `k` list = public keys; `kt` = fractional weights, e.g. `[["1/2","1/2","1/2"],["1/3","1/3","1/3","1/3"]]` — subarrays = *clauses* joined by OR; each clause satisfied when its signing weights sum ≥ 1.0. (primer §2.4).
- **SAID (Self-Addressing Identifier):** content-addressable identifier = cryptographic hash of the data structure itself, embedded in the structure via the dummy-fill-then-overwrite derivation. (primer §3.2).
- **ACDC (Authentic Chained Data Container):** protocol for attestations/VCs prioritizing "security, compactness, and provenance"; "turning data into a rigid, verifiable graph." Fixes "the copy-paste vulnerability of standard digital documents." (primer §3). Issued *by* and *to* AIDs.
- **IPEX (Issuance and Presentation EXchange protocol):** "issuance protocol for exchanging credentials." Offer → request → grant flow. (primer §3.1). [Note source expands acronym loosely.]
- **Edges / chaining:** ACDC edges point to other ACDCs by SAID; "any number of chains, with links expressing any type of connection with any desired weight" → a verifiable DAG. Can include "foreign evidence types" (signed PDFs, W3C VCs, X509, biometrics). (primer §3.4).
- **Anchored signature:** signature whose association to data is provable via an anchoring entry in the KEL, independent of container. (primer §3.3, was).
- **CESR (Composable Event Streaming Representation):** serialization format; "the language they speak." Text↔binary via "concatenation composability"; self-framing via "a framing code prefix" carrying type+size; enables pipelining and crypto-agility. (primer §4).
- **DKMI (Decentralized Key Management Infrastructure):** the category KERI belongs to. (primer §1.3, kspqs).

## 7. Worked examples & usage patterns

- **Weighted-multisig corporate board** (primer §2.4): 3 founders + 4 investors; "majority of Founders OR supermajority of Investors" → `kt = [["1/2","1/2","1/2"],["1/3","1/3","1/3","1/3"]]`. Any 2 founders (0.5×2=1.0) or any 3 investors satisfy.
- **Break-glass recovery** (primer §2.4): Phone(0.5)+Laptop(0.5)+ColdStorage(1.0), threshold 1.0. Lose phone → cold-storage key rotates unilaterally. "The recovery logic is baked into the math of the identifier itself."
- **Supply-chain provenance DAG** (primer §3.4): Mine (AU, ACDC1 "Raw Ore" schema A, SAID X; edges → mine's legal-identity credential + RMI ethical-source certification) → Refiner (TH, ACDC2 "Refined Metal" schema B, SAID Y; edge → ACDC1 by SAID X) → Manufacturer (ACDC3 "Part" schema C, SAID Z; edge → ACDC2) → Retailer (ACDC4 "Ethically Sourced Product" → ACDC3). Consumer scans QR → verifier follows *all* edges, verifying signatures/anchors/non-revocation transitively. "**transitive trust**" — "not because the manufacturer said so, but because the cryptographic chain leads back to the mine." "no party in the middle can swap out a bad component… without breaking the chain."
- **ACDC issuance flow** (primer §3.1): Construction → Structuring (schema) → Anchoring (digest into KEL/TEL = anchored signature) → Transport via IPEX (offer/request/grant) → Receipt (holder anchors in own KEL). Issuer log: "I issued Credential X at Sequence 50"; holder log: "I accepted Credential X at Sequence 12."
- **Selective disclosure** (primer §3.5): payload structured into sections ("Personal Info," "Medical Info," "License Info"), each with random salt + digest; holder reveals only "License Info" via Merkle-proof style, others shown as hash only. More flexible than SD-JWT, "less exotic cryptography than AnonCreds" (x509-prob).
- **Right-to-be-forgotten / GDPR** (primer §3.5): KEL stores only anchors (hashes); payload held off-chain by issuer/holder; delete payload → hash remains for chain integrity but "without the payload, the hash is irreversible." Contrast blockchain: "'forgetting' an identifier requires a hard fork of the entire chain." KERI: "burning the identity without disrupting the global ecosystem."
- **CESR code-table examples** (primer §4.2): `A`=Ed25519 256-bit seed (size 44); `E`=Blake3-256 digest (44); `0B`=Ed25519 signature (88); `-A##`=generic pipeline group counter (code length 4). Pipelining "cryptographic traffic cop" routes on first 1–4 bytes without full deserialization. Crypto-agility example: assigning `D` prefix to "post-quantum Falcon-512 public keys" — old parsers still handle stream: "Read Code → Lookup Length → Read Bytes."
- **Incremental verification** (primer §5): verification is O(n) in history theoretically but O(Δn) in practice — "Verifiers cache the state and only process new events since their last check." "For a decades-old identity that rotates quarterly, verifying history from any recent cache point is fast."

## 8. Honest tradeoffs (the author's own "cost" ledger)

- Formal literature "thin"; KAWA's strongest analysis is a non-peer-reviewed Master's thesis. (wtbo §5).
- Malicious controllers *can* fork; safety = deployment question (watcher density/maturity). (wtbo §5).
- Catastrophic double-compromise recovery = abandon identifier — "genuine operational pain" — "but it is also the *only* recovery mode X.509 supports for *any* key compromise." (wtbo §5).

## 9. Open questions, unresolved tensions, and the record of corrections

### 9.1 `kspqs` and `wtbo` do not state the pre-rotation guarantee at the same strength — and the spec has now sided with `wtbo`

**Unresolved, and the most important thing in this note for a reviewer to hold.** The two papers describe the same mechanism and land in different places:

- `kspqs` §Recovery and Invalidation, L88 @`181569b64`: "The attacker is always one step behind. This makes KERI's chain of control authority fundamentally resilient, not just resistant, to quantum attack."
- `wtbo` §4, L99 @`181569b64`: "the hash protects the *next* key, never the one currently exposed, so safety rests on the controller detecting compromise and rotating before an adversary can exploit what the rotation reveals."

These are not contradictory — `kspqs` is describing the *rotation* authority and `wtbo` the *signing* authority — but read alone, `kspqs` reads as an unconditional guarantee and `wtbo` reads as a race with a live window. `kspqs` 1.2 did **not** import `wtbo`'s qualifier, though both were revised on the same date (2026-08-20). The KERI specification has, this cycle, conceded the `wtbo` framing in its own words (hiding pre-rotated keys "protects the next key pair but does not protect the currently exposed one" — verify against the spec note before citing, as that wording is second-hand here and this note has not read the spec). **Doctrine should cite `wtbo` §4 L99, not `kspqs`, whenever the pre-rotation guarantee is stated.** Where a chapter cites `kspqs` for pre-rotation strength, it must carry the `wtbo` qualifier alongside it.

### 9.2 The "effective 128 bits" number is now contested by a body the author cites

Open. `keri-doctrine.md` claim 7 leans on the Grover-vs-Shor asymmetry and, through `kspqs`, on "an effective 128 bits of post-quantum security" from a 256-bit hash. ENISA's draft v3 asks for 384-bit outputs "on the ground that a 256-bit output's quantum security level could arguably drop below 128 bits" (kspqs §The Pre-Rotation Mechanism, L72 @`181569b64`). Hardman's answer is that this is "a parameter choice, not a change of primitive" (same line) — which is sound as far as it goes, and is an assessment rather than a result. Two things the corpus does not know and should not guess: whether KERI/CESR's actual digest code points can express a 384-bit commitment in the pre-rotation `n` field without a spec change, and whether the deployed ecosystem uses anything but Blake3-256. **Neither question is answerable from this source set.** Flagged for whoever mines the CESR and KERI specs.

## 10. New source: `prog-a.md` — "Progressive Assurance" (v0.9, 2026-09-15)

**Status: v0.9, published the day of this run, and it shows.** The paper carries commented-out co-author frontmatter naming Karla McKenna of GLEIF, §2.1's reference-assurance material reads in a different voice from the rest, and two paragraphs are hard-wrapped mid-sentence. Treat it as a **draft position paper**, a full tier below `wtbo` and `x509-prob` in settledness, and say so wherever a chapter cites it. It is nonetheless squarely in this note's territory: it is the author's org-identity argument, built on the same X.509 critique, and it introduces a framework the rest of the corpus has nowhere.

### 10.1 The Progressive Assurance Model — four levels of *control* assurance

The paper's organizing move is to split identity assurance into two independent axes and then develop only one of them. **Reference assurance** "asks: how confidently is the identifier tied to a specific legal entity and the available reference data about the entity?" (prog-a §2.1, L87 @`181569b64`). **Control assurance** "asks: how strongly is *control* of the identifier bound to the entity, an accountable human, or a governed program? This is the axis the rest of this paper develops, in four levels (LoA 0 through LoA 3)." (prog-a §2.1, L98 @`181569b64`). The four levels, each quoted from its own subsection:

- **LoA 0 — unambiguous reference affidavit.** "LoA 0 provides zero proof of *control*. It is an affidavit — an assertion of fact for the existence of the entity and certain reference data about it — not a credential conferring entitlement." (prog-a §2.2, L108 @`181569b64`). "Anyone can use it to refer to the entity, including competitors, critics, or attackers" (same, L108).
- **LoA 1 — foundational control.** Binds "a legal entity, a cryptographic identifier, and a controllable digital asset — usually an internet domain or a phone number — into a single verifiable claim" (prog-a §2.3, L112 @`181569b64`), positioned as the replacement for a Domain Validated X.509 cert. Its own limit is stated: "LoA 1 proves *some* agent associated with the organization wants the identifier; it does not prove which agent, or whether they are authorized to act for the organization." (L121).
- **LoA 2 — human accountability.** Adds proof of the requester's own legal identity plus verifiable delegation. Limit: "Delegation is one-way at this level: a properly authorized requester can still go rogue without tripping automated alarms. LoA 2 also makes no claim about transparency, duplicity detection, or recovery from compromise." (prog-a §2.4, L133 @`181569b64`).
- **LoA 3 — robust governance (the LE vLEI).** "requires cryptographic proof that the organization's entire identity-management program operates under an externally audited governance framework" (prog-a §2.5, L137 @`181569b64`); the organization "must demonstrate practical competence: AIDs configured with weighted multi-signature thresholds, independent witnesses for compromise detection, and pre-rotation of keys" (L139). Limit, stated plainly: "No system is perfectly secure. Even at LoA 3, a zero-day exploit or a sophisticated social engineering attack can succeed." (L141).

**The doctrinal point the ladder makes:** "The same identifier persists; the same evidence chain persists; only the level of assurance changes." (prog-a §1.3, L73 @`181569b64`). This is the identity-is-sameness thesis from `x509-prob` and `wtbo` §1, applied to an assurance ladder — the identifier does not change as vetting deepens, which is precisely what an X.509 upgrade path cannot offer.

### 10.2 What is genuinely new here versus the rest of the source set

- **Assurance and friction are treated as separable, and that is the whole argument.** "Assurance and friction rise together, and most business interactions fall between these extremes, lacking a fit-for-purpose solution." (prog-a abstract, L10 @`181569b64`). The gap claim: "The market has been forced to choose between low-friction-but-limited and rigorous-but-demanding." (§1.3, L71).
- **The vLEI's friction is defended, not apologized for.** "That rigor necessarily comes with friction: the vetting is demanding and deliberate by design, because higher assurance cannot be conjured without more thorough verification." (prog-a §1.2, L67 @`181569b64`). Useful for chapter 06 — it is the author, who works in this ecosystem, declining to claim that KERI makes vetting cheap.
- **Brand ≠ legal entity.** "Most digital infrastructure does not distinguish between a brand name like 'Coca-Cola' and a specific legal entity" (prog-a §1.1, L37 @`181569b64`); "The legal entity is what can be sued, what owes taxes, what holds contracts, and what carries reputational consequence." (same, L37). A cleaner statement of the org-identity problem than anything in `x509-prob`.
- **The walled-garden taxonomy.** Three "verified identity" systems whose verification "lives inside a single platform, channel, or industry consortium. It cannot move. It cannot be reused. It cannot be combined with evidence from elsewhere." (prog-a §3.1, L159 @`181569b64`) — STIR/SHAKEN, social-media badges, EV certs. On EV specifically: "Browser vendors have, at various times, removed the special EV UI treatment entirely without warning, leaving the organizations that paid for EV with assurance that no longer renders." (L157). This is a sharper version of the "3 browser vendors are the arbiter" shibboleth in §5 of this note.
- **Anchoring restated with unusual precision.** "The credential is provably bound to the identifier's keystate at a particular sequence number, not to a specific keypair or to an identifier's keystate at an implicit point in time that is only safe to evaluate in the present." (prog-a §4.1, L199 @`181569b64`). This is the tightest one-sentence statement of the `was.md` anchored-signature doctrine anywhere in the source set, and it names the *second* failure mode (implicit-present keystate) that `was.md` covers only by implication.
- **The RAW/JPEG analogy appears here too, and the convergence is worth noting.** "A RAW file is the archival source, lossless and reusable; a JPEG is exported for a specific use… You don't archive in JPEG." (prog-a §4.2, L211 @`181569b64`). `wtbo` Note [c] uses the same image for deriving X.509 artifacts from KERI; `raw/06` records it as the lossless-vs-lossy metaphor from `acdc-vc-diff.md`. **Three papers, one analogy, three different targets** (format choice, legal-artifact derivation, assurance derivation) — a chapter should pick one target per use and not let the analogy do triple duty in a single paragraph.
- **Derivative evidence, with the cost named.** "The model treats ACDCs as the source of truth and other formats as derivable presentations… The SD-JWT inherits its authority from the foundational credential but does not carry its permanence: SD-JWTs are signed by keys, not by identifiers, and lack native chaining for delegation." (prog-a §4.2, L209 @`181569b64`). **This material overlaps `raw/06`'s remit** (`sdjwt-acdc.md`, `acdc-vc-diff.md`) and is flagged for its owner rather than developed here.

### 10.3 Where prog-a needs a hedge the paper does not supply

- The LoA ladder is **a proposal by one author with a commercial interest** (the paper cites two Provenant schemas, `org-vet` and GCD, as its reference mechanisms at L131 and L265). No standards body has adopted it, and the paper does not claim one has. Any chapter citing it must say "proposed" and must not present LoA 0–3 as an established scale alongside eIDAS LoAs or NIST IALs, which it superficially resembles.
- "LoA 2 maps roughly to the rigor of an Extended Validation X.509 certificate" (prog-a §2.4, L125 @`181569b64`) is an unsupported equivalence — no criterion-by-criterion mapping is given. Repeat it as the author's rough calibration, with the word "roughly" intact.
- The paper asserts "RFC 9881 defines a path for X.509 to use post-quantum signatures" (prog-a §2.3, L116 @`181569b64`). **This note has not verified that RFC number or its content**, and `wtbo` — the more carefully sourced paper — does not cite it. Do not propagate the RFC number into a chapter without checking it.

## 11. New source: `active-discovery.md` — "Building Active Discovery" (v1.1, 2026-08-12)

**Mined narrowly.** This is a privacy/discovery-architecture paper (a Microsoft Research matching platform, Tor v3 onion services, verifiable attestations). Most of it is out of scope for a KERI doctrine corpus. One section, §Proving who is asking, is squarely in scope, plus two methodological items worth recording.

### 11.1 The three requirements Hardman puts on identity evidence, stated independently of KERI

Valuable because he derives them from an application's needs and *then* reaches for KERI, rather than the reverse. "The evidence must be verifiable by a stranger without a callback to its issuer, because a callback tells the issuer that a verification is happening and when. It must remain checkable later, since the decision may be litigated long after the keys involved have rotated. And it must support chaining" (active-discovery §Proving who is asking, L99 @`181569b64`).

- **Evidence outlives keys.** "A verifier usually needs historical provability rather than present-tense key validity: what was valid then, not only what is valid now… Binding evidence to a rotating identifier rather than to a key makes that answerable." (§Proving who is asking, L103 @`181569b64`).
- **Chaining survives rotation.** "Where authority is bound to a key, rotating the key breaks the delegation, and every downstream grant has to be reissued. KERI-style delegation binds to the identifier, so rotation does not disturb the chain." (L105 @`181569b64`). Adds a consequence the rest of the source set does not state: "it keeps multi-step delegation to software agents off the reissuance treadmill" (L105).
- **Open-loop verification, named as such.** "verification is open-loop: a stranger can check authority without consulting its issuer" (L105 @`181569b64`, citing `sda.md`). This is the same property the `keri-doctrine.md` shibboleth row "A registry / status endpoint is consulted at verification time" turns on. `sda.md` is `raw/06`'s source; the phrasing is recorded here only because `active-discovery` is this note's source.
- **The honest concession about tribal loyalty.** "I recommend KERI with ACDCs, for reasons I have developed elsewhere rather than a preference for the local tribe." (L101 @`181569b64`), followed by a ranked list of alternatives — OID4VP first, then W3C VCs, then DIDComm — and the verdict that "All of them satisfy the part of the requirement that the architecture depends on: a stranger can check the evidence without a callback… a shop that already runs one of these should use it and know what it is paying." (L111 @`181569b64`). **This is the most non-advocacy sentence in the entire priority-paper set** and is worth quoting in any chapter that risks reading as a KERI sales document.

### 11.2 Two methodological items

- **An author retracting his own prior claim, in print.** "There is a fifth property I used to claim, and I am retracting it here… In talks and drafts I said a design like this should leave no aggregator at all… I no longer think that is achievable for discovery among strangers, and the reason looks structural, not like a shortage of cleverness on my part." (active-discovery §What the design must deliver, L51 @`181569b64`). The replacement invariant: "*No party can act on the linkage alone, and whoever holds a piece of it is blinded to its meaning*. That is what a split does. It is not what an absence does." (L57 @`181569b64`). Relevant to this corpus twice over: it is a direct instance of standards §3's append-and-correct discipline practiced by the author himself, and it is a caution that "a design Hardman advocated in a talk" is not a stable citation.
- **A disclaimed post-quantum claim, correctly scoped.** "KERI's pre-rotation commits to the next key with a quantum-safe hash rather than exposing it, so control survives even an adversary who later breaks the signing algorithm." (L107 @`181569b64`). Note the scoping — *control* survives, not every signature — which is `wtbo`'s framing rather than `kspqs`'s. A third data point for §9.1.
