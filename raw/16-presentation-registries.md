## 1. #1095 — the origin document and its security argument

The frame is the tripartite Issuer–Holder–Verifier model, with the Issuee as Holder ("This imbues the ACDC with what others call strong Holder binding"). The Verifier must verify two things, and #1095's whole contribution is that **these two are not symmetric**:

> "1) That Issuance came from the Issuer (Issuer impersonation fraud) 2) The Presenter of the ACDC at the time of presentation is indeed the Issuee. (Issuee impersonation fraud)"

**Issuer-side: rampant, so detectability is mandatory.**

> "the worst form of fraud with the most widespread potential of harm would be rampant impersonation fraud that could result when the Issuer's signing keys are compromised. This could annihilate trust in the ecosystem… The ROI to an attacker for compromising the Issuer keys could be huge, especially if that Issuer is a root-of-trust for Identity assurance for an ecosystem."

The mitigation is the existing TEL-anchored-in-KEL structure, and Sam states the resulting property in strong terms: "an impersonator cannot impersonate the Issuer of an ACDC merely by compromising the signing keys of the Issuer. The impersonator MUST anchor the ACDC in a TEL that is anchored in the Issuer's KEL. This anchor enables the Issuer to detect any fraudulent issuances without requiring the cooperation of any other party." Plus a claim worth flagging as a claim: "AFAIK, only KERI/ACDC provides such protection in a decentralized identity system."

The reasoning is explicitly the detect-and-recover posture: "Issuer key compromise has both high susceptibility and non-zero vulnerability, therefore protection comes from recoverability. Detect and recover."

**Issuee-side: localized, so protection is optional and elective.** Three reasons given:

1. **Low ROI.** "Because the fraud is localized to a single Issuee, the ROI to the attacker for compromising the Issuee's keys is low." Plus a note that prophylactic rotation is available and effective, "because the verifier always checks the presentation as being signed by the latest keys of the Issuee."
2. **Use-case-specific mitigations are acceptable here.** A Verifier could require a key rotation as part of the presentation. Sam concedes the cost openly: "This violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party, but for localized harm, the party most likely to be harmed is the Verifier… which provides incentives for them to protect themselves." He also names the underlying capability as distinctive: the Issuee's ability "to rotate signing keys without reissuing the ACDC is a unique super-power of KERI/ACDC as a decentralized identity system."
3. **The Issuee can add its own detectability** — which is the usage registry.

### The mechanism as first stated

> "The ACDC must include a requirement that the ACDC is only verifiable when the presentation of the ACDC by the Issuee is anchored as the latest state in an Issuee-controlled *usage registry*. The verifier won't accept the credential if it's also not anchored in the Issuee's (in addition to the Issuer's) registry. The anchor enables the Issuee to detect fraudulent presentations of its credentials. The Issuee can then do a rotation recovery of its keys and also dispute the fraudulent presentations."

Four properties that #1613 does not restate and that the bible has nowhere:

- **One registry, many ACDCs, no leak.** "A *usage registry* can be employed to track the usage of one or more ACDCs by the Issuee… This means that the registry does not leak specific usage of a given ACDC to third parties." The blindable state registry is what makes many-to-one safe.
- **Linkability caveat, with its escape.** "A given usage registry is bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable. A bulk-issued ACDC with independent AIDs can remove this linkage." So the correlation story is *conditional on* independent-registry bulk issuance, which ties this directly to that workstream.
- **The verifier's time-delay trick.** "by adding a time delay from presentation anchor to acceptance, the verifier can assume that a compromised Issuee would do a recovery, invalidating the fraudulent presentation." This converts detection into prevention at the cost of latency, and it appears nowhere else in the corpus.
- **The Issuee needs its own Registrar.** "the Issuee must have its own Registrar to manage its usage registries." That is real infrastructure, and it is why the last sentence of the section reads: "This is an optional protection measure. Many Issuees will feel that their key management is sufficient… and therefore would not want the extra protection and friction of a usage Registrar."

**Incentive design.** The Verifier is given a reason to enforce: "The verifier has a vested interest in not accepting unanchored presentations as the presentation of an Issuee unanchored ACDC that declares it must be Issuee usage anchored, must be considered fraudulent."

**Provisioning, and the tentativeness of the field choice.** "An Issuee could request at the time of Issuance that their ACDC be backed by an Issuee usage registry. In response, the Issuer issues a variant of the ACDC that designates that it is to be Issuee presentation protected. **That designation needs to be defined. This could be an `rd` field in the attribute section.**" As of #1095 the field is a suggestion, not a design.

**Diagrams (not vendored here):** `PresentationUsageRegistrarObserver` and `PresentationUsageRegistryTEL`, attached to the discussion body.

## 1a. #1098 — the registry as a reason to refuse unlinkable ZKPs

*Section inserted rather than renumbered, for the same reason as §4a. `raw/11` owns #1098's age-proof mechanism; this section takes only what bears on registries and detectability.*

[#1098](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1098) (`[proposal]`) argues for a static sparse-Merkle-tree age predicate over a ZKP predicate proof, and the deciding argument is detectability, not cost. The Issuer-side half is the argument §1 already records from #1095, restated as a constraint on cryptographic choices: with unlinkable ZKPs "the Issuer of the credential is unable, by virtue of that unlinkability, to detect that a credential has been fraudulently issued"; "Detection requires that the Issuer collude with or cooperate with the verifier and/or holder, which cooperation breaks unlinkability"; so "the use of unlinkable ZKPs is therefore precluded to preserve the detectability of an Issuer signing infrastructure compromise…" (the sentence ends "as one of the inviolable properties of KERI/ACDCs").

**The Issuee-side half is what this note needs.** "Likewise, the key compromise of an Issuee (Holder) can be made detectable using a presentation registry. Which would also be defeated by an unlinkable ZKP." The consequence drawn is specific: "a given Issuee (Holder) wallet could be impersonated and used to present fraudulent proofs of age." The conclusion counts both halves as what the SMT approach buys — "strong security (detectability of key compromise for both the Issuer and the Issuee)" — and the usage section names the presentation registry's role by function: "anti-fraud protection derived from the strong detectability properties of key compromise with anchored KERI/ACDCs for Issuers and anchored presentations for Issuees".

Three things follow, all `[proposal]`.

**The presentation registry is used as a premise in a design decision, not only proposed.** #1095 presents Issuee-side protection as "an optional protection measure" (§1). #1098 does not contradict that — it says detectability "can be made" available, not that it must be used — but it treats the *possibility* of a presentation registry as something a credential format must not foreclose. A cryptographic choice that would make an Issuee registry unenforceable is ruled out on that ground alongside the Issuer-side ground. That is a stronger status for the idea than "optional protection measure": optional to use, but mandatory to keep possible.

**It names the property the registry protects as one KERI treats as non-negotiable.** "Inviolable" is attached in #1098 to Issuer-side detectability; the Issuee-side sentence follows with "Likewise". So the Issuee registry is offered as the presentation-time analogue of the property KERI ranks highest, which is a sharper statement of purpose than #1613's two benefits (§2.3) and agrees with KERIcon's "not just the issuer" (§5).

**Provenance caution.** #1098 was created 2025-10-27, two weeks after #1095 coined "usage registry", and was last edited 2026-09-26. The fetched body says "presentation registry". Without the edit history this note cannot say whether that sentence was in the October 2025 text or was added in September 2026, so it is evidence of Sam's position at 2026-09-26, not of when he first held it. §2.7 records the same pattern in #1613: these bodies change silently.

## 2. #1613 — the developed design

### 2.0 What the discussion says about its own standing, and the hierarchy the registry sits in

Two framing facts bear on how much weight §2 carries.

**#1613 declares itself a partial supersession.** Its opening line: "this discussion supersedes much of the “Multiply Endorsed” discussion (see #1555).    It does not supersede the proposal for a new edge operator." Both halves matter. The supersession explains why #1555 is quiet; the carve-out means the edge-operator proposal in #1555 is still live and is a separate design decision from anything here. **#1555 is not in this bible's source manifest** (`refresh/sources.yaml`), so the corpus is currently reading a discussion that positions itself against a document the corpus does not track. That is a manifest question, not something a refresh may fix unattended — it is raised in §8 item 8. #1627 states the relationship from the other side and dates it: "#1555 predates #1613 which largely superseded #1555" (#1627 body, snapshot 2026-09-15).

**A presentation registry is the top of a three-rung preference order, not one option among equals.** #1613 §Authentication Factor Types names the three factor types as registry anchor, KEL anchor and signature, and then imposes a strict precedence: "the highest-preference factor is used, and the others are ignored." So a presentation registry, where one is required, is not merely *sufficient* to authenticate a `grant` — it *displaces* the alternatives. This is the discussion's own version of the framing #1618 restates symmetrically in §3, stated by Sam first and with an ordering #1618 does not give. Note the register: the preference is argued from perpetual verifiability, fraud detectability and lifecycle state, and #1613 is explicit that "a bare signature does not" provide the first two.

### 2.1 The trigger is a three-part test

> "The issuer of a given ACDC can signal to a verifier that the `grant` message in an IPEX MUST be anchored in a presentation registry controlled by the Issuee of that ACDC at the time of presentation… That signal is provided when both a non-empty `rd` field and a valid AID as the Issuee in the issuee `i` field are provided at the top level of the ACDCs attribute `a` section (not the top level of the ACDC itself) **and the `rd` field at the top level of the ACDC is not empty**."

All three conditions. If there is no Issuee `i` in `a`, an `rd` in `a` imposes no anchoring requirement. If the ACDC's *top-level* `rd` is empty or missing, then the `rd` in `a` "refers to an ACDC state registry, not an IPEX presentation registry." The top-level `rd` is doing disambiguation work, which is easy to miss.

### 2.2 What the registry must contain

> "the value of the registry `rd` field in the attribute `a` section MUST be the SAID of the `rip` event of the associated presentation Registry. The Issuee of the associated ACDC, who is also a Grantor of that IPEX, MUST control this registry. The value of the ACDC SAID field in the blinded attribute block of the latest non-vacuous event in that presentation Registry must be the SAID of the `grant` message."

Note the field reuse: the slot that normally holds an ACDC SAID holds a `grant` SAID instead.

keripy at `94f5423a3` enforces three of the four requirements in that quotation and not the fourth, all `[code]`. The `rd` value must be the registry's `rip` SAID: `_vetRegistry` raises `MisregistryError` when the verified chain's `regid` differs from the declared one ("Require the vetted chain to be the registry named by the ACDC.", `ipexing.py:2073`). The Issuee must control the registry: `vetBinds` raises `MisbindingError` when the incepter differs from the required controller ("if controller is not None and record.issuer != controller:", `regeventing.py:714`), and the presentation path passes the `a`-section `i` as the controller. The latest non-vacuous event must bind the `grant` SAID: `vetBinds` walks back over vacuous updates and requires the first non-vacuous one to have `td == target`, where the target is the `grant` SAID (§7.2). The clause "who is also a Grantor of that IPEX" is not enforced: the Issuee need not sign or anchor the `grant`, and the registry alone authenticates it (§2.6, §7.3).

**KRAM couples to this.** "in order to pass KRAM, a `grant` message anchor must happen in a timely fashion relative to the IPEX exchange. The `grant` message… must be created, then anchored, and then pass the receiver's KRAM." So a presentation registry inserts a registry write into the critical path of a freshness window — see `bible/09-kram-and-request-authentication.md`.

keripy does not make the registry anchor pass KRAM; it makes the registry check depend on KRAM having already passed (`[code]` at `94f5423a3`). KRAM records a receiver timestamp, `rdt`, documented as "receiver datetime when KRAM accepted the complete message" (`src/keri/recording.py:532`), and the presentation check refuses without it: "A presentation requirement needs a completed KRAM acceptance." (`ipexing.py:2181`). The registry history is then cut off at that time: `vetBinds` drops updates whose `dt` is later and rejects a registry whose `rip` is later ("Presentation registries use the grant's KRAM acceptance time as this historical cutoff;", `regeventing.py:655-656`). Two consequences the design text does not state. The timeliness test compares the TEL event's own `dt`, which the Issuee asserts, against the receiver's clock; the KEL event anchoring that TEL event may arrive afterwards, because a `grant` waiting on it escrows and is later accepted (`tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2955`: "Vic has the TEL update but not the KEL event that authenticates it."). And since the bound target is this `grant`'s SAID, which cannot exist before the `grant` is built, the binding event is necessarily created after the `grant` body is fixed — a lower bound by construction and an upper bound by the asserted `dt`. The tests build a draft `grant`, bind its SAID in the registry, then rebuild the identical `grant` with the proofs attached (`tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2706`: "Fix the Grant before binding it in Core's presentation registry."). See §8 item 5.

### 2.3 Two benefits, only one of which is fraud protection

**Fraud protection, and its precondition.** "an imposter that merely compromises the Grantor's signing infrastructure can't avoid the requirement without also compromising the Issuer." The Issuer must be a *different party*, and Sam states the self-issued hole explicitly: "For ACDCs that the Grantor self-issues, this is only a vulnerability if the Issuance authentication factor is merely an unanchored signature. An imposter who controls the Grantor's signing infrastructure can create ACDCs whose authentication factor is an unanchored signature."

Multi-ACDC DAGs multiply the work: "When multiple ACDCs in the granted DAG each have a different presentation registry ID field value… the verifier must verify all of the anchors." And a precise limit on what one anchor buys: because the `grant`'s `o` field names the DAG's origin, "anchoring the exchange in one presentation registry provides a perpetually verifiable proof of the presentation of all ACDCs in that DAG, **but not necessarily a fresh proof of control for all Issuee AIDs in that DAG**. Multiple anchors in different registries provide multiple vectors of detectability and multiple fresh proofs-of-control; the perpetual verifiability is redundant."

The fraud-protection half is `[code]` on the verifier side at `94f5423a3`, including the property that makes it work: a presenter cannot opt out. The requirement is read from issuance metadata on every presentation `grant`, independent of `ax`: "The disclosed Issuee registry makes its proof mandatory for this proxy presentation; omitting it must not disable the check." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8213-8214`). A presenter cannot hide the declaration by compacting the attribute section either, because a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` is compact is refused outright: "A compact attribute commits to the same ACDC but hides whether the issuer mandated this presentation registry." (`test_ipexing.py:8291-8292`; `_presentationRegistries` returns `None` for a string `a`, `ipexing.py:2119-2120`). The multi-ACDC rule is `[code]` in its strict form: `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` collects every declaration across the walked DAG and fails the whole `grant` if any one does not bind it ("One valid presentation registry cannot replace the child's proof.", `test_ipexing.py:8521`; "Reject a DAG when one rd-bearing node omits presentation evidence.", `tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2381`). Because each registry must bind this `grant`'s SAID, each one is also a fresh proof of control for its Issuee, which is the "multiple fresh proofs-of-control" case. Two cases leave a declaration unenforced, and neither is the one-anchor-covers-the-DAG case Sam hedges about. The origin of an issuance `grant` is exempt, because issuance is not a presentation (§7.2). And an `i`/`rd` pair in an `A` aggregate entry is not a declaration at all (§2.4). Nothing checks that Issuer and Issuee differ, so the self-issued hole Sam concedes is present in code exactly as conceded.

**Correlation resistance, which works even when fraud protection cannot.** If a Grantor directly anchors a `grant` SAID in its public KEL and the Grantee does the same, "a third party… would be able to detect the same grant SAID in both the Grantor's KEL and the Grantee's KEL, thereby correlating the IPEX." Anchoring in a blinded presentation registry instead means "neither the grant SAID nor any other artifacts of the granted DAG appear in the KEL of the Grantor," so "a Grantee could anchor every message in the IPEX without providing a point of correlation between the KELs of the Grantor and Grantee."

**The self-issued bespoke pattern.** A Grantor can get correlation resistance with no cooperating Issuer by self-issuing a bespoke origin ACDC carrying `i` and `rd` in its `a` section — "This means the Grantor is both Issuer and Issuee of that bespoke ACDC." With a hard constraint attached: "the bespoke ACDC itself must use a blinded state Registry for its Issuer authentication so that the SAID of its ACDC is not correlatable. If it uses a direct KEL anchor, then that anchor itself would be correlatable. If it used a bare attached signature… then the ACDC itself would not be perpetually verifiable despite using a perpetually verifiable presentation registry."

### 2.4 The `rd`-in-`a` ambiguity, and Sam's own doubt

The v1 spec also allows `rd` in `a` as a *hidden ACDC state registry*. #1613 proposes EGF-declared disambiguation with "The default, if not otherwise specified, should be for anchoring presentation exchanges," floats "We may want to limit the use case to only presentation registries in the v1.1 spec," and then argues the hiding case down: its only benefit arises "when the ACDC in compact form is made public, independent of any given presentation, and the fact of it having a registry must remain undisclosed. It is not clear that this is a worthy use case… Given the potentially dubious value, we might want to forbid using the `rd` field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry." The section ends genuinely unresolved: "Not sure if either is a worthy use case."

**The two uses are mutually exclusive per ACDC.** The same section states it flatly: "a presentation registry may not be used with an ACDC that uses the `rd` field in the attribute `a` section as an Issuer-controlled ACDC state." One `rd` slot, one meaning, chosen at issuance and not recoverable later. That raises the stakes on the disambiguation question in §8 item 2 considerably — this is not a labelling nicety but a permanent either/or imposed on every targeted ACDC that wants either feature. It also supplies the argument #1613 uses to talk itself out of the hiding case: hiding "is not useful with full independent Registry bulk issuance since each presentation could use a different member of the bulk issued set", so the correlation problem hiding solves is already solved better elsewhere, and the cost of keeping it is forfeiting presentation registries for that ACDC.

**And v1.1 did not touch any of this.** `raw/03` reports §Registry SAID Field and the reserved-fields row byte-identical between `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`, with "usage registry" occurring exactly once at either pin. Spot-checked here and confirmed. So none of the three resolutions #1613 floats has been taken up in the branch most likely to carry it, and §8 item 2 has its negative answer for `v1.1` rather than its resolution.

**keripy has taken one of them.** At `94f5423a3` the code implements #1613's three-part test as the disambiguation rule, confined to the attribute section, and refuses the hiding case rather than supporting it (`[code]`). With a non-empty top-level `rd`, an `rd` and `i` pair at the top level of a disclosed `a` is a presentation-registry declaration: "Only the top-level attribute pair declares a presentation registry." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:7977`; `ipexing.py:2125-2138`). The same pair in an `A` aggregate entry is not: "Aggregate registries have application-specific semantics and do not become presentation requirements merely from an i/rd pair." (`test_ipexing.py:7991-7992`). That narrows keripy to exactly what #1613 says, which names the attribute `a` section only. Without a top-level `rd`, an `rd` in `a` is classed as "hidden issuer registry, not a presentation registry." (`ipexing.py:2110`) and the ACDC fails verification; the Issuer-authentication code is explicit that this form "must not fall through to another factor." (`ipexing.py:1845`; test `test_ipex_v2_rejects_hidden_issuer_registry_signature_fallback`, `tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:5505`: "A Grant cannot replace an unsupported hidden issuer registry with a signature."). The refusal is for `a` only; an `rd` in an `A` entry of an ACDC with no top-level `rd` is not examined. That is `[code]` for the closest of #1613's options — "forbid using the rd field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry" — with one difference: #1613 floated that as a way to "enable a presentation registry for an ACDC without an Issuer state registry", and keripy does not enable that; a presentation registry is recognized only when a top-level `rd` is present. It is an implementation choice, not a spec resolution (§8 item 2).

### 2.5 Interaction with `ax`

The presentation-registry requirement is independent of and stronger than `ax`, where an Issuer-signaled presentation registry applies and the Grantor is the Issuee: "Notwithstanding the presence or absence of the ax field" … "then that grant message MUST be anchored in that registry for the grant to be valid." (#1613 > New Anchored Exchange ax Field). "Otherwise, the presumption is that the grant is fraudulent." With a liability claim attached: "Any verifier (Disclosee) accepting such a `grant` is presumptively in violation of any contractual or regulatory protection afforded to the real Grantor by colluding with a fraudulent grantor."

And a scoping judgment about which registry may be used: "There is no security advantage to using a presentation registry specified by the Grantor, as the main purpose of a presentation registry is to allow the Grantor to detect a compromise of its signing key infrastructure… Thus, the main use case for impersonation fraud detection of the Grantor requires a pre-specified presentation registry **by an Issuer different from the Grantor**, which must be prespecified in one of the ACDCs not in the `grant` message." On tooling: "we should not add special tooling for this special case; instead, we should use the existing tooling for a bulk-issued ACDC to handle it."

**The `ax` requirement has an alternative-satisfaction rule, and it is what landed in code.** #1613 §Origin AID Anchoring proposes that when `ax` is truthy, "either the sender must anchor the grant or the Issuee or Issuer of the origin ACDC of the grant must anchor the grant" — three acceptable anchorers, applied in that order of priority, so that a sender acting as a proxy negotiator for an undisclosed AID can still satisfy the requirement. The rule exists to let the important Grantor stay out of the exchange until the `grant`. It is deliberately partial: "we choose not to define every possible combination; we define only the most important and leave the rest to the EGF or future development." All three are implemented at `94f5423a3`, and a presentation-registry anchor counts as one of them (`[code]`). Under a truthy `ax`, `IpexHandler.verify` collects the AIDs that anchored the `grant` — the sender via its own source-seal couple, any non-sender via an explicit source-seal triple, and every Issuee authenticated by a presentation registry (`anchorers = set(tethered)`, `ipexing.py:801`) — and accepts if any of them is one of three: "A truthy ax requires one permitted Grant anchorer." (`ipexing.py:797`), over `candidates = (serder.pre, origin.iseaid, origin.israid)` (`:816`). The code checks membership in a set, so #1613's "order of priority" has no operational effect — any one suffices, which is what #1613's next sentence says anyway. The code at this pin states no policy about narrowing the three; the test `test_ipex_v2_origin_issuee_seal_satisfies_proxy_grant_ax` shows the origin Issuee's own KEL seal qualifying a proxy's `grant` ("The proxy is the sender, but it is not the qualifying anchorer.", `tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8666`). Separately, the independence clause at the head of this subsection is `[code]`: the presentation-registry check runs on every presentation `grant` whether or not `ax` is present (§2.3), and #1613's rejection of a Grantor-specified registry has a `[code]` counterpart in that the code recognizes only registries declared in a DAG node; nothing on the `grant` itself can name one.

### 2.6 Double authentication, and why KRAM cannot see a presentation registry

This material is in #1613's 2026-09-02 edit, and it changes how §2.2's KRAM coupling should be read.

A presentation registry anchor does **not** substitute for the ordinary signature or KEL anchor on a `grant`. #1613 §ACDC Grantor Authentication Factor: "KRAM does not recognize or process a presentation registry-based authentication factor for the grant message," and therefore "a presentation registry-anchored grant message is always double-authenticated." The requirement is stated as an obligation, not an observation: "When the Grantor as Issuee for the required presentation registry is also the grant sender, then the Grantor MUST be doubly authenticated; otherwise, the grant message will not pass KRAM." Sam gives the layering reason plainly — "the processing of presentation registry validation happens higher up in the stack than grant message authentication" — and calls the resulting duplication "unavoidable."

Three consequences worth carrying. The `grant` therefore carries an **ephemeral** factor and a **perpetually verifiable** one simultaneously, which is exactly the combination §2.0's preference order would otherwise suppress — the precedence rule that says a higher factor makes lower ones "ignored" does not reach across the KRAM boundary, because KRAM is below it. A verifier that treats registry anchoring as *replacing* the signature check will reject valid grants or accept invalid ones depending on which way it errs. And the latency argument in §8 item 5 is worse than it looked: the registry write does not replace a step, it is added to one.

This is also the paragraph where the absence of any presentation-registry handling in `src/keri/core/kraming.py` stops being a gap and becomes conformance. #1613 says KRAM must not process it, and keripy puts the check in `ipexing.py`, above KRAM (§7).

**What the code does — confirmed for the sender, departed from for anyone else.** Both halves are `[code]` at `94f5423a3`.

When the Issuee whose registry is declared is also the `grant` sender — every SEDI test, where the holder sends — double authentication holds by construction: KRAM authenticates the sender on the `grant` as it does for any exchange, and the registry check runs above it in `IpexHandler.verify`. The layering is as #1613 describes. The sender factor may be a signature or a direct KEL anchor of the `grant` ("KRAM accepts either sender authentication factor before IPEX validates the Issuee's presentation registry.", `tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8178-8179`); `_sign` attaches a sender signature only "when no sender anchor was created so the wire carries one sender factor." (`ipexing.py:438`). `kraming.py` is unchanged between `80f77b73b` and `94f5423a3` and contains no presentation-registry logic; its only involvement is recording the acceptance time the registry check consumes (§2.2).

When the Issuee is not the sender, keripy does not require the Issuee to endorse the `grant`. The proxy test is explicit: "The proxy signature authenticates the sender while the timely Issuee TEL supplies Bob's fresh proof without multiple endorsement." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8186-8187`). The `grant` is accepted with only the proxy's signature attached, and the test asserts that no Issuee signature or seal was stored (`:8193-8197`). #1613 §ACDC Grantor Authentication Factor says the opposite for this case: "To be accepted by KRAM, the grant message must be endorsed by each Grantor", hence "a presentation registry-anchored grant message is always double-authenticated." A reader of #1613's unedited body will meet that text, and it is not what keripy does. The shipped behavior is deliberate. A check requiring the Issuee's endorsement was written and withdrawn inside PR #1693, so no first-parent commit on `main` has carried it: commit `790797bcd` (2026-09-21) carried "A registry supplements, but never replaces, a Grant endorsement." (`ipexing.py:1543` at `4c53f56a1`) and `b7c170f93` "remove grantor requisite" deleted it. A different Grantor check, which scoped declarations to participating Grantors, was likewise written and removed inside PR #1734 (§8 item 11).

The two can be reconciled on #1613's own definitions, but only by a step #1613 does not take. In #1613 the Grantors are "the set of endorser AIDs to the grant message", and the registry "MUST be anchored … when the Issuee is the Grantor of that IPEX" (§Registry Anchored Granted ACDCs). In the code, an Issuee who never endorses is still authenticated, through the registry alone: it enters the `tethered` set and can satisfy `ax` (§2.5). Effectively a presentation-registry anchor makes the Issuee a Grantor without an endorsement. That is coherent — the anchor is a KEL-anchored commitment to the exact `grant` SAID, which is at least as strong as a signature — but it is a design change, and #1613 does not record it. One side effect follows from reading the code, though no test asserts it: the endorser index `eidx` (`src/keri/db/basing.py:608`, `:1184`) is filled only from accepted non-sender endorsements ("Only behavior-approved non-sender endorsements enter this durable", `src/keri/peer/exchanging.py:752`), so an Issuee authenticated by registry alone is not indexed against the `grant`. The multi-registry test shows the same pattern with two Issuees: "The sender signature and both Issuee TEL factors accept the Grant" (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8534`), and neither Issuee signs. See §8 item 12.

## 4a. #1627 — where the presentation-architecture argument touches registries

*Section inserted rather than renumbered, because `raw/16 §6` and `§8` are cited from `refresh/prompts/standards.md`, `raw/03` and `keri-doctrine.md`.*

#1627 surveys four ways to shape a credential presentation — Portable Datalake, Credential Soup, Verifiable DAG, DAG Soup — and argues for the ACDC DAG. `bible/08` already tracks its list form for `dp`, `o` and `ax`. What chapter 10 needs is the argument, and specifically where it bears on registries. Note throughout that this is `[proposal]`: a design discussion, and one whose body never uses the words "presentation registry."

**The bespoke origin ACDC is specified here in terms #1613 says forfeit correlation resistance.** #1627's central proposal for the SEDI case is a lightweight bespoke ACDC as the DAG's origin — a "recipe" that converts a soup into a DAG — and it is explicit that the recipe is cheap: "This origin does not need to be anchored or use a registry, so it's simple." It "merely needs to be signed and have attached signatures because it's a one-time use ACDC." **That is precisely the construction #1613 §Correlation Protected Registry rules out.** #1613 requires that the self-issued bespoke ACDC "must use a blinded state Registry for its Issuer authentication so that the SAID of its ACDC is not correlatable," and says that a bare attached signature leaves the ACDC not perpetually verifiable "despite using a perpetually verifiable presentation registry." Both are Sam, three weeks apart, and they are not in contradiction — they are two different bespoke ACDCs for two different jobs — but nothing in either document says so, and the shared name invites the wrong one. A reader who takes #1627's recipe pattern and adds a presentation registry to it gets a correlatable presentation and a `grant` whose perpetual verifiability the origin ACDC cannot carry. This is a new open question; see §8 item 9.

**#1627 independently names the limit §2.3 states as a hedge.** #1613 says anchoring in one presentation registry proves the presentation of the whole DAG "but not necessarily a fresh proof of control for all Issuee AIDs in that DAG." #1627 reaches the same wall from the multiple-endorsement side and states the general form: "the mere presence of an AID in a DAG is not a sufficiently precise signal of required fresh endorsement." So the gap is not an artifact of registries; it is a property of using DAG membership as a signal at all, and it is unresolved in both documents. #1627 names the missing piece — "multiply-endorsed awareness" — and says "more tooling is needed."

**A fourth registry kind appears, which further undercuts the name.** Discussing reissuance cascades in an ontology DAG, #1627 proposes to "use a transfer registry where a state change is not to revoke an ACDC but to provide a forward reference" to a replacement ACDC's registry. With #1550's capability-control use, that makes at least four jobs for one primitive — issuance/revocation state, usage, agent capability, transfer — and strengthens §8 item 1's argument that the general name should not be "presentation registry." The `rd` gloss in the ACDC spec (§6) already lists transfer among the purposes, so this one is `[spec]` as a field purpose even though `[proposal]` as a reissuance strategy.

**The comment stream, which is where the registries actually are.** Four of #1627's five comments are dhh1128's and Sam's, 2026-08-25 to 2026-09-02. Two are load-bearing here. dhh1128 on 2026-09-01 flags an implementation conflict: #1629 "merged with `o` as a scalar string, and `IpexHandler.verify` now rejects a list outright" while #1627's preferred design requires the list form. keripy resolves that conflict in the list form's favour: at `94f5423a3` `IpexHandler.verify` requires `ax` and `o` to be single-entry lists (`_validSingleDagList`, `src/keri/acdc/ipexing.py:576`, `:594`, `:715`), so the comment describes code that no longer exists. Sam's reply on 2026-09-02 is the comment that broke §0's silence and that was subsequently promoted into #1613's body (§2.7).

## 4b. #1740 — auxiliary exchanges for blindable state registries

Section inserted rather than renumbered, for the same reason as §4a. Everything in #1740 is `[proposal]`: one author's draft, posted with "So I have completed my draft of this discussion." (#1740 comment, SmithSamuelM, 2026-10-30) and followed by a request to "add examples of the attachments for unblinding" (#1740 comment, SmithSamuelM, 2026-11-01).

### Where it belongs, and why it is mined here

#1740 is about the Issuer's state registry, not about a presentation registry. Its registries are the ones "that governs the state of the associated ACDC" (#1740 > Auxiliary Data Exchanges > Issuance Exchange), whose events carry the `issued` state; the body never says "presentation registry", "usage registry", "issuee registry" or "issuee-controlled". Sam's own distinction applies: an Issuer registry tracks ACDC state, while "A presentation registry on the other hand is not tracking ACDC state, but is merely logging presentations" ([#1574 comment](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1574#discussioncomment-17938568), SmithSamuelM, 2026-08-07; re-read 2026-11-03; mined as `raw/21` R-042). Its vocabulary is the specification's: "blindable state registries", the `bup` event, and the `rd` that names a registry by "the SAID of the `rip` event" (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for DHKE). `raw/03` owns that construct, and its spec text says only that the blind derives from a salt the Issuer shares with the Discloser, not how the salt travels (`raw/03`, Blindable state / BLID). #1740 is the first written proposal for how it travels.

It is mined here because it touches this note's material at four points. It is the second IPEX design to separate an issuance exchange from a presentation exchange, which keripy's presentation-registry code also does (§7.2). It proposes a rule about `admit` that sits on top of the state recording keripy does (§7.9). It names an edge case that keripy's registry verification answers by refusal (§8 item 17). And it changes the issuance order in a way keripy's `grant` verification does not accept (§8 item 15). The IPEX message semantics as a whole are `raw/09`'s and `bible/08`'s.

### The two exchanges

The premise: "Both an IPEX issuance exchange and an IPEX presentation exchange need the exchange of auxiliary data related to the blindable state registries" (#1740 > Introduction). The mechanism is optional and in-band: "This discussion describes an optional in-band mechanism and its trade-offs." (#1740 > Introduction).

For issuance, the Issuer and Issuee "must exchange a cryptographic-strength (high-entropy) shared secret salt" (#1740 > Auxiliary Data Exchanges > Issuance Exchange), and "The mechanism described here assumes each registry in a given issuance gets a unique salt." (same). The proposed way is "via a Diffie-Hellman key exchange (DHKE)" (same), carried by a signed `qry` from Issuer to Issuee and a signed `rpy` back, on route `/ipex/dhke`, each holding the exchange identifier `x` and a `pk` field map from registry `rd` to that side's public key: "Each public key is identified by the registry identifier for the associated registry." (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for DHKE). The pair is bound to the IPEX by `x` alone: "This ephemerally binds the auxiliary message pair to the associated IPEX issuance exchange without changing the IPEX exchange messages." (same).

For presentation, the Discloser normally needs no extra exchange: "Typically, the discloser can attach this set of unblinded attribute blocks to the `grant` message in the exchange." (#1740 > Auxiliary Data Exchanges > Presentation Exchange). The auxiliary pair covers one edge case, a Discloser that "does not have an active internet connection at the time of the presentation" (same) while the verifier holds later registry events. Then "the disclosee (verifier) needs a way to provide these later registry events to the discloser (presenter)" (same). The `qry` on route `/ipex/unblind` carries `x` and an `re` map from registry `rd` to a list of event SAIDs, with the events attached as nested substreams; the `rpy` returns unblinded blocks for the subset the Discloser chooses, because "a malicious verifier may ask for more events to be unblinded than it actually needs." (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for Unblinding Registry State).

### Why a sidecar rather than new IPEX messages

The argument is correlation. IPEX messages are hash-chained and may be KEL-anchored, so anything in the chain is publicly auditable once anchored. "Using a non-hash chained with the other IPEX message ephemeral pair of messages for the DHKE prevents the DHKE from becoming publicly correlatable." (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for DHKE). The messages are disposable: "These can be deleted after the DHKE completes." (same). The timing argument is that the sidecar runs while the IPEX is live, after `grant` and before `admit`, so neither side does the work for an exchange that is later abandoned, and an out-of-band exchange before the IPEX would have no `x` to name.

### The `admit` rule, in both directions

#1740 makes `admit` conditional on registry state. For issuance: "we propose that the `admit` message MUST NOT be sent by the Issuee until it has successfully verified the existence of the appropriate `issued` state" (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for DHKE). For presentation: "Consequently, the Disclosee (verifier) MUST NOT send an `admit` message until it has fully verified the state of any ACDCs in the disclosure." (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for Unblinding Registry State). Both are obligations on the sender of `admit`, not on a verifier of it, so they bind application behavior rather than message validity.

### The issuance order it proposes

With the auxiliary DHKE, the bup that records issuance comes after the `grant`: "The Issuer sends the query after its `grant` message." (#1740 > Auxiliary Mechanism for DHKE), and once both sides hold the salt, "The issuer can now issue a blindable update, `bup` event, to the registry, and subsequently the Issuee can verify it by unblinding it." (same). The out-of-band alternative keeps the old order, since "it must occur before the IPEX issuance so the Issuee can verify the registry at issuance." (same).

### What the draft leaves loose

The examples are illustrative rather than exact. The example `rpy` carries an `i` equal to the exchange identifier `x`, and its `dt` is 2020-08-21 while the `qry` it answers is dated 2025-08-21. The public keys in both examples carry the `D` code, which keripy's code table defines as an "Ed25519 verification key basic derivation" (keripy core/coring.py, MtrDex, L239 @`94f5423a3`) rather than the X25519 encryption key `C`, which the same table says "may be converted from Ed25519 or Ed25519N." (L238); #1740 does not say which. The presentation section repeats two sentences from the issuance section without adapting them, including deletion "after the DHKE completes" and an "outstanding (live) Issuance exchange". The draft mentions "an appropriate post-quantum safe key-generation algorithm" as an alternative to elliptic-curve DH and says no more. It does not say how per-registry salts relate to the bulk-issuance salt derivation `raw/21` R-044 records, beyond allowing "a hierarchy rooted in one shared secret salt" (#1740 > Issuance Exchange).

### Code status

No code implements either exchange at keripy `94f5423a3`: `git grep -i` over `src/` and `tests/` finds no `/ipex/dhke`, no `/ipex/unblind` and no "dhke". The word "unblind" occurs only as the local primitive `Blinder.unblind` (`src/keri/core/structing.py:1487`) and its test uses, which recompute a blinded block from a salt the caller already holds; no message carries a salt, a DH public key or a set of unblinded blocks requested by a verifier.

## 6. What is normative in the ACDC specification

`spec/spec-body.md` @`f0bd097de` (2026-08-28). **"Presentation registry" does not appear at all, and "usage registry" appears exactly once, in a gloss.** What does:

- **The nested-`rd` slot exists and its purpose list already includes usage.** Top-level `rd` is glossed "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, or retraction registry for ACDC" (`:23`); the *not-at-top-level* entry reads "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, retraction, **or usage** registry for ACDC when not at top-level" (`:48`). That single word is the only trace of this design in any specification.
- **Nested `rd` is explicitly open-ended.** "When the registry SAID, `rd` field is used at the top level for the Issuer's registry, a registry SAID, `rd` field that appears nested in the Attributed, `a`, or Aggregate, `A`, section MAY be used for some other registry, such as an application-specific or Issuer-specific registry" (`:85`). Note "Issuer-specific" — the spec does not contemplate an *Issuee*-controlled registry.
- **The motivating rationale in the spec is bulk issuance and graduated disclosure**, not fraud detection: nesting "may better facilitate contractually protected disclosure of the bulk-issued registry" (`:326`).
- **`rd` binds to a `rip` event.** "The Registry SAID, `rd` field value MUST be the value of the SAID, `d` field of the Registry Inception, `rip` event. Because the Issuer `i` field appears in the `rip` event, the Registry SAID, `rd` field value cryptographically binds the Registry to the Issuer AID" (`:2019`). **This is a live tension**: the `rip` binds the registry to *its own* incepting AID. For a presentation registry that AID is the Issuee, so the sentence's use of "Issuer" is about the registry's issuer, not the ACDC's — worth stating carefully in any spec text.
- **Blindable state registries are normative** and there is a worked "Registry-Dependent Issuance Lifecycle" walking `rip` → issuance → state → verification → revocation. The spec calls a `rip` "a vacuous placeholder that reveals nothing about what will later be issued" — the origin of #1613's "latest non-vacuous event" phrasing. (That lifecycle section and §Graduated Disclosure are in **`main`**, tier `[spec]`, not `v1.1`-only; `raw/03` re-anchored the "vacuous placeholder" quote at `f0bd097de`.)

**All five quotes above are present at `f0bd097de`.** `raw/03` reports the same material byte-identical in `v1.1` at `2362e48a1`, with **no Issuee-controlled registry anywhere in that branch** — zero hits for "Issuee-controlled", "Issuee controlled" or "Issuee's registry". Spot-checked here: "presentation registry" has zero occurrences and "usage registry" exactly one at either pin. The single-word trace in the reserved-fields gloss is still the only trace in any specification, on either line.

**Code has outrun both specification lines.** The spec pins were not re-read this pass; they are `raw/03`'s to advance, and §6's statements stand at `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`. keripy at `94f5423a3` has an Issuee-controlled registry in its sense exactly: a registry whose incepting AID must equal the `a`-section Issuee (§2.2, §7.2). The facade calls the write path "Bind a grant message SAID in an issuee-controlled registry." (`src/keri/acdc/registraring.py:419`). So the sentence above that the spec contemplates only an Issuer-specific registry describes a gap between specification and reference implementation, not the state of the art. An implementer following either ACDC spec line would not know that a `grant` to a keripy verifier can be refused for lacking an Issuee registry anchor that no specification mentions. That is a conformance hazard, and §8 item 13 carries it.

### 6.1 What `v1.1` adds that this design leans on, and one label collision

`raw/03` owns `v1.1` and the `dp` disclosure-paths construct; this is a cross-reference, not a re-mining.

**The origin node is now normatively defined, which §2.3's argument needs.** `v1.1` §Disclosure Paths at `2362e48a1`: "That DAG MUST have exactly one source node, called the origin node." The whole of §2.3's claim — that committing to the origin ACDC's SAID commits to every ACDC reachable from it, and therefore that one presentation-registry anchor covers the DAG — rests on a DAG having exactly one origin. That premise is `[spec-next]`. The same section makes the bespoke-origin construction normative too: where an exchange conveys several unchained ACDCs, "a DAG MUST be formed by issuing a bespoke ACDC whose node is the origin." So the object #1613 and #1627 disagree about (§4a) is itself specified, while the disagreement — whether it needs a blinded state registry — is not.

**The `o` label means two different things and the spec only defines one of them.** In the ACDC specification, at both pins, `o` is the **Operator** field of an Edge or Edge-group block. The `o` this note has been calling "the `grant`'s origin field" is a field of the IPEX exchange message, which the ACDC spec does not define at all — it lives in #1613, #1627, #1629 and keripy. The collision is harmless while the two never appear in the same field map, and they do not, but any prose that says "the `o` field" without saying which one is ambiguous, and §2.3's "the `grant`'s `o` field names the DAG's origin" is `[proposal]` plus `[code]`, never `[spec]` or `[spec-next]`. The *concept* it names is `[spec-next]`; the *field carrying it* is not standardized anywhere.

## 7. keripy implementation status @`94f5423a3` (2026-10-30)

Read from code at `94f5423a3` with `git show` and `git grep`, never from a diff alone. Every claim in §§7.1–7.9 is `[code]` at `94f5423a3` unless marked otherwise.

The verifier-side presentation registry is built, on presentation `grant`s only, and it fails closed. The Issuee's write path is built. An Issuer registry's state is recorded and not judged. Discovery of a foreign registry is not built, the V2 IPEX handler is not wired into any keripy application, and nothing in either specification describes any of it.

### 7.1 What is implemented, in one paragraph

On every `/ipex/grant` that discloses a DAG, after the DAG walk, the disclosure-plan and Offer-fulfillment checks, the evaluation of the disclosed DAG and per-node schema validation have passed, `IpexHandler.verify` runs `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph`: "Verify every presentation registry declared for this disclosure." (`src/keri/acdc/ipexing.py:788`). For each node it reads the `(i, rd)` pair at the top level of a disclosed `a` section, provided the node also has a top-level `rd`; aggregate entries are not consulted. When the `grant` is an issuance — its sender is the origin's Issuer and its recipient `ri` the origin's Issuee — the origin's own declaration is skipped, and every other node's still applies. For each remaining pair it requires that the registry named by `rd` is incepted by that `i`; that its latest non-vacuous update, as of the receiver's KRAM acceptance time, binds this `grant`'s SAID; and that the node carries a KEL source reference from the Issuee to the binding event. Any failure rejects the `grant`. The Issuees so authenticated are returned as the `tethered` set and count as anchorers for a truthy `ax`. Once the `grant` has cleared every check, the Issuer-registry state of each registry-backed node is written to `Baser.exrs` (§7.9). The other five verbs never consult a presentation registry, which matches #1613's statement that the `grant` is "the presentation".

### 7.2 What is checked, and where it fails closed

The chain is `verify` → `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` (`ipexing.py:2140`) → `_presentationRegistries` (`:2099`) → `_vetRegistry` (`:2009`) → `regeventing.vetBinds` (`src/keri/acdc/regeventing.py:647`) → `regeventing.vet`. Each step refuses rather than skipping:

- Declaration discovery (`_presentationRegistries`). The trigger is #1613's three-part test on the attribute section: "Both fields are required to select a presentation registry." (`:2125`), and a top-level `rd` must be present. Without it, an inner `a.rd` is "hidden issuer registry, not a presentation registry." (`:2110`), which fails the node (§2.4). A compact `a` on a registry-backed ACDC returns `None` and fails the `grant`, since the docstring treats as unsupported a registry-backed ACDC that "hides the top-level attribute block that carries the presentation requirement." (`:2103-2104`). A malformed `rd` or `i` fails it too. The `A` section plays no part: an `i`/`rd` pair in an aggregate entry, a compact `A` and a partially disclosed `A` all yield no declaration (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8018-8023`). So each node declares at most one presentation registry.
- Issuance exemption. `issuance = (serder.pre == origin.israid and serder.ked.get("ri", "") == origin.iseaid)` (`ipexing.py:2147-2148`), and then "Only the origin being issued is exempt." (`:2168`). The tests state both sides of the line. "The same declaration is inactive while the Issuer grants the credential to its Issuee." (`test_ipexing.py:8236-8237`), but "When the Issuer discloses the credential to someone other than its Issuee, it is a presentation and the declaration applies." (`:8252-8253`). A linked credential in an issuance `grant` is not exempt: "Omitting the child's declared registry must not be excused by the fact that the origin is undergoing original issuance." (`:8588-8589`).
- KRAM precondition. "A presentation requirement needs a completed KRAM acceptance." (`ipexing.py:2181`). With no `kramTMSC` record carrying `rdt`, the `grant` fails. `rdt` is set only when KRAM accepts the complete message: for a multi-key sender a partial-signature cache is written with an empty `rdt` until the threshold is met ("A partial cache is only a replay marker. Receiver time is" recorded once the complete threshold is accepted, `src/keri/core/kraming.py:1658-1661`; `kraming.py` is unchanged since `80f77b73b`). The registry binding must be dated no later than that acceptance: "Require a valid binding anchored before the KRAM-accepted send." (`ipexing.py:2200`). The proxy test exercises the first half: "Complete TEL evidence still fails closed without KRAM acceptance." (`test_ipexing.py:8142`). The KRAM side of this coupling is recorded in `raw/15`.
- Registry evidence. The TEL must be held locally: "Registry verification requires an injected local Regery." (`ipexing.py:1980`), and a handler without one returns `None`. A missing `rip`, head or intermediate update raises `MissingChainError`, which is retryable from escrow.
- Historical cutoff. `vetBinds` drops updates whose `dt` is later than the KRAM acceptance time and raises `MisbindingError` if the `rip` itself is later (`regeventing.py:686-705`). Issuer registries get no cutoff: "issuer registries use the current head by omitting it." (`regeventing.py:656-657`).
- Controller. "if controller is not None and record.issuer != controller:" raises `MisbindingError` (`regeventing.py:714`). This is the Issuee-control check, and it replaces the Issuer-equality check for this path (§7.4).
- Binding. Walking newest-first, every update back to the latest non-vacuous one must have exactly one disclosure attached to the node, otherwise `UnverifiedBlindError`. The first non-vacuous one must bind the target, otherwise `MisbindingError` "does not bind expected target" (`regeventing.py:747-748`). The target is the `grant` SAID (`ipexing.py:2195`). A trailing vacuous update therefore needs its own disclosure: "A trailing vacuous update preserves the latest non-vacuous binding and therefore requires its own proof alongside the presentation." (`test_ipexing.py:8073-8074`). The state word the binding carries is not checked on this path; any non-vacuous update that binds the `grant` SAID satisfies it, whatever its `ts`.
- Source. "Presentation timing requires the Grant to carry this bup's KEL source." (`regeventing.py:750`). The node's attached source triples are filtered to the Issuee and matched against the TEL events they actually seal (`ipexing.py:2023-2059`). A source whose KEL event is not yet local is retryable, and otherwise the result is `MisanchorError` and refusal (`:2086-2090`).
- Declared registry is the vetted registry. `MisregistryError` if the vetted chain's `regid` differs from the declared `rd` (`:2073-2077`). The test that a valid registry cannot be substituted for the declared one is `test_ipex_v2_rejects_substituted_presentation_registry` (`test_ipexing.py:8346`).

Every permanent error maps to `return None` (`ipexing.py:2093-2096`), and a `None` from `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` maps to `return False` in `verify` (`:794-795`). Nothing falls back to a weaker factor. A later presentation in the same registry cannot displace an accepted one, because the cutoff ignores it: "A later presentation in the same issuee registry must not replace the independently anchored proof for this already accepted grant." (`test_ipexing.py:8316-8317`).

### 7.3 Who must sign the `grant`, and the multiply-endorsed `grant`

`grant()` takes `endorsers` and `anchorers`: "Additional local AIDs that sign the Grant." while "The sender remains the Discloser and KRAM-authenticated outer sender." (`ipexing.py:2607-2608`). `_sign` attaches a signature group per endorser and, per anchorer, creates a KEL event sealing the `grant` SAID and attaches a source-seal triple naming that AID (`ipexing.py:342-467`); the sender either signs or anchors, not both (§2.6). On receipt, `verifyEvidence` keeps non-sender evidence only for `grant` — "Only Grant accepts supplemental grantor evidence." (`:2232`) — and, per its docstring, "Grants then require signatures from each endorser's current Kever while keeping perpetually verifiable historical seals" (`:2214-2216`), so a seal survives the anchorer's later rotation and a signature does not. It prefers a seal over signatures from the same AID: "A valid seal is the stronger factor for an AID, so do not" retain a redundant signature (`:2251`). Unresolved non-sender evidence on a `grant` is dropped rather than escrowed ("Grants drop unresolved optional evidence after Exchanger requests the missing KEL.", `:2218-2219`). Other verbs refuse any non-sender evidence (`:2261-2263`). Accepted non-sender endorsers are indexed in `eidx` (`src/keri/db/basing.py:608`, `src/keri/peer/exchanging.py:752-756`).

Against #1613 §Multiply Endorsed IPEX message and §KRAM Augmentation (`[proposal]`), this implements: non-sender signatures and non-sender seal-source references as endorsements; validation after KRAM rather than in it — "the IPEX processor MUST validate all non-sender endorsements after KRAM." — and the preference rule "If the seal source reference is valid, then the set of signatures is ignored." It does not implement, or this pass did not find:

- Endorsement on any verb but `grant`. #1613 says the other messages "do not need multiple endorsements", so this is conformance.
- A tethering check. #1613 §Timeliness Tethered Presentations defines tethering as the property that a Grantor's fresh proof of control extends to that AID's appearances in the DAG. The code does not require any Issuee in the DAG to be a Grantor. The only use of the word in code is the `tethered` set of registry-authenticated Issuees, consumed by the `ax` rule alone.
- A threshold check on non-sender signatures. #1613 says an endorsement whose verified signatures do not meet the endorser's threshold is invalid. This pass did not read `verifyAttachments`, so whether non-sender signature groups are threshold-checked is unverified.

#1613's own scope note still applies, since it "does not solve for the more generic use cases of a proxy negotiator for multiple other AID". The code does not either, beyond the three-AID `ax` rule. #1555 is not in the manifest and was not read, so this note cannot compare the code with #1555's design (§8 item 8).

The `grant` does not need the presentation-registry Issuee as a Grantor (§2.6). The Grantor claims in §2.2–§2.6 stand as follows at `94f5423a3`. The Issuee must control the registry: `[code]`. The Issuee "is also a Grantor": not enforced. The requirement applies "when the Issuee is the Grantor": the code applies it to every declaration in the DAG whoever sends, except the origin's own declaration in an issuance `grant`. The `grant` is "always double-authenticated": true when the Issuee is the sender, false otherwise. The sender is always KRAM-authenticated, by signature or by KEL anchor, and Grantors may be non-sender AIDs: `[code]`.

### 7.4 The Issuer-equality check is scoped, not removed

`vetBindings` requires that the ACDC's "issuer must match the issuer that incepted the presented registry" (`regeventing.py:494`). `vetBinds` calls it only for an Issuer registry, and the presentation path gets a binding label instead: "if acdc is not None else 'presentation')" (`regeventing.py:758`). The presentation path never passes `acdc`; it passes `controller=issuee` (`ipexing.py:2192-2198`), which `vetBinds` checks against the registry incepter. So keripy distinguishes an Issuee-controlled registry from an Issuer-controlled one by which `rd` named it, and enforces the right incepter for each (§8 item 4).

### 7.5 The Issuee's write path

`Registrar.present` — "Bind a grant message SAID in an issuee-controlled registry." (`src/keri/acdc/registraring.py:419`) — and `Registrar.vacate` (`:425`) delegate to the registry event engine's `present`, which validates the target as a SAID and builds a blinded update with default state `"presented"` (`regeventing.py:1005`), and `vacate`, which builds a vacuous update (`regeventing.py:1017`). Both reuse the same `_blind` path as Issuer issuance. The Issuer's `issue` now validates the ACDC against its schema before blinding ("Reject malformed credentials before deriving or staging an issuance event.", `regeventing.py:1000`); `present` has nothing comparable to validate and checks only that the target is a SAID. Nothing in `present` checks that the caller's AID is an Issuee of anything; control is enforced only on the verifier side, by the incepter check. The test flow is: build the `grant` ("Build the grant first because the presentation TEL binds its SAID.", `test_ipexing.py:8060`), `present` its SAID, anchor the update in the Issuee's KEL, optionally `vacate`, then send the same `grant` with the disclosures and the source reference attached (`test_ipexing.py:8060-8114`; `tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2706`). Sending through `grant(..., anchorers=[])` tells the builder not to add a sender KEL anchor: "empty list leaves anchoring to a presentation registry." (`ipexing.py:2611`).

### 7.6 SEDI examples after #1697

The SEDI schemas in `tests/sedi/test_sedi.py` at `94f5423a3` still allow an inner `'rd': {'description': 'Issuee Presentation Registry SAID', 'type': 'string'},` (e.g. `:147`), and the worked examples still populate it on Guy's Core credential (`"rd": guyPreRids[0],`, `:5490`). ### 7.9 Issuer registry state on the V2 path: recorded, not judged

This is about the Issuer's state registry named by a node's top-level `rd`, not about a presentation registry, and it is here because #1740 and the presentation-registry check both sit next to it. `IpexHandler` carries no state policy. Its class comment cites the ACDC spec's per-registry transaction state and the EGF's authority over acceptability (`ipexing.py:484-492`), and `_verifyIssuerAuthNode` says that when a node authenticates through a registry, the "state the Issuer blinded into the registry's head is recorded, not judged" (`ipexing.py:1785`). The store's own description is "Recorded, never judged on the protocol path." (`src/keri/db/basing.py:596`). Each record, `ExchangeRegistryStateRecord` (`src/keri/recording.py:181`), keys on the exchange SAID and the disclosed node SAID and holds the registry SAID, the head event's SAID and sequence number, the disclosed state and its timestamp. It is written only after the `grant` passes every check, so "A refused grant leaves no record behind." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:9434`), and "nothing on the protocol path reads these records back; an application or EGF evaluator does." (`recording.py:187-188`). The tests accept a `grant` whose Edge-group conjoins a revoked far node and assert that the record says `"revoked"` (`test_ipexing.py:9416-9421`), record the origin's own revoked state the same way (`:9429-9432`), and record a registry-specific state, `"withdrawn"` (`:9549-9550`): what a registry says "is recorded in ``Baser.exrs`` and never judged, because which states make an ACDC acceptable is the EGF's rule rather than the protocol's." (`:9199-9200`).

Three boundaries keep this from reaching the presentation registry. What is still enforced for an Issuer registry is authentication: the chain must verify against the Issuer's KEL, the head must bind the node's SAID, and every update back to the latest non-vacuous one must be disclosed; a TEL not yet held locally makes the node "unknown (retryable) when the verifier does not yet hold enough KEL or TEL evidence to conclude." (`ipexing.py:1797-1799`). The presentation registry's binding requirement is separate and still enforced in full (§7.2). And `exrs` holds only Issuer-registry records collected during the DAG evaluation; `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` writes nothing to it. At `80f77b73b` the V2 path neither judged nor recorded Issuer-registry state, so the change between the pins is that the state is now kept.

## 8. Open questions and unresolved tensions

Each item states its position at the current pins: keripy `94f5423a3`, ACDC spec `main` `f0bd097de` and `v1.1` `2362e48a1` as `raw/03` reports them, and the discussion edits named in the header.

1. Name. Open. "Issuee usage registry" (#1095, #1550) vs "presentation registry" (#1613, #1618) vs "user presentation registry" (KERIcon). #1550 shows the primitive used for agent capability control, #1627 adds a transfer registry (§4a), and the ACDC spec's own `rd` gloss lists four purposes. Four uses, one of which is presentation, and the name of the whole is the name of the one. The reference implementation uses the narrow name and scopes the mechanism to the narrow use: the only registry an Issuee writes through the facade binds a `grant` SAID with state `"presented"` (§7.5, §7.7). #1740 uses the specification's general term, "blindable state registry", for the substrate both kinds share (§4b). Code names do not bind a specification, but they set the vocabulary most implementers meet first, which makes the case for a general name more urgent.
2. `rd`-in-`a` disambiguation. Open in the specifications, settled in keripy. #1613 floats EGF-declared disambiguation, restricting the attribute `rd` to presentation registries in `v1.1`, or forbidding the hidden-state use, and settles none. `raw/03` reports the relevant spec text byte-identical between `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`. The choice is permanent per ACDC and forecloses the other feature (§2.4). keripy's rule: an `a`-section `rd` with a top-level `rd` is a presentation registry, an `a`-section `rd` without one is refused as an unsupported hidden Issuer registry, and an `rd` in an `A` entry is neither (§2.4, `[code]`). That is #1613's default "for anchoring presentation exchanges" plus the forbid option, minus the presentation registry without an Issuer state registry that #1613 said forbidding would enable. A v1-conformant ACDC that uses `a.rd` as a hidden state registry is rejected by keripy's V2 IPEX verifier.
3. #1095's lack of comments does not mean the design is dead. Sam named presentation registries as work to be built on a call on 2026-09-01, wrote about them on #1627 on 2026-09-02, and keripy implements them (§0). A quiet discussion thread is not evidence of a dead idea, which is a caution about this corpus generally.
4. The `rip`-binds-to-Issuer wording. Open in the specification. Spec `:2019` says the `rd` value "cryptographically binds the Registry to the Issuer AID," which for a presentation registry means the Issuee. keripy reads "Issuer AID" as "whoever incepted this registry": its Issuer-equality check runs only for a top-level `rd`, and the presentation path checks that the incepter equals the `a`-section Issuee (§7.4). No specification text says so.
5. Latency, and the timing rule keripy chose. A presentation registry inserts a registry write between message creation and the verifier's acceptance, and the verifier's time-delay trick from #1095 adds more deliberately. §2.6 shows the write is additional to the sender's signature or KEL anchor rather than replacing either, since KRAM cannot see a registry factor. Nothing in `bible/09` or `raw/15` gives a window class that would accommodate a registry round-trip plus an observer fetch. keripy's rule is looser than "must pass KRAM": the registry check runs after KRAM and accepts a binding whose TEL `dt` is no later than the receiver's KRAM acceptance time, and the KEL event anchoring that binding may arrive later, through escrow (§2.2). The Issuee asserts the TEL `dt`; the acceptance time comes from the receiver's clock. The binding cannot predate the `grant` it names, which bounds it below only. A compromised signer could backdate `dt`, but then the TEL event is anchored in the real Issuee's KEL and is detectable, which is the point of the mechanism. So the looseness may be acceptable, but nobody has written that argument down.
6. The trusted-third-party concession is two concessions, not one. #1095 admits Verifier-specific protection "violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party" and justifies it by localized harm and aligned incentives. #1613 makes a second, distinct relaxation: the presentation registry is not a self-sufficient protection. In Sam's words it gives the Grantor "an Issuer-Verifier-protected mechanism to detect compromise of its signing keys" — it requires an Issuer that is a different party and does not collude, and a Verifier that enforces, since "The verifier then enforces the protection by not accepting presentations (grant messages) that are not so anchored…" — the sentence continues with the condition that the issued ACDC carry both `rd` and `i`, which narrows the rule without weakening the dependency. So the Issuee-side detectability story depends on two other parties, and the mechanism offered as the alternative to trusting a third party is itself two-party-dependent. Both concessions are acknowledged in the sources; the doctrine residual should name the second as well as the first.
7. Correlation resistance is conditional. It requires blinded registry state and, for unlinkability of the registry itself, bulk issuance with independent AIDs. A plain usage registry is "bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable."
8. #1555 is not in the manifest. #1613 opens by positioning itself against Multiply Endorsed (#1555), superseding "much of" it while explicitly preserving its edge-operator proposal, and #1627 returns to that proposal and argues against it (§4a). PRs #1693 and #1734 implement multiply endorsed `grant`s (§7.3). Without #1555 in the manifest, the corpus can compare that code only with #1613's restatement, and cannot say which of #1555's original requirements the code drops. A refresh may not retarget `refresh/sources.yaml`; this is raised for review, not applied.
9. Two incompatible bespoke origin ACDCs share a name. #1613 §Correlation Protected Registry requires the self-issued bespoke origin to use a blinded state registry for Issuer authentication, or the correlation resistance a presentation registry buys is given back at the origin node. #1627's SEDI recipe pattern specifies the opposite: no registry, no anchor, bare signatures, because it is one-time use. Both are Sam, three weeks apart, and both are coherent for their own purpose. Neither says the other exists. Any worked example combining a presentation registry with a #1627-style recipe origin is wrong in a way neither source warns about.
10. The refusal split is unaudited. `regeventing.vet` raises `MissingAnchorError` as retryable and the other named errors as permanent, and `_vetRegistry` hard-codes that division (`ipexing.py:2081-2096`). `raw/09-keripy-code.md` open question 6 routes the completeness question here. Two facts are known. `MisanchorError` is retryable when a required presentation source's KEL event is not yet local and permanent otherwise (`:2086-2090`), so the classification depends on context, not only on the error class. And `UnverifiedBlindError`, raised when an update after the disclosed state has no disclosure, is permanent; for an Issuer registry, which has no cutoff, that is the edge case #1740's `/ipex/unblind` exists for (item 17). The completeness audit `raw/09` asked for is not done.
11. The code's requirement is unconditional apart from issuance, and #1613's is not. #1613 makes the anchoring requirement apply "at the time of presentation by the Issuee of that ACDC when the Issuee is the Grantor of that IPEX" (§Registry Anchored Granted ACDCs), and repeats the condition at §New Anchored Exchange `ax` Field. keripy applies it to every declaration in the granted DAG whoever sends the `grant`, exempting only the origin's own declaration when the `grant` is an issuance (§7.2). The two agree when the holder presents its own credential. They differ for the case #1613 §Anchoring Requirement Complication describes, where "an ACDC is presented as evidence, but the Issuee AID is unrelated to the Discloser's AID", and for an Issuer re-presenting a credential it issued, which keripy treats as a presentation (§7.2). Under keripy's rule, nobody can present such an ACDC unless its Issuee writes this `grant`'s SAID into its registry. The choice was made deliberately: a Grantor-scoped version, commented "Merely appearing in an ACDC does not assign that role." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph`, L1900 @`8a7f33e0c`), was written and removed inside PR #1734 (`3c11a4215`). It is the strongest reading of "an imposter … can't avoid the requirement", but it makes a presentation-registry ACDC unusable as third-party evidence, and neither #1613 nor the code comments say whether that was weighed. JUDGMENT for Daniel: whether to raise this with the implementer. It is a scoping question, not a bug report.
12. Single versus double authentication for a non-sender Issuee. #1613 says every Grantor must endorse the `grant` and that a registry-anchored `grant` is "always double-authenticated". keripy accepts a proxy-sent `grant` in which the Issuee's only participation is the registry binding (§2.6), a choice made deliberately inside PR #1693. On its face it is defensible, because a KEL-anchored binding to the exact `grant` SAID is a stronger factor than a signature. But the design document and the code disagree about what a Grantor is, and a reader of #1613 will expect a check the code does not make. One downstream effect: the endorser index does not list registry-only Issuees, so business logic that finds a holder's presentations through `eidx` will miss them. JUDGMENT for Daniel: whether this goes upstream as a request to update #1613's text to match the code, or the reverse.
13. keripy refuses `grant`s for reasons no specification states. At `94f5423a3` the V2 IPEX verifier rejects three kinds of `grant` on presentation-registry grounds: one whose ACDC declares an Issuee registry that does not bind it; one carrying an ACDC with an `a`-section `rd` and no top-level `rd`, which is v1-legal as a hidden state registry; and one carrying a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` is compact. All three are `[code]` (§7.2), and none is `[spec]` or `[spec-next]` (§6). The third follows from the first, because a verifier cannot tell an omitted requirement from a hidden one. A peer implementation built from either spec line will produce `grant`s keripy rejects, with no normative text to explain why. The chapter should say so plainly.
14. Aggregate entries and presentation registries. Declaration discovery no longer reads `A` (§2.4), so a registry-backed ACDC's aggregate can be selectively disclosed without failing the presentation check: a compact or partial `A` yields no declaration rather than a refusal (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:8022-8023`). The SEDI Age credential, with a top-level `rd` and an `A`, is therefore not blocked by declaration discovery; whether it can be presented through keripy IPEX is still untested, since no IPEX test at `94f5423a3` presents it (flagged for `raw/11`). The rule has a converse that nothing documents: an Issuer that places an `i`/`rd` pair in an aggregate entry, expecting a presentation requirement, gets none, silently. #1613 speaks only of the attribute section, so keripy matches the design; the hazard is for a schema author who reads the spec's permission that a nested `rd` in the Attributed or Aggregate section "MAY be used for some other registry" (§6) as license to put a presentation registry in `A`.
15. #1740's in-band issuance order does not fit keripy's `grant` verification. #1740 has the Issuer send `grant`, run the DHKE, and only then write the `bup` that records issuance (§4b). keripy at `94f5423a3` authenticates a registry-backed node by that `bup`'s disclosure, carried on the `grant`: "A grant with rd must carry exactly one node-local registry proof group." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:5721`), and `_vetRegistry` returns `None` without one ("Require disclosed proofs and a locally available TEL chain.", `ipexing.py:2013`). So an issuance `grant` sent before its `bup` exists is refused, not escrowed, by a keripy V2 recipient. #1740's out-of-band alternative, a salt exchanged before the IPEX, fits keripy as it stands. Either #1740's issuance order needs a `grant` that carries no registry proof for the node being issued, or keripy's issuance path needs a way to defer that proof until after `admit` is prompted; neither source says which. JUDGMENT for Daniel: worth raising on #1740 itself, since it is an ordering consequence the draft does not discuss.
16. Nothing in keripy enforces #1740's `admit` rule. #1740 says the Issuee, and the Disclosee in a presentation, MUST NOT send `admit` until the registry state is verified (§4b). keripy authenticates the state proof on `grant` and records the state in `exrs` for an application to judge, and nothing on the protocol path reads `exrs` back (§7.9); the `admit` builder and handler do not consult it. The two designs are compatible — #1740's rule is a sender obligation and keripy leaves judgment to the application — but the obligation currently has no implementation and no hook. Whether `admit` should carry or reference what was verified is not addressed in #1740.
17. #1740's presentation edge case is a refusal in keripy. When a verifier holds a later `bup` in an Issuer registry than the presenter disclosed, #1740 proposes `/ipex/unblind` to ask for the missing disclosures. keripy at `94f5423a3` vets an Issuer registry against the verifier's current head with no cutoff, requires exactly one disclosure for every update back to the latest non-vacuous one (`regeventing.py:651-653`), and treats a missing one as the permanent `UnverifiedBlindError` (`ipexing.py:2093-2096`). So the `grant` is refused rather than held for an auxiliary exchange. This follows from reading the code; no test at this pin constructs it for an Issuer registry, though the presentation-registry analogue is tested (`test_ipexing.py:8093-8104`). A presentation registry does not meet the edge case in keripy, because its history is cut off at the KRAM acceptance time and its controller is the Issuee who writes it.
18. Per-registry salts and the bulk-issuance salt. #1740 assumes one salt per registry, exchanged by one DH public key per `rd`, and allows a hierarchy from one salt without specifying its path. `raw/21` R-044 records Sam's ruling that a bulk set's registry blinding factors can derive from the bulk salt by index, and that the derivation path is unsettled between `k.s`, `k.` and `k|`. For a bulk set of N registries, #1740 as drafted exchanges N public keys unless the hierarchy is used, and the hierarchy's path is the same open question. `raw/21` and `raw/03` own the derivation; this item records that #1740 depends on its answer.
