## 1. #1095 — the origin document and its security argument

The frame is the tripartite Issuer–Holder–Verifier model, with the Issuee as Holder ("This imbues the ACDC with what others call strong Holder binding"). The Verifier must verify two things, and #1095's whole contribution is that **these two are not symmetric**:

> "1) That Issuance came from the Issuer (Issuer impersonation fraud) 2) The Presenter of the ACDC at the time of presentation is indeed the Issuee. (Issuee impersonation fraud)"

**Issuer-side: rampant, so detectability is mandatory.**

> "the worst form of fraud with the most widespread potential of harm would be rampant impersonation fraud that could result when the Issuer's signing keys are compromised. This could annihilate trust in the ecosystem… The ROI to an attacker for compromising the Issuer keys could be huge, especially if that Issuer is a root-of-trust for Identity assurance for an ecosystem."

The mitigation is the existing TEL-anchored-in-KEL structure, and Sam states the resulting property in strong terms: "an impersonator cannot impersonate the Issuer of an ACDC merely by compromising the signing keys of the Issuer. The impersonator MUST anchor the ACDC in a TEL that is anchored in the Issuer's KEL. This anchor enables the Issuer to detect any fraudulent issuances without requiring the cooperation of any other party." Plus a claim worth flagging as a claim: "AFAIK, only KERI/ACDC provides such protection in a decentralized identity system."

The reasoning is explicitly the detect-and-recover posture: "Issuer key compromise has both high susceptibility and non-zero vulnerability, therefore protection comes from recoverability. Detect and recover."

**Issuee-side: localized, so protection is optional and elective.** Three reasons given:

1. **Low ROI.** "Because the fraud is localized to a single Issuee, the ROI to the attacker for compromising the Issuee's keys is low." Plus a note that prophylactic rotation is available and effective, "because the verifier always checks the presentation as being signed by the latest keys of the Issuee."
2. **Use-case-specific mitigations are acceptable here.** A Verifier could require a key rotation as part of the presentation. Sam concedes the cost openly: "This violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party, but for localized harm, the party most likely to be harmed is the Verifier… which provides incentives for them to protect themselves." He also names the underlying capability as distinctive: the Issuee's ability "to rotate signing keys without reissuing the ACDC is a unique super-power of KERI/ACDC as a decentralized identity system."
3. **The Issuee can add its own detectability** — which is the usage registry.

### The mechanism as first stated

> "The ACDC must include a requirement that the ACDC is only verifiable when the presentation of the ACDC by the Issuee is anchored as the latest state in an Issuee-controlled *usage registry*. The verifier won't accept the credential if it's also not anchored in the Issuee's (in addition to the Issuer's) registry. The anchor enables the Issuee to detect fraudulent presentations of its credentials. The Issuee can then do a rotation recovery of its keys and also dispute the fraudulent presentations."

Four properties that #1613 does not restate and that the bible has nowhere:

- **One registry, many ACDCs, no leak.** "A *usage registry* can be employed to track the usage of one or more ACDCs by the Issuee… This means that the registry does not leak specific usage of a given ACDC to third parties." The blindable state registry is what makes many-to-one safe.
- **Linkability caveat, with its escape.** "A given usage registry is bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable. A bulk-issued ACDC with independent AIDs can remove this linkage." So the correlation story is *conditional on* independent-registry bulk issuance, which ties this directly to that workstream.
- **The verifier's time-delay trick.** "by adding a time delay from presentation anchor to acceptance, the verifier can assume that a compromised Issuee would do a recovery, invalidating the fraudulent presentation." This converts detection into prevention at the cost of latency, and it appears nowhere else in the corpus.
- **The Issuee needs its own Registrar.** "the Issuee must have its own Registrar to manage its usage registries." That is real infrastructure, and it is why the last sentence of the section reads: "This is an optional protection measure. Many Issuees will feel that their key management is sufficient… and therefore would not want the extra protection and friction of a usage Registrar."

**Incentive design.** The Verifier is given a reason to enforce: "The verifier has a vested interest in not accepting unanchored presentations as the presentation of an Issuee unanchored ACDC that declares it must be Issuee usage anchored, must be considered fraudulent."

**Provisioning, and the tentativeness of the field choice.** "An Issuee could request at the time of Issuance that their ACDC be backed by an Issuee usage registry. In response, the Issuer issues a variant of the ACDC that designates that it is to be Issuee presentation protected. **That designation needs to be defined. This could be an `rd` field in the attribute section.**" As of #1095 the field is a suggestion, not a design.

**Diagrams (not vendored here):** `PresentationUsageRegistrarObserver` and `PresentationUsageRegistryTEL`, attached to the discussion body.

## 1a. #1098 — the registry as a reason to refuse unlinkable ZKPs

*Mined 2026-10-03. Section inserted rather than renumbered, for the same reason as §4a. `raw/11` owns #1098's age-proof mechanism; this section takes only what bears on registries and detectability.*

[#1098](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1098) (`[P]`) argues for a static sparse-Merkle-tree age predicate over a ZKP predicate proof, and the deciding argument is detectability, not cost. The Issuer-side half is the argument §1 already records from #1095, restated as a constraint on cryptographic choices: with unlinkable ZKPs "the Issuer of the credential is unable, by virtue of that unlinkability, to detect that a credential has been fraudulently issued"; "Detection requires that the Issuer collude with or cooperate with the verifier and/or holder, which cooperation breaks unlinkability"; so "the use of unlinkable ZKPs is therefore precluded to preserve the detectability of an Issuer signing infrastructure compromise…" (the sentence ends "as one of the inviolable properties of KERI/ACDCs").

**The Issuee-side half is what this note needs.** "Likewise, the key compromise of an Issuee (Holder) can be made detectable using a presentation registry. Which would also be defeated by an unlinkable ZKP." The consequence drawn is specific: "a given Issuee (Holder) wallet could be impersonated and used to present fraudulent proofs of age." The conclusion counts both halves as what the SMT approach buys — "strong security (detectability of key compromise for both the Issuer and the Issuee)" — and the usage section names the presentation registry's role by function: "anti-fraud protection derived from the strong detectability properties of key compromise with anchored KERI/ACDCs for Issuers and anchored presentations for Issuees".

Three things follow, all `[P]`.

**The presentation registry is used as a premise in a design decision, not only proposed.** #1095 presents Issuee-side protection as "an optional protection measure" (§1). #1098 does not contradict that — it says detectability "can be made" available, not that it must be used — but it treats the *possibility* of a presentation registry as something a credential format must not foreclose. A cryptographic choice that would make an Issuee registry unenforceable is ruled out on that ground alongside the Issuer-side ground. That is a stronger status for the idea than "optional protection measure": optional to use, but mandatory to keep possible.

**It names the property the registry protects as one KERI treats as non-negotiable.** "Inviolable" is attached in #1098 to Issuer-side detectability; the Issuee-side sentence follows with "Likewise". So the Issuee registry is offered as the presentation-time analogue of the property KERI ranks highest, which is a sharper statement of purpose than #1613's two benefits (§2.3) and agrees with KERIcon's "not just the issuer" (§5).

**Provenance caution.** #1098 was created 2025-10-27, two weeks after #1095 coined "usage registry", and was last edited 2026-09-26. The fetched body says "presentation registry". Without the edit history this note cannot say whether that sentence was in the October 2025 text or was added in September 2026, so it is evidence of Sam's position at 2026-09-26, not of when he first held it. §2.7 records the same pattern in #1613: these bodies change silently.

## 2. #1613 — the developed design

### 2.0 What the discussion says about its own standing, and the hierarchy the registry sits in

Two framing facts were not mined in the first pass and both bear on how much weight §2 carries.

**#1613 declares itself a partial supersession.** Its opening line: "this discussion supersedes much of the “Multiply Endorsed” discussion (see #1555).    It does not supersede the proposal for a new edge operator." Both halves matter. The supersession explains why #1555 is quiet; the carve-out means the edge-operator proposal in #1555 is still live and is a separate design decision from anything here. **#1555 is not in this bible's source manifest** (`refresh/sources.yaml`), so the corpus is currently reading a discussion that positions itself against a document the corpus does not track. That is a manifest question, not something a refresh may fix unattended — it is raised in §8 item 8. #1627 states the relationship from the other side and dates it: "#1555 predates #1613 which largely superseded #1555" (#1627 body, snapshot 2026-09-15).

**A presentation registry is the top of a three-rung preference order, not one option among equals.** #1613 §Authentication Factor Types names the three factor types as registry anchor, KEL anchor and signature, and then imposes a strict precedence: "the highest-preference factor is used, and the others are ignored." So a presentation registry, where one is required, is not merely *sufficient* to authenticate a `grant` — it *displaces* the alternatives. This is the discussion's own version of the framing #1618 restates symmetrically in §3, stated by Sam first and with an ordering #1618 does not give. Note the register: the preference is argued from perpetual verifiability, fraud detectability and lifecycle state, and #1613 is explicit that "a bare signature does not" provide the first two.

### 2.1 The trigger is a three-part test

> "The issuer of a given ACDC can signal to a verifier that the `grant` message in an IPEX MUST be anchored in a presentation registry controlled by the Issuee of that ACDC at the time of presentation… That signal is provided when both a non-empty `rd` field and a valid AID as the Issuee in the issuee `i` field are provided at the top level of the ACDCs attribute `a` section (not the top level of the ACDC itself) **and the `rd` field at the top level of the ACDC is not empty**."

All three conditions. If there is no Issuee `i` in `a`, an `rd` in `a` imposes no anchoring requirement. If the ACDC's *top-level* `rd` is empty or missing, then the `rd` in `a` "refers to an ACDC state registry, not an IPEX presentation registry." The top-level `rd` is doing disambiguation work, which is easy to miss.

### 2.2 What the registry must contain

> "the value of the registry `rd` field in the attribute `a` section MUST be the SAID of the `rip` event of the associated presentation Registry. The Issuee of the associated ACDC, who is also a Grantor of that IPEX, MUST control this registry. The value of the ACDC SAID field in the blinded attribute block of the latest non-vacuous event in that presentation Registry must be the SAID of the `grant` message."

Note the field reuse: the slot that normally holds an ACDC SAID holds a `grant` SAID instead.

*Checked against code 2026-10-03, at `80f77b73b`.* Three of the four requirements in that quotation are now `[K]`, and one is not. The `rd` value must be the registry's `rip` SAID: `_vetRegistry` raises `MisregistryError` when the verified chain's `regid` differs from the declared one. The Issuee must control the registry: `vetBinds` raises `MisbindingError` when `record.issuer != controller` (`regeventing.py:714`), and the presentation path passes the `a`-section `i` as the controller. The latest non-vacuous event must bind the `grant` SAID: `vetBinds` walks back over vacuous updates and requires the first non-vacuous one to have `td == target`, where the target is the `grant` SAID (§7.2). The clause **"who is also a Grantor of that IPEX" is not enforced**: since `b7c170f93` the Issuee need not sign or anchor the `grant` at all, and the registry alone authenticates it (§2.6, §7.3).

**KRAM couples to this.** "in order to pass KRAM, a `grant` message anchor must happen in a timely fashion relative to the IPEX exchange. The `grant` message… must be created, then anchored, and then pass the receiver's KRAM." So a presentation registry inserts a registry write into the critical path of a freshness window — see `bible/09-kram-and-request-authentication.md`.

*Qualified 2026-10-03 by the code.* keripy does not make the registry anchor pass KRAM; it makes the registry check depend on KRAM having already passed. KRAM now records a receiver timestamp, `rdt`, documented as "receiver datetime when KRAM accepted the complete message" (`src/keri/recording.py:505`), and the presentation check refuses without it: "A presentation requirement needs a completed KRAM acceptance." (`ipexing.py:1524`). The registry history is then cut off at that time — `vetBinds` drops updates whose `dt` is later and rejects a registry whose `rip` is later (`regeventing.py:655`: "Presentation registries use the grant's KRAM" acceptance time). Two consequences the design text does not state. The timeliness test compares the TEL event's own `dt`, which the Issuee asserts, against the receiver's clock; the KEL event anchoring that TEL event may arrive afterwards, because a `grant` waiting on it escrows and is later accepted (`tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2745`: "Vic has the TEL update but not the KEL event that authenticates it."). And since the bound target is this `grant`'s SAID, which cannot exist before the `grant` is built, the binding event is necessarily created after the `grant` body is fixed — a lower bound by construction and an upper bound by the asserted `dt`, both `[K]`. The tests build a draft `grant`, bind its SAID in the registry, then rebuild the identical `grant` with the proofs attached (`tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2496`: "Fix the Grant before binding it in Core's presentation registry."). See §8 item 5.

### 2.3 Two benefits, only one of which is fraud protection

**Fraud protection, and its precondition.** "an imposter that merely compromises the Grantor's signing infrastructure can't avoid the requirement without also compromising the Issuer." The Issuer must be a *different party*, and Sam states the self-issued hole explicitly: "For ACDCs that the Grantor self-issues, this is only a vulnerability if the Issuance authentication factor is merely an unanchored signature. An imposter who controls the Grantor's signing infrastructure can create ACDCs whose authentication factor is an unanchored signature."

Multi-ACDC DAGs multiply the work: "When multiple ACDCs in the granted DAG each have a different presentation registry ID field value… the verifier must verify all of the anchors." And a precise limit on what one anchor buys: because the `grant`'s `o` field names the DAG's origin, "anchoring the exchange in one presentation registry provides a perpetually verifiable proof of the presentation of all ACDCs in that DAG, **but not necessarily a fresh proof of control for all Issuee AIDs in that DAG**. Multiple anchors in different registries provide multiple vectors of detectability and multiple fresh proofs-of-control; the perpetual verifiability is redundant."

*Checked against code 2026-10-03, at `80f77b73b`.* The fraud-protection half is `[K]` on the verifier side, including the property that makes it work: the Grantor cannot opt out. The requirement is read from issuance metadata on every `grant`, independent of `ax` — the test says "Presentation anchoring is mandatory from issuance metadata even" when a later grant omits `ax` (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:6232`) — and a presenter cannot hide the declaration by compacting the attribute section, because a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` or `A` is undisclosed is refused outright, because a verifier cannot distinguish an "omitted requirement from an undisclosed one using only its digest." (`ipexing.py:1415`; the test of the same point is at `test_ipexing.py:6246`). The multi-ACDC rule is `[K]` in its strict form: `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` collects every declaration across the walked DAG and fails the whole `grant` if any one does not bind it ("Every declared node-local presentation registry must bind the grant.", `test_ipexing.py:6355`; "Reject a DAG when one rd-bearing node omits presentation evidence.", `tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:2171`). Because each registry must bind *this* `grant`'s SAID, each one is also a fresh proof of control for its Issuee, which is the "multiple fresh proofs-of-control" case. The case of one anchor covering the DAG while other Issuees go unproven is not selectable: there is no way to declare a registry and have it ignored. Nothing checks that Issuer and Issuee differ, so the self-issued hole Sam concedes is present in code exactly as conceded.

**Correlation resistance, which works even when fraud protection cannot.** If a Grantor directly anchors a `grant` SAID in its public KEL and the Grantee does the same, "a third party… would be able to detect the same grant SAID in both the Grantor's KEL and the Grantee's KEL, thereby correlating the IPEX." Anchoring in a blinded presentation registry instead means "neither the grant SAID nor any other artifacts of the granted DAG appear in the KEL of the Grantor," so "a Grantee could anchor every message in the IPEX without providing a point of correlation between the KELs of the Grantor and Grantee."

**The self-issued bespoke pattern.** A Grantor can get correlation resistance with no cooperating Issuer by self-issuing a bespoke origin ACDC carrying `i` and `rd` in its `a` section — "This means the Grantor is both Issuer and Issuee of that bespoke ACDC." With a hard constraint attached: "the bespoke ACDC itself must use a blinded state Registry for its Issuer authentication so that the SAID of its ACDC is not correlatable. If it uses a direct KEL anchor, then that anchor itself would be correlatable. If it used a bare attached signature… then the ACDC itself would not be perpetually verifiable despite using a perpetually verifiable presentation registry."

### 2.4 The `rd`-in-`a` ambiguity, and Sam's own doubt

The v1 spec also allows `rd` in `a` as a *hidden ACDC state registry*. #1613 proposes EGF-declared disambiguation with "The default, if not otherwise specified, should be for anchoring presentation exchanges," floats "We may want to limit the use case to only presentation registries in the v1.1 spec," and then argues the hiding case down: its only benefit arises "when the ACDC in compact form is made public, independent of any given presentation, and the fact of it having a registry must remain undisclosed. It is not clear that this is a worthy use case… Given the potentially dubious value, we might want to forbid using the `rd` field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry." The section ends genuinely unresolved: "Not sure if either is a worthy use case."

**The two uses are mutually exclusive per ACDC, which the first pass did not record.** The same section states it flatly: "a presentation registry may not be used with an ACDC that uses the `rd` field in the attribute `a` section as an Issuer-controlled ACDC state." One `rd` slot, one meaning, chosen at issuance and not recoverable later. That raises the stakes on the disambiguation question in §8 item 2 considerably — this is not a labelling nicety but a permanent either/or imposed on every targeted ACDC that wants either feature. It also supplies the argument #1613 uses to talk itself out of the hiding case: hiding "is not useful with full independent Registry bulk issuance since each presentation could use a different member of the bulk issued set", so the correlation problem hiding solves is already solved better elsewhere, and the cost of keeping it is forfeiting presentation registries for that ACDC.

**And v1.1 did not touch any of this.** `raw/03` reports §Registry SAID Field and the reserved-fields row byte-identical between `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`, with "usage registry" occurring exactly once at either pin. Spot-checked here and confirmed. So none of the three resolutions #1613 floats has been taken up in the branch most likely to carry it, and §8 item 2 has its negative answer for `v1.1` rather than its resolution.

**keripy has taken one of them, 2026-10-03.** At `80f77b73b` the code implements #1613's three-part test as the disambiguation rule and **refuses the hiding case** rather than supporting it. With a non-empty top-level `rd`, an `rd` and `i` pair in `a` — or, beyond what #1613 says, in a disclosed `A` aggregate entry — is a presentation-registry declaration (`ipexing.py:1467-1488`). Without a top-level `rd`, an `rd` in `a` or `A` is classed as "hidden issuer registry, not a presentation registry." (`ipexing.py:1419`) and the ACDC fails verification; the Issuer-authentication code is explicit that this form "must not fall through to another factor" such as a signature (`ipexing.py:1155`; test `test_ipex_v2_rejects_hidden_issuer_registry_signature_fallback`, `tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:3558`: "A Grant cannot replace an unsupported hidden issuer registry with a signature."). That is `[K]` for the closest of #1613's options — "forbid using the rd field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry" — with one difference: #1613 floated that as a way to "enable a presentation registry for an ACDC without an Issuer state registry", and keripy does not enable that; a presentation registry is recognized only when a top-level `rd` is present. It is an implementation choice, not a spec resolution, and §8 item 2 is updated accordingly.

### 2.5 Interaction with `ax`

The presentation-registry requirement is **independent of and stronger than** `ax`: "Notwithstanding the presence or absence of the `ax` field, when an ACDC in a granted DAG meets the requirements for a Issuer signaled presentation anchor registry… then that `grant` message MUST be anchored in that registry for the `grant` to be valid. Otherwise, the presumption is that the grant is fraudulent." With a liability claim attached: "Any verifier (Disclosee) accepting such a `grant` is presumptively in violation of any contractual or regulatory protection afforded to the real Grantor by colluding with a fraudulent grantor."

And a scoping judgment about which registry may be used: "There is no security advantage to using a presentation registry specified by the Grantor, as the main purpose of a presentation registry is to allow the Grantor to detect a compromise of its signing key infrastructure… Thus, the main use case for impersonation fraud detection of the Grantor requires a pre-specified presentation registry **by an Issuer different from the Grantor**, which must be prespecified in one of the ACDCs not in the `grant` message." On tooling: "we should not add special tooling for this special case; instead, we should use the existing tooling for a bulk-issued ACDC to handle it."

**The `ax` requirement has an alternative-satisfaction rule the first pass missed, and it is what landed in code.** #1613 §Origin AID Anchoring proposes that when `ax` is truthy, "either the sender must anchor the grant or the Issuee or Issuer of the origin ACDC of the grant must anchor the grant" — three acceptable anchorers, applied in that order of priority, so that a sender acting as a proxy negotiator for an undisclosed AID can still satisfy the requirement. The rule exists to let the important Grantor stay out of the exchange until the `grant`. It is deliberately partial: "we choose not to define every possible combination; we define only the most important and leave the rest to the EGF or future development." ~~**Only the first of those three is implemented** at `b8f60166b` — see §7 — and the distinction matters, because the sender-anchors case is the one that needs no knowledge of the DAG at all.~~ **Superseded 2026-10-03: all three are implemented at `80f77b73b`, and a presentation-registry anchor counts as one of them.** Under a truthy `ax`, `IpexHandler.verify` collects the AIDs that anchored the `grant` — the sender via its own source-seal couple, any non-sender via an explicit source-seal triple, and every Issuee authenticated by a presentation registry ("Presentation registries authenticate their controlling Issuees.", `ipexing.py:626`) — and accepts if any of them is one of three: "Restrict default qualification to sender, Issuee, or issuer." (`ipexing.py:647`, over `(serder.pre, origin.iseaid, origin.israid)` at `:648`). Two differences from #1613's wording, both `[K]`. The code checks membership in a set, so #1613's "order of priority" has no operational effect — any one suffices, which is what #1613's next sentence says anyway. And the comment states the boundary as policy: "Default IPEX policy deliberately stops at these three AIDs;" with credential-specific policy free to narrow it (`ipexing.py:645-646`), which is #1613's "leave the rest to the EGF". Separately, the independence clause at the head of this subsection is `[K]`: the presentation-registry check runs on every `grant` whether or not `ax` is present (§2.3), and #1613's rejection of a Grantor-specified registry has a `[K]` counterpart in that the code recognizes only registries declared in a DAG node; nothing on the `grant` itself can name one.

### 2.6 Double authentication, and why KRAM cannot see a presentation registry

This is the sharpest new material in the 2026-09-02 edit, and it changes how §2.2's KRAM coupling should be read.

A presentation registry anchor does **not** substitute for the ordinary signature or KEL anchor on a `grant`. #1613 §ACDC Grantor Authentication Factor: "KRAM does not recognize or process a presentation registry-based authentication factor for the grant message," and therefore "a presentation registry-anchored grant message is always double-authenticated." The requirement is stated as an obligation, not an observation: "When the Grantor as Issuee for the required presentation registry is also the grant sender, then the Grantor MUST be doubly authenticated; otherwise, the grant message will not pass KRAM." Sam gives the layering reason plainly — "the processing of presentation registry validation happens higher up in the stack than grant message authentication" — and calls the resulting duplication "unavoidable."

Three consequences worth carrying. The `grant` therefore carries an **ephemeral** factor and a **perpetually verifiable** one simultaneously, which is exactly the combination §2.0's preference order would otherwise suppress — the precedence rule that says a higher factor makes lower ones "ignored" does not reach across the KRAM boundary, because KRAM is below it. A verifier that treats registry anchoring as *replacing* the signature check will reject valid grants or accept invalid ones depending on which way it errs. And the latency argument in §8 item 5 is worse than it looked: the registry write does not replace a step, it is added to one.

This is also the paragraph where the absence of any presentation-registry handling in `src/keri/core/kraming.py` stops being a gap and becomes conformance. #1613 says KRAM must not process it. §7's negative is about `ipexing.py`, where the check would belong, not about KRAM.

**What the code does, 2026-10-03 — confirmed for the sender, departed from for anyone else.** Both halves are `[K]` at `80f77b73b`.

When the Issuee whose registry is declared is also the `grant` sender — every SEDI test, where the holder sends — double authentication holds by construction: KRAM authenticates the sender's signature on the `grant` as it does for any exchange, and the registry check runs above it in `IpexHandler.verify`. The layering is as #1613 describes. `kraming.py` still contains no presentation-registry logic; its only new involvement is recording the acceptance time the registry check consumes (§2.2).

When the Issuee is **not** the sender, keripy no longer requires the Issuee to endorse the `grant`. The test of the proxy case is explicit: "Sender authentication plus the Issuee's presentation TEL is" sufficient, and "the Issuee does not need a redundant EXN endorsement." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:6205-6206`). The grant is accepted with only the proxy's signature attached, and the test asserts that no Issuee signature was stored. #1613 §ACDC Grantor Authentication Factor says the opposite for this case: "To be accepted by KRAM, the grant message must be endorsed by each Grantor", hence "a presentation registry-anchored grant message is always double-authenticated." The code did require it for four days. Commit `790797bcd` (2026-09-21) carried "A registry supplements, but never replaces, a Grant endorsement." (`ipexing.py:1543` at `4c53f56a1`), and `b7c170f93` "remove grantor requisite" (2026-09-25) deleted that check together with the Grantor set it consulted. Both commits are inside PR #1693; the merge `7f5900748` has `b7c170f93` as its second parent. So **no first-parent commit on `main` ever enforced the requisite** (`790797bcd` is reachable from `main` only through the merge's second parent). It was tried and withdrawn before landing, and the shipped behavior is deliberate rather than an omission.

The two can be reconciled on #1613's own definitions, but only by a step #1613 does not take. In #1613 the Grantors are "the set of endorser AIDs to the grant message", and the registry "MUST be anchored … when the Issuee is the Grantor of that IPEX" (§Registry Anchored Granted ACDCs). In the code, an Issuee who never endorses is still authenticated, through the registry alone: it enters the `tethered` set and can satisfy `ax` (§2.5). Effectively a presentation-registry anchor makes the Issuee a Grantor without an endorsement. That is coherent — the anchor is a KEL-anchored commitment to the exact `grant` SAID, which is at least as strong as a signature — but it is a design change, and #1613 does not record it. One side effect follows from reading the code, though no test asserts it: the new endorser index `eidx` (`src/keri/db/basing.py:591`) is filled from accepted signature groups and source seals only (`src/keri/peer/exchanging.py:366-371`), so an Issuee authenticated by registry alone is not indexed against the `grant`. The multi-registry test shows the same pattern with two Issuees: "The sender factor and both Issuee TEL factors accept the Grant." (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:6488`), and neither Issuee signs. See §8 item 12.

## 4a. #1627 — where the presentation-architecture argument touches registries

*Mined 2026-09-15. Section inserted rather than renumbered, because `raw/16 §6` and `§8` are cited from `refresh/prompts/standards.md`, `raw/03` and `keri-doctrine.md`.*

#1627 surveys four ways to shape a credential presentation — Portable Datalake, Credential Soup, Verifiable DAG, DAG Soup — and argues for the ACDC DAG. `bible/08` already tracks its list form for `dp`, `o` and `ax`. What chapter 10 needs is the argument, and specifically where it bears on registries. Note throughout that this is `[P]`: a design discussion, and one whose body never uses the words "presentation registry."

**The bespoke origin ACDC is specified here in terms #1613 says forfeit correlation resistance.** #1627's central proposal for the SEDI case is a lightweight bespoke ACDC as the DAG's origin — a "recipe" that converts a soup into a DAG — and it is explicit that the recipe is cheap: "This origin does not need to be anchored or use a registry, so it's simple." It "merely needs to be signed and have attached signatures because it's a one-time use ACDC." **That is precisely the construction #1613 §Correlation Protected Registry rules out.** #1613 requires that the self-issued bespoke ACDC "must use a blinded state Registry for its Issuer authentication so that the SAID of its ACDC is not correlatable," and says that a bare attached signature leaves the ACDC not perpetually verifiable "despite using a perpetually verifiable presentation registry." Both are Sam, three weeks apart, and they are not in contradiction — they are two different bespoke ACDCs for two different jobs — but nothing in either document says so, and the shared name invites the wrong one. A reader who takes #1627's recipe pattern and adds a presentation registry to it gets a correlatable presentation and a `grant` whose perpetual verifiability the origin ACDC cannot carry. This is a new open question; see §8 item 9.

**#1627 independently names the limit §2.3 states as a hedge.** #1613 says anchoring in one presentation registry proves the presentation of the whole DAG "but not necessarily a fresh proof of control for all Issuee AIDs in that DAG." #1627 reaches the same wall from the multiple-endorsement side and states the general form: "the mere presence of an AID in a DAG is not a sufficiently precise signal of required fresh endorsement." So the gap is not an artifact of registries; it is a property of using DAG membership as a signal at all, and it is unresolved in both documents. #1627 names the missing piece — "multiply-endorsed awareness" — and says "more tooling is needed."

**A fourth registry kind appears, which further undercuts the name.** Discussing reissuance cascades in an ontology DAG, #1627 proposes to "use a transfer registry where a state change is not to revoke an ACDC but to provide a forward reference" to a replacement ACDC's registry. With #1550's capability-control use, that makes at least four jobs for one primitive — issuance/revocation state, usage, agent capability, transfer — and strengthens §8 item 1's argument that the general name should not be "presentation registry." The `rd` gloss in the ACDC spec (§6) already lists transfer among the purposes, so this one is `[N]` as a field purpose even though `[P]` as a reissuance strategy.

**The comment stream, which is where the registries actually are.** Four of #1627's five comments are dhh1128's and Sam's, 2026-08-25 to 2026-09-02. ## 6. What is normative in the ACDC specification

`spec/spec-body.md` @`f0bd097de` (2026-08-28; the same commit as the `f0bd097` short form used in the first pass). **"Presentation registry" does not appear at all, and "usage registry" appears exactly once, in a gloss.** What does:

- **The nested-`rd` slot exists and its purpose list already includes usage.** Top-level `rd` is glossed "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, or retraction registry for ACDC" (`:23`); the *not-at-top-level* entry reads "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, retraction, **or usage** registry for ACDC when not at top-level" (`:48`). That single word is the only trace of this design in any specification.
- **Nested `rd` is explicitly open-ended.** "When the registry SAID, `rd` field is used at the top level for the Issuer's registry, a registry SAID, `rd` field that appears nested in the Attributed, `a`, or Aggregate, `A`, section MAY be used for some other registry, such as an application-specific or Issuer-specific registry" (`:85`). Note "Issuer-specific" — the spec does not contemplate an *Issuee*-controlled registry.
- **The motivating rationale in the spec is bulk issuance and graduated disclosure**, not fraud detection: nesting "may better facilitate contractually protected disclosure of the bulk-issued registry" (`:326`).
- **`rd` binds to a `rip` event.** "The Registry SAID, `rd` field value MUST be the value of the SAID, `d` field of the Registry Inception, `rip` event. Because the Issuer `i` field appears in the `rip` event, the Registry SAID, `rd` field value cryptographically binds the Registry to the Issuer AID" (`:2019`). **This is a live tension**: the `rip` binds the registry to *its own* incepting AID. For a presentation registry that AID is the Issuee, so the sentence's use of "Issuer" is about the registry's issuer, not the ACDC's — worth stating carefully in any spec text.
- **Blindable state registries are normative** and there is a worked "Registry-Dependent Issuance Lifecycle" walking `rip` → issuance → state → verification → revocation. The spec calls a `rip` "a vacuous placeholder that reveals nothing about what will later be issued" — the origin of #1613's "latest non-vacuous event" phrasing. (That lifecycle section and §Graduated Disclosure are in **`main`**, tier `[N]`, not `v1.1`-only; `raw/03` re-anchored the "vacuous placeholder" quote at `f0bd097de`.)

**Re-verified 2026-09-15 at `f0bd097de`.** All five quotes above are present at that pin, which is the same commit as the `f0bd097` this section was written against, so nothing in §6 has moved. `raw/03` reports the same material byte-identical in `v1.1` at `2362e48a1`, with **no Issuee-controlled registry anywhere in that branch** — zero hits for "Issuee-controlled", "Issuee controlled" or "Issuee's registry". Spot-checked here: "presentation registry" has zero occurrences and "usage registry" exactly one at either pin. The single-word trace in the reserved-fields gloss is still the only trace in any specification, on either line.

**Added 2026-10-03: code has now outrun both specification lines.** The spec pins were not re-read this pass. They are `raw/03`'s to advance, and §6's statements stand at `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`. What has changed is the comparison. keripy at `80f77b73b` has an Issuee-controlled registry in its sense exactly: a registry whose incepting AID must equal the `a`-section Issuee (§2.2, §7.2). The facade calls the write path "Bind a grant message SAID in an issuee-controlled registry." (`src/keri/acdc/registraring.py:413`). So the sentence "the spec does not contemplate an *Issuee*-controlled registry" is now a statement about a gap between specification and reference implementation, not about the state of the art. An implementer following either ACDC spec line would not know that a `grant` to a keripy verifier can be refused for lacking an Issuee registry anchor that no specification mentions. ### 6.1 What `v1.1` adds that this design leans on, and one label collision

`raw/03` owns `v1.1` and the `dp` disclosure-paths construct; this is a cross-reference, not a re-mining.

**The origin node is now normatively defined, which §2.3's argument needs.** `v1.1` §Disclosure Paths at `2362e48a1`: "That DAG MUST have exactly one source node, called the origin node." The whole of §2.3's claim — that committing to the origin ACDC's SAID commits to every ACDC reachable from it, and therefore that one presentation-registry anchor covers the DAG — rests on a DAG having exactly one origin. That premise is `[N1.1]`. The same section makes the bespoke-origin construction normative too: where an exchange conveys several unchained ACDCs, "a DAG MUST be formed by issuing a bespoke ACDC whose node is the origin." So the object #1613 and #1627 disagree about (§4a) is itself specified, while the disagreement — whether it needs a blinded state registry — is not.

**The `o` label means two different things and the spec only defines one of them.** In the ACDC specification, at both pins, `o` is the **Operator** field of an Edge or Edge-group block. The `o` this note has been calling "the `grant`'s origin field" is a field of the IPEX exchange message, which the ACDC spec does not define at all — it lives in #1613, #1627, #1629 and keripy. The collision is harmless while the two never appear in the same field map, and they do not, but any prose that says "the `o` field" without saying which one is ambiguous, and §2.3's "the `grant`'s `o` field names the DAG's origin" is `[P]` plus `[K]`, never `[N]` or `[N1.1]`. The *concept* it names is `[N1.1]`; the *field carrying it* is not standardized anywhere.

## 7. keripy implementation status @`80f77b73b` (2026-10-03)

*Re-read 2026-10-03 from code at `80f77b73b`, read with `git show`/`git grep` and never from a diff alone. The 2026-09-15 statement at `b8f60166b` is retained verbatim under §7.9, with superseded markers on the claims that no longer hold. Every claim in §§7.1–7.8 is `[K]` at `80f77b73b` unless marked otherwise.*

**The verifier-side presentation registry is built, on `grant` only, and it fails closed. The Issuee's write path is built. Discovery of a foreign registry is not built, and nothing in either specification describes any of it.**

### 7.1 What is implemented, in one paragraph

On every `/ipex/grant`, after the DAG walk, the edge semantics and Issuer authentication of every node have passed, `IpexHandler.verify` runs `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` — "Verify every presentation registry declared across the DAG." (`src/keri/acdc/ipexing.py:609`). For each node it collects the `(i, rd)` pairs declared in a disclosed `a` section or disclosed `A` aggregate entries, provided the node also has a top-level `rd`. For each pair it requires that the registry named by `rd` is incepted by that `i`; that its latest non-vacuous update, as of the receiver's KRAM acceptance time, binds this `grant`'s SAID; and that the node carries a KEL source reference from the Issuee to the binding event. Any failure rejects the `grant`. The Issuees so authenticated are returned as the `tethered` set and count as anchorers for a truthy `ax`. The other five verbs are untouched: `apply`, `offer`, `agree`, `admit` and `spurn` never consult a presentation registry, which matches #1613's statement that the `grant` is "the presentation".

### 7.2 What is checked, and where it fails closed

The chain is `verify` → `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` (`ipexing.py:1493`) → `_presentationRegistries` (`:1409`) → `_vetRegistry` (`:1319`) → `regeventing.vetBinds` (`src/keri/acdc/regeventing.py:647`) → `regeventing.vet`. Each step refuses rather than skipping:

- **Declaration discovery** (`_presentationRegistries`). The trigger is #1613's three-part test, extended to `A`. A top-level `rd` is required, and without it an inner `rd` is "hidden issuer registry, not a presentation registry." (`:1419`), which fails the node (§2.4). A compact `a` or `A`, or any undisclosed aggregate entry, returns `None` and fails the `grant` — "Every inspected aggregate entry must be a mapping." (`:1458`) — because hiding could conceal a requirement (§2.3). A malformed `rd` or `i` fails it too. The comment at the point of collection states the semantics: "The declaration is the issuer's signal that this Issuee must" authenticate the grant through this node-local registry (`:1516`).
- **KRAM precondition.** "A presentation requirement needs a completed KRAM acceptance." (`:1524`). With no `kramTMSC` record carrying `rdt`, the `grant` fails. `rdt` is set only when KRAM accepts the complete message: for a multi-key sender a partial-signature cache is written with an empty `rdt` until the threshold is met ("A partial cache is only a replay marker. Receiver time is" recorded once the complete threshold is accepted, `kraming.py:1658-1661`). So a `grant` carrying any presentation-registry declaration is rejected unless KRAM fully accepted it, and the registry binding must be dated no later than that acceptance — the `_vetRegistry` call is commented "Require a valid binding anchored before the KRAM-accepted send." (`ipexing.py:1543`). The proxy test exercises the first half: complete evidence is still refused when dispatched without a KRAM decision (`tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py:6169`). The KRAM side of this coupling is recorded in `raw/15`.
- **Registry evidence.** The TEL must be held locally: "Registry verification requires an injected local Regery." (`:1290`), and a handler without one returns `None`. A missing `rip`, head or intermediate update raises `MissingChainError`, which is retryable from escrow.
- **Historical cutoff.** `vetBinds` drops updates whose `dt` is later than the KRAM acceptance time and raises `MisbindingError` if the `rip` itself is later (`regeventing.py:686-705`).
- **Controller.** "if controller is not None and record.issuer != controller:" raises `MisbindingError` (`regeventing.py:714`). This is the Issuee-control check, and it replaces the Issuer-equality check for this path (§7.4).
- **Binding.** Walking newest-first, every update back to the latest non-vacuous one must have exactly one disclosure attached to the node, otherwise `UnverifiedBlindError`. The first non-vacuous one must bind the target, otherwise `MisbindingError` "does not bind expected target" (`regeventing.py:748`). The target is the `grant` SAID (`ipexing.py:1537`). A trailing vacuous update therefore needs its own disclosure, as the proxy test shows (`test_ipexing.py:6118-6121`, `:6136-6147`).
- **Source.** "Presentation timing requires the Grant to carry this bup's KEL source." (`regeventing.py:750`). The node's attached source triples are filtered to the Issuee and matched against the TEL events they actually seal (`ipexing.py:1333-1364`). A source whose KEL event is not yet local is retryable, and otherwise the result is `MisanchorError` and refusal (`:1395-1400`).
- **Declared registry is the vetted registry.** `MisregistryError` if the vetted chain's `regid` differs from the declared `rd` (`:1384-1387`). The test that a valid registry cannot be substituted for the declared one is `test_ipex_v2_rejects_substituted_presentation_registry` (`test_ipexing.py:6301`).

Every permanent error maps to `return None`, and every `None` maps to `return False` in `verify` ("Reject any malformed, missing, or invalid presentation factor.", `ipexing.py:616`). Nothing falls back to a weaker factor.

### 7.3 Who must sign the `grant`, and the multiply-endorsed `grant`

`grant()` gained `endorsers` and `anchorers`: "Additional local grantors that sign the" grant, while "The sender remains the KRAM-authenticated outer sender." (`ipexing.py:1946-1947`). `_sign` attaches a signature group per endorser and, per anchorer, creates a KEL event sealing the `grant` SAID and attaches a source-seal triple naming that AID (`ipexing.py:280-407`). On receipt, `verifyEvidence` keeps non-sender evidence only for `grant` — "Only Grant accepts supplemental grantor evidence." (`:1574`) — drops signatures not at the endorser's current establishment state and stale seals, and prefers a seal over signatures from the same AID: "A valid current seal is the stronger factor for an AID, so do not" retain a redundant signature (`:1608`). Other verbs refuse any non-sender evidence (`:1561`). Accepted endorsers are indexed in a new `eidx` store (`src/keri/db/basing.py:591`, `src/keri/peer/exchanging.py:620`).

Against #1613 §Multiply Endorsed IPEX message and §KRAM Augmentation (`[P]`), this implements: non-sender signatures and non-sender seal-source references as endorsements; validation after KRAM rather than in it — "the IPEX processor MUST validate all non-sender endorsements after KRAM." — and the preference rule "If the seal source reference is valid, then the set of signatures is ignored." It does **not** implement, or this pass did not find:

- **Endorsement on any verb but `grant`.** #1613 says the other messages "do not need multiple endorsements", so this is conformance.
- **A tethering check.** #1613 §Timeliness Tethered Presentations defines tethering as the property that a Grantor's fresh proof of control extends to that AID's appearances in the DAG. The code does not require any Issuee in the DAG to be a Grantor. The only use of the word in code is the `tethered` set of registry-authenticated Issuees, consumed by the `ax` rule alone.
- **A threshold check on non-sender signatures.** #1613 says an endorsement whose verified signatures do not meet the endorser's threshold is invalid. This pass did not read `verifyAttachments`, so whether non-sender signature groups are threshold-checked is unverified.

#1613's own scope note still applies, since it "does not solve for the more generic use cases of a proxy negotiator for multiple other AID". The code does not either, beyond the three-AID `ax` rule. #1555 is not in the manifest and was not read, so this note cannot compare the code with #1555's design (§8 item 8).

**The `grant` no longer needs the presentation-registry Issuee as a Grantor.** This is the "grantor requisite" `b7c170f93` removed, analysed in §2.6. At `80f77b73b` the Grantor claims in §2.2–§2.6 stand as follows. The Issuee must control the registry: confirmed `[K]`. The Issuee "is also a Grantor": not enforced. The requirement applies "when the Issuee is the Grantor": the code applies it unconditionally, to every declaration in the DAG, whoever sends. The `grant` is "always double-authenticated": true when the Issuee is the sender, false otherwise. The sender is always KRAM-authenticated, since the outer signature is untouched, and Grantors may be non-sender AIDs: `[K]`.

### 7.5 The Issuee's write path

`Registrar.present` — "Bind a grant message SAID in an issuee-controlled registry." (`src/keri/acdc/registraring.py:413`) — and `Registrar.vacate` (`:419`) delegate to the registry event engine's `present`, which validates the target as a SAID and builds a blinded update with default state `"presented"` (`regeventing.py:1000`), and `vacate`, which builds a vacuous update (`regeventing.py:1012`). ## 8. Open questions and unresolved tensions

*Revised 2026-09-15; revised again 2026-10-03 against keripy `80f77b73b` and #1098. Each item carries its state at the current pins. Nothing is deleted.*

1. **Name. Still open, and the case for the usage name got stronger.** "Issuee usage registry" (#1095, #1550) vs "presentation registry" (#1613, #1618) vs "user presentation registry" (KERIcon). #1550 shows the primitive is used beyond presentation; #1627 adds a fourth job, the transfer registry (§4a), and the ACDC spec's own `rd` gloss lists four purposes. Four uses, one of which is presentation, and the name of the whole is the name of the one. *2026-10-03: the reference implementation has now picked the narrow name.* keripy calls it a "presentation registry" or "issuee-controlled registry", the SEDI schemas call it an "Issuee Presentation Registry", and "usage registry" occurs nowhere in the tree (§7.7). The code also scopes the mechanism to its narrow use: the only registry an Issuee writes through the new facade binds a `grant` SAID with state `"presented"`. Code names do not bind a specification, but they do set the vocabulary most implementers will meet first. That makes the case for a general name more urgent, not weaker, and it remains open.
2. **`rd`-in-`a` disambiguation. Still open, and it is now known that `v1.1` does not settle it.** EGF-declared, restricted to presentation registries in `v1.1`, or left ambiguous? #1613 argues both directions and settles neither. `raw/03` reports the relevant spec text byte-identical between `f0bd097de` and `2362e48a1`, so none of the three resolutions has been taken up in the branch most likely to carry it. This still blocks any normative text — and §2.4's newly mined mutual-exclusion rule raises what it costs, since the choice is permanent per ACDC and forecloses the other feature entirely. *2026-10-03: resolved in keripy, still open in the specifications.* At `80f77b73b`, an `a`-section `rd` with a top-level `rd` is a presentation registry, and an `a`-section `rd` without one is refused as an unsupported hidden Issuer registry (§2.4, `[K]`). That is the "default … for anchoring presentation exchanges" plus the "forbid" option, minus the "presentation registry without an Issuer state registry" case #1613 said forbidding would enable. Interoperability now depends on the specifications either adopting this rule or rejecting it explicitly. A v1-conformant ACDC that uses `a.rd` as a hidden state registry is rejected by keripy's IPEX verifier today.
3. ~~**Is the ten-month silence meaningful?**~~ **Resolved: no.** Sam named presentation registries as work to be built on a call on 2026-09-01 (§0). The written record understates the design's standing, which is a caution about this corpus generally: a dead discussion thread is not evidence of a dead idea. *Reinforced 2026-09-15, and the evidence improved: the resolution no longer rests on the call. Sam wrote about presentation registries on #1627 on 2026-09-02, and PR #1674 stated on 2026-09-11 that presentation-registry anchoring "will come in a follow up PR." The corpus held a true claim on uncitable evidence for four days, which is the argument for recording the weak-provenance class explicitly rather than either using it silently or refusing it.*
4. **The `rip`-binds-to-Issuer wording. Still open, and now with a concrete implementation cost attached.** Spec `:2019` says the `rd` value "cryptographically binds the Registry to the Issuer AID," which for a presentation registry means the Issuee. As of 2026-09-01 this was a wording problem for future spec text. As of `b8f60166b` it is also a code problem: `vetBindings` enforces exactly this reading, refusing any ACDC whose `i` differs from the registry incepter's (§7.2). The spec ambiguity has been resolved in code, in the direction that excludes this design. *2026-10-03: the code problem is gone and the wording problem remains.* At `80f77b73b` the Issuer-equality check runs only for a top-level `rd`, and the presentation path checks instead that the registry's incepter equals the `a`-section Issuee (§7.4). So keripy now reads the spec's "Issuer AID" as "whoever incepted this registry", which is the reading this item asked for. Spec `:2019` still says "the Issuer AID", and no specification text says otherwise.
5. **Latency. Still open, and worse than stated.** A presentation registry inserts a registry write between message creation and KRAM acceptance, and the verifier's time-delay trick from #1095 adds more deliberately. §2.6 shows the write is *additional* to the signature or KEL anchor rather than replacing either, since KRAM cannot see a registry factor at all. Nothing connects the two designs numerically, and nothing in `bible/09` or `raw/15` gives a window class that would accommodate a registry round-trip plus an observer fetch. *2026-10-03: keripy has chosen a timing rule, and it is looser than "must pass KRAM".* The registry write is not inside the KRAM window. The registry check runs after KRAM and accepts a binding whose TEL `dt` is no later than the receiver's KRAM acceptance time. The KEL event anchoring that binding may arrive later, through escrow (§2.2). The Issuee asserts the TEL `dt`; the receiver's acceptance time comes from the receiver's clock. Whether an Issuee-asserted timestamp is enough for the timeliness #1613 requires is a design question the code answers implicitly. The supporting argument — that the binding cannot predate the `grant` it names — is sound for the lower bound only. A compromised signer could backdate `dt`, but then the TEL event is anchored in the real Issuee's KEL and is detectable, which is the point of the mechanism. So the looseness may be acceptable, but nobody has written that argument down.
6. **The trusted-third-party concession. Still open, and it is two concessions, not one.** #1095 admits Verifier-specific protection "violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party" and justifies it by localized harm and aligned incentives. #1613 makes a second, distinct relaxation that the first pass did not record: the presentation registry itself is not a self-sufficient protection. In Sam's words it gives the Grantor "an Issuer-Verifier-protected mechanism to detect compromise of its signing keys" — it requires an Issuer that is a different party and does not collude, and a Verifier that enforces, since "The verifier then enforces the protection by not accepting presentations (grant messages) that are not so anchored…" — the sentence continues with the condition that the issued ACDC carry both `rd` and `i`, which narrows the rule without weakening the dependency. So the Issuee-side detectability story depends on two other parties at both ends, and the mechanism offered as the alternative to trusting a third party is itself two-party-dependent. Both concessions are acknowledged in the sources and neither is an oversight; the doctrine residual should name the second as well as the first.
7. **Correlation resistance is conditional. Still open, unchanged.** It requires blinded registry state *and*, for unlinkability of the registry itself, bulk issuance with independent AIDs. A plain usage registry is "bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable."

11. **New 2026-10-03 — the code's requirement is unconditional, and #1613's is not.** #1613 makes the anchoring requirement apply "at the time of presentation by the Issuee of that ACDC when the Issuee is the Grantor of that IPEX" (§Registry Anchored Granted ACDCs), and repeats the condition at §New Anchored Exchange `ax` Field. keripy applies it to every declaration in the granted DAG, whoever sends the `grant` (§7.3). The two agree when the holder presents its own credential. They differ for the case #1613 §Anchoring Requirement Complication describes, where "an ACDC is presented as evidence, but the Issuee AID is unrelated to the Discloser's AID". Under keripy's rule, nobody can present such an ACDC unless its Issuee writes this `grant`'s SAID into its registry. That may be the intent: it is the strongest reading of "an imposter … can't avoid the requirement". But it makes a presentation-registry ACDC unusable as third-party evidence, and neither #1613 nor the code comments say whether that was weighed. **JUDGMENT for Daniel:** whether to raise this with the implementer. It is a scoping question, not a bug report.
12. **New 2026-10-03 — single versus double authentication for a non-sender Issuee.** #1613 says every Grantor must endorse the `grant` and that a registry-anchored `grant` is "always double-authenticated". keripy accepts a proxy-sent `grant` in which the Issuee's only participation is the registry binding (§2.6). The change was deliberate (`b7c170f93` inside PR #1693), and on its face it is defensible, because a KEL-anchored binding to the exact `grant` SAID is a stronger factor than a signature. But the design document and the code now disagree about what a Grantor is. A reader of #1613 will expect a check the code does not make. One downstream effect follows: the endorser index does not list registry-only Issuees, so any business logic that finds a holder's presentations through `eidx` will miss them. **JUDGMENT for Daniel:** whether this goes upstream as a request to update #1613's text to match the code, or the reverse.
13. **New 2026-10-03 — keripy refuses `grant`s for reasons no specification states.** At `80f77b73b` an IPEX verifier rejects four kinds of `grant`. First, one whose ACDC declares an Issuee registry that does not bind it. Second, one carrying an ACDC with an `a`-section `rd` and no top-level `rd`, which is v1-legal as a hidden state registry. Third, one carrying a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` is compact. Fourth, one carrying a registry-backed ACDC with any undisclosed `A` entry. All four are `[K]` (§7.2), and none is `[N]` or `[N1.1]` (§6). The third and fourth follow from the first, because a verifier cannot tell an omitted requirement from a hidden one. A peer implementation built from either spec line will produce `grant`s keripy rejects, with no normative text to explain why. The chapter should say so plainly.
14. **New 2026-10-03 — the disclosure cost of fail-closed declaration discovery is untested for aggregates.** A registry-backed ACDC can be granted only with its `a` section at least partly expanded and its `A` entries all disclosed. For `a` this costs little: SEDI tests compact the nested blocks and keep the top level expanded (`tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py:1425`). For `A`, rejecting any aggregate entry that is not a mapping — "Every inspected aggregate entry must be a mapping." (`ipexing.py:1458`) — appears to rule out selective aggregate disclosure of a registry-backed ACDC altogether. The SEDI Age credential is exactly such an ACDC: its schema requires a top-level `rd` and an `A`. No IPEX test at `80f77b73b` presents it. This note has not checked how keripy represents an undisclosed aggregate element. If an undisclosed element is not a mapping, then an Age credential cannot be selectively presented through keripy IPEX at this pin. That would collide with `raw/11`'s age-proof design. **Unverified; flagged for `raw/11` and for the next pass.**
