# 08 — Presentation Architectures and the IPEX Disclosure Model {#presentation}

## Thesis {#thesis}

An ACDC presentation is not the disclosure of a credential. It is the disclosure of a directed acyclic graph of credentials, normalized so that the graph has exactly one source node — the origin — and negotiated by naming paths into that graph rather than by naming attributes. That single structural commitment is what lets ACDC absorb the other verifiable-credential architectures as special cases: a one-node DAG is a portable data lake, and a bespoke origin node with N edges is a credential soup that has been converted into something cryptographically rigid. Everything hard about ACDC presentation follows from a second observation, which the graph structure does not answer: a DAG commits to data, and a presentation must also establish who is presenting, right now, and with what authority. That is the authentication-factor problem, and it is where the current design is least settled — freshness (KRAM), multiple endorsement, anchoring (`ax`), tethering, and presentation registries are all answers to it, all proposed since late July, and none of them present in any specification branch today. This chapter separates the tiers rigorously, because the single most common error in this area is quoting a design discussion as though it were the spec — and the second most common, which this chapter itself committed in its previous draft, is quoting the forward branch of a specification as though it were the standardizing one.

## 1. The four presentation architectures {#architectures}

The taxonomy is from discussion #1627, "ACDC Presentation Architectures" (SmithSamuelM, 2026-08-19). It is a framing document rather than a proposal for most of its length, and the framing is useful independent of whether one accepts its conclusions. [proposal]

### Portable Datalake {#portable-datalake}

One document, nested blocks, all attributes about one subject from one issuer; the holder discloses a subset at presentation time, often by checking boxes against an array. This is mDoc/mDL and mainstream W3C VC. Its advantage is simplicity. Its named disadvantages: it cannot express delegation or a delegation chain; everything must come from exactly one issuer, "Not one root-of-trust with a distributed set of hierarchically delegated Issuers, but one and only one Issuer"; the issuer must pull from every relevant database to issue it, so the architecture pushes issuers toward exactly the consolidated data lake that maximizes breach blast radius; and reissuance is forced at the periodicity of the most rapidly changing attribute in the document.

#### Credential Soup {#credential-soup}

Attributes drawn from several credentials, potentially several issuers and several issuees, assembled at presentation time. This was Sovrin's model. It permits issuer-side database partitioning and reissuance tuned to each attribute's dynamism. Its costs are that presentation negotiation becomes ambiguous (an attribute may be sourced from any of several credentials), that each distinct source needs its own fresh proof of control, that proving several issuee AIDs represent the same party needs a mechanism the soup does not supply, and that a soup "cannot support delegation in any normative secure way. At least not as a cryptographically verifiable data structure."

#### Verifiable DAG {#verifiable-dag}

ACDC's default. Edges carry properties, each node is separably authenticable, and each edge carries a cryptographic digest of the node it points to. The consequence: "A verifiable cryptographic commitment to the origin ACDC of that DAG is a verifiable commitment to every node in the DAG," giving the graph Merkle-tree-like properties. Typically only the issuer or issuee of the origin needs a fresh authentication factor. Delegation is native. Forgery "must be in totality, and not piecemeal," where a soup can be forged piecemeal. The costs are verification complexity and a reissuance cascade discussed in [section 12](#sedi-recipes).

#### DAG Soup {#dag-soup-architecture}

Several disconnected DAGs presented in one exchange, each with its own origin, requiring a multiply-endorsed grant. Most general, most complex, and the subject of #1627's only concrete proposal ([section 11](#dag-soup)).

#### The load-bearing claim of the taxonomy {#subsumption-claim}

The load-bearing claim of the taxonomy is that ACDC subsumes the other two: "the ACDC DAG, as a special case, can mimic either a portable data lake or a credential soup as needed." A single ACDC with a selectively disclosable aggregate section is a portable data lake. A bespoke origin ACDC with N edges is a credential soup that has been given a verifiable spine. This is well-supported by the normative structure in [section 2](#one-dag-one-origin) and [section 3](#bespoke-origin) and is, in this chapter's judgment, the strongest argument in #1627.

## 2. The normalized structure: one DAG, one origin {#one-dag-one-origin}

This is the structural commitment everything else rests on, and it is [spec-next] — present in the `v1.1` branch at `2362e48a1` and absent from the standardizing line, where the section that states it does not exist. The previous draft of this chapter marked it [spec]. That was an over-tiering, and it mattered: a reader who took "one DAG, one origin" as binding today would be treating an unadopted branch as conformance. The structure is well-argued and is the premise of everything from [section 4](#disclosure-paths) through [section 12](#sedi-recipes), but nothing obliges an implementer to honor it yet.

An ACDC is not a node; it is a graph fragment. "It consists of a near-side node and that node's outgoing Edges, which its Edge Section contains. An ACDC holds no Edges incoming to its own node, only outgoing ones; its incoming Edges belong to other fragments" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > DAG of ACDCs, L1817 @2362e48a1). A source node "has no incoming Edges. It MUST either have no Edges at all or only outgoing Edges" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > DAG of ACDCs, L1819 @2362e48a1). [spec-next]

The normalization is two sentences, and both are worth memorizing because the rest of the design quotes them constantly: "That DAG MUST have exactly one source node, called the origin node" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > DAG of ACDCs, L1821 @2362e48a1), and where the material to be conveyed is not already connected, "a DAG MUST be formed by issuing a bespoke ACDC whose node is the origin, with Edges chaining back to every other ACDC the exchange includes" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > DAG of ACDCs, L1823 @2362e48a1). The second gives the bespoke-ACDC construction of [section 3](#bespoke-origin) a structural job it did not have before, on top of its contractual one. The branch states plainly what the normalization is for: "This gives every exchange the same normalized structure, on which the `dp` field depends." [spec-next]

Two consequences an adversarial reviewer should hold onto.

First, the origin is a source, not a root of authority, and these run in opposite directions. Edges point near→far and carry a digest of the far node, so authority flows toward the origin from the far nodes, while commitment flows from the origin to everything reachable. #1627 uses "root of the DAG" for the far-side, deepest node — "if a given ACDC is the root of the DAG, and that ACDC's attributes change and it must be reissued, then every branch that sinks into the ACDC must be reissued" — which is the correct cascade direction but the opposite end of the graph from the origin. The terms "origin" and "root" name opposite ends here. Anyone reading #1627 alongside the spec should translate rather than assume.

Second, the DAG is well-ordered and therefore linearizable. Field maps inside an ACDC MUST be insertion-ordered — that part is [spec], on both branches — and arrays are ordered, and each Edge Section is an ordered field map, so "A well-ordered DAG with a single source node linearizes into a unique, reproducible order" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > DAG of ACDCs > Ordering, L1831 @2362e48a1). Breadth-first from the origin is the chosen order, "because in some applications, such as a dossier using joint issuance, the joint-issued ACDCs fall together in breadth-first order and scatter in depth-first." Depth-first would also be unique and reproducible; the choice is argued from readability rather than correctness. This reproducibility is what lets `dp` identify an ACDC by position rather than by SAID ([section 4](#disclosure-paths)), which is in turn what keeps a disclosure request from introducing correlators of its own. [spec-next]

One vocabulary trap the branch sets deliberately, and it will bite anyone reading [section 4](#disclosure-paths) quickly. "A DAG of ACDCs is therefore a graph of subgraphs, one subgraph per ACDC. So in what follows, node may mean a node of the DAG of ACDCs or a node of one ACDC's subgraph." The overload is intentional and is never disambiguated by a qualifier, so "node path" in [section 4](#disclosure-paths) means a node of the subgraph — a block within one ACDC — while "origin node" means a node of the DAG of ACDCs. [spec-next]

## 3. The bespoke origin ACDC {#bespoke-origin}

A bespoke (disclosure-specific) ACDC is issued by the Discloser, for one exchange, specifically to become the origin. It is [spec] and has been for some time: "A given Discloser issues its own bespoke ACDC referencing some other ACDC via an Edge. This means that the normal validation logic and tooling for a chained ACDC can be applied without complicating the presentation exchange logic" (ACDC spec > Disclosure-specific (Bespoke) Issued ACDCs, L1862 @f0bd097de; identical at L2063 @2362e48a1). The spec names the rich-presentation use directly: it "effectively enables a type of rich presentation or combined disclosure where multiple ACDCs MAY be referenced by edges in the bespoke ACDC... without requiring any new tooling."

Note the tier split running through this section. The bespoke construction itself is [spec] — it is in `main`, and was there before any of the current design conversation started. The structural job [section 2](#one-dag-one-origin) gives it, normalizing several unchained ACDCs into one DAG with a single origin, is [spec-next] and lives only in the forward branch. A reader who conflates them will think the whole origin-node apparatus is standardized because the bespoke ACDC is.

Its two standard jobs are to carry presentation-specific contractual terms in its rule section — the spec's worked example carries an anti-assimilation clause and a one-time-purpose clause (ACDC spec > Disclosure-specific (Bespoke) Issued ACDCs > Example of a Bespoke Issued ACDC, L2069-2114 @2362e48a1) — and to name the Disclosee as its Issuee, so that "Signing the agreement to the offer of that bespoke ACDC consummates a contract between the named Issuer and the named Issuee."

### A purpose clause belongs in the bespoke origin {#purpose-clause}

Asked whether an exchange message should carry a human-readable statement of purpose in its `m` field, Sam pointed at the bespoke origin's rule section instead: "The advantage of putting it in the DAG is that it gets all the security, perpetuality, etc of the DAG" (keripy discussion #1617, comment of 2026-08-13, R-064, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1617#discussioncomment-18006490). His hesitation is the design ethos, not the mechanism: "I am always worried when we do stuff like that , that we are not following the design ethos of minimally sufficient means." It is a stated preference, not a ruling for removal. Phil Feairheller replied that Locksmith and keripy's `kli` both use `m`; Sam asked whether it reaches the user and did not decide (same discussion, 2026-08-13 and 2026-08-14). [proposal]

### The bespoke origin as recipe {#recipe}

#1627 adds a third job and a name for it: the bespoke origin as recipe. [proposal] The argument is that a set of unchained ACDCs issued by one root-of-trust to one issuee — the SEDI shape, State → citizen — looks like a soup but is a degenerate one, since "All the ingredients came from the same cupboard." A bespoke origin with one edge per ingredient converts it into a DAG at presentation time. The origin "does not need to be anchored or use a registry, so it's simple... It merely needs to be signed and have attached signatures because it's a one-time use ACDC."

The interoperability move is the interesting part: the bespoke ACDC is one-time-use but its schema is not. "The schema of the bespoke ACDC, however, is not one-time use. It acts as a permanent recipe for the soup... an EGF can define the schema of the bespoke ACDC for all its seminal use cases." Since type-is-schema and the schema SAID is a cryptographic commitment, an EGF-published recipe schema gives tooling a fixed target without fixing the instances.

#### A correction that matters, because the sentence will be quoted {#recipe-correction}

#1627 justifies the edge operator on a recipe edge as follows: "the Issuer of the origin ACDC (recipe) is the Issuee of the ACDCs on the far side of each of its edges. Therefore, each edge from the recipe ACDC can use the I2I edge operator." That is right, and it is exactly the `I2I` relation ([section 5](#edges)). The restatement immediately following it — "the Issuer of the recipe is the citizen AID, and the Issuer of all the far node ACDCs is also the citizen AID" — is wrong: the far nodes are issued by the State and the citizen is their Issuee. The first sentence is the correct one; the second contradicts it and would, if implemented, describe a set of self-issued credentials.

## 4. Disclosure paths: the `dp` field {#disclosure-paths}

`dp` is how a disclosure is requested, and it is the month's flagship movement: after evolving through discussions #1542 → #1512 → #1549, it landed in the ACDC `v1.1` branch as a ~185-line section that declares its own normativity, and it landed in keripy as a validated wire field. Both movements are real and neither is as large as it sounds, for opposite reasons. On the spec side the tier is [spec-next] and stops there: `## Disclosure Paths` exists only on `v1.1` at `2362e48a1`, and the standardizing line at `f0bd097de` contains the phrase zero times, so there is no `main` text to upgrade it with. On the code side the tier is [code] for the wire shape only, and nothing validates a disclosure against the plan it negotiated — see the end of this section.

The section asserts its own status in its first sentence: "This section is normative. It defines the path syntax that designates the parts of an ACDC, or of a DAG of chained ACDCs" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths, L1811 @2362e48a1). That assertion is worth reading carefully rather than at face value, because the only place the branch binds `dp` to a wire format is the Disclosure Paths in `apply` and `offer` subsection, which sits inside the IPEX section that opens "This section is non-normative." The syntax is normative and its sole use is not — a seam in the branch itself, not an artifact of this chapter's reading, and it is carried as an open question in [section 14](#open-questions). [spec-next]

### Not an ACDC field {#dp-not-acdc-field}

It is not an ACDC field. This is the first of two shape facts that are easy to state wrongly, and the spec states it flatly: "The `dp` field is not an ACDC field. It appears in the messages that negotiate a disclosure, not in the ACDCs that are disclosed" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths, L1813 @2362e48a1). It is deliberately not added to the reserved-label table for that reason — unlike `_`, which is ([section 5](#edges)). An ACDC that carried a `dp` field would be making a category error: the disclosure plan belongs to the negotiation, and the negotiation is not what gets signed as evidence. This matters for [section 11](#dag-soup). [spec-next]

#### Shape {#dp-shape}

The value is "a list of tuples," each of which "represents one ACDC in the DAG that is the object of the exchange and is of the form `(ACDCSchemaSAID, PathPrefix, [paths])`", and "In a serialization that has no distinct tuple type, such as JSON, each tuple MUST be represented as a three-element array" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field, L1881 @2362e48a1). The first element is the qb64 SAID of that ACDC's schema. The three-element form is the settled one; a two-element form appears in #1549's body and was superseded by its own comment stream on 2026-08-04. [spec-next]

#### Why a list and not a field map {#dp-why-list}

This is the design decision most directly at stake in [section 11](#dag-soup): "A list of tuples is used rather than a field map keyed by Schema SAID so that a Schema SAID MAY appear more than once. Two ACDCs of the same type, that is, of the same Schema SAID, MAY appear in one DAG, and a field map could not tell them apart" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field, L1883 @2362e48a1). The branch's own worked example is such a case, with a research report and a project report sharing one schema. [spec-next]

#### Why paths and not schemas {#why-paths}

The reason a disclosure request is a path over a graph, rather than a schema describing the wanted subset, is Sam's layering argument, stated while `dp` was being designed: "So disclosure is reasoning about exposure. What parts to expose when. Not what those parts mean." (keripy PR #1505, comment of 2026-07-16, R-058, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/pull/1505#issuecomment-4992746261). Knowledge graphs reason about semantics, and in the same comment ACDC exists "to invert that layering", putting security and correlation resistance below the semantic layer, so schema stays type and nothing more. It is his design rationale, not specification text. [proposal]

#### Path syntax {#path-syntax}

"A path is a tuple of components," each a field label or, where the collection is an array, a zero-based integer offset. A path beginning with `/` is DAG-absolute, rooted at the origin's top level; one that does not is ACDC-relative. Edge traversal MUST be DAG-absolute and "MUST therefore begin with `/e`"; the hop across an edge to the far ACDC is written with the virtual component `_`, so `/e/reports/project/_/a/author` descends the origin's edge section to the `project` edge, hops, and lands on the far ACDC's `a/author` (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Path Syntax > Traversing Edges, L1845 @2362e48a1). The `n` field label is not a path component; "`_` stands for the traversal that `n` designates." Two hops nest the same way. Requirements bind the effective path — prefix and path taken together — not the entry alone. [spec-next]

#### Two delimiters, and the label restriction that pays for them {#label-restrictions}

Components are separated by `/` in the ordinary serialization "and when serialized compactly as a CESR primitive, by `-`". That choice forces two new rules, which `v1.1` states in a section that does not exist on `main` at all: a field label "MUST NOT, therefore, contain the character `-`", and since that leaves `_` as the only free Base64 character, "The label `_` MUST therefore be reserved as a virtual path component denoting that hop" and a field MUST NOT be labeled with a single `_`. Only the lone label is forbidden — `first_name` is fine — and the restriction does not reach schema-section labels, which no path descends into. This is backward-incompatible for any already-issued ACDC carrying such a label, and the branch argues the cost is low without saying anything about migrating; [section 14](#open-questions) carries that as an open question. [spec-next]

#### Node vs leaf: the hammer and the scalpel {#node-vs-leaf}

A path ending in `/` designates a whole node and "the full expansion of every branch beneath it", plus enough of the branch leading to it to validate the SAIDs along the way; a path ending in a non-empty component "designates a single leaf" and "requires no sibling branch" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Path Syntax > Node Paths and Leaf Paths, L1855-1861 @2362e48a1). The spec's own slogan is "The node form is a hammer, the leaf form a scalpel." `a/d/` and `a/` designate the same block, and the top-level `d/` therefore designates a whole ACDC. [spec-next]

#### Closures {#closures}

Closures are the semantics, and they depend on how the section blinds. "A disclosure path translates into a closure over the fields or array elements it implies," and "The disclosure designated by a set of paths is the union of their closures" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Path Syntax > Closures, L1865 @2362e48a1). In a partially disclosable section — Attribute, Edge, Rule — one SAID and one UUID cover the whole block, so a leaf path "closes over every simple field of that block. Its nested sub-blocks stay compacted as their SAIDs." That is why a leaf path into an edge yields the edge's `n` along with its siblings: it is exactly what verifying the edge against the far ACDC requires. In the selectively disclosable Aggregate Section each block carries its own `d` and `u`, so a leaf path there "closes over that block alone and tells nothing of any sibling." The schema section is always disclosed and needs no path; the rule section is usually but not necessarily disclosed, "MUST NOT be assumed fully disclosed," and must be designated when wanted. And the boundary that keeps this mechanism from being mistaken for a permissions system: "A closure states what a path discloses. It does not bound what the Disclosee may then do with what it holds." Unblinding an edge yields the far SAID and so enables a further request, but that request is a separate disclosure with a closure of its own. Chain-Link Confidentiality does that job; paths do not. [spec-next]

#### Prefix factoring {#prefix-factoring}

The prefix is the DAG-absolute route to the ACDC the tuple names; it "MUST be either the empty string or a DAG-absolute path that both begins and ends with the path delimiter." Effective path is prefix ⧺ entry with nothing inserted, and "An entry of a path list MUST NOT begin with the path delimiter," which makes the concatenation always well-formed (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field > Path Prefix, L1889-1891 @2362e48a1) and holds equally of the compact `-` serialization. A prefix "MUST NOT reach past the top level of the ACDC it names." With a non-empty prefix, an empty entry `""` designates the whole named ACDC; with an empty prefix it designates nothing, and `d/` is the shortest way to say "the whole of this one." [spec-next]

#### Ordering, and when it stops being sufficient {#dp-ordering}

Elements MUST appear in breadth-first order and "The zeroth element MUST represent the origin node ACDC" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field > Identifying Each Tuple's ACDC, L1899 @2362e48a1). Where prefixes are non-empty, position is not what identifies a tuple, and the list "MAY omit any ACDC from which nothing is requested." The ordering is required anyway, on the grounds that any party generating `dp` must linearize the DAG to walk it. The exception that forces explicit prefixes: where a schema makes an edge or edge-group optional, "a party that knows only the Schema cannot generate a total ordering… The ordering then has gaps. In that case the prefix of every element after the zeroth MUST be non-empty" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field > Identifying Each Tuple's ACDC, L1911 @2362e48a1). Completeness follows the same split: with empty prefixes the list must hold one element per ACDC, an element requesting nothing carrying an empty path list; with non-empty prefixes it may omit such ACDCs entirely. [spec-next]

#### Placement: the second detail that is easy to state wrongly {#dp-placement}

"So where a `dp` field appears in an `apply` or `offer`, it MUST appear in that message's query section, `q`, and not in its attribute section, `a`" (ACDC spec > IPEX Protocol Messages > Disclosure Paths in `apply` and `offer`, L2015 @2362e48a1). The reasoning is the ReST analogy the `exn` message shape already encodes — the route `r` holds the path, `q` holds the query string, `a` holds the body, and "A request to disclose names which parts of which ACDCs are wanted. It is a query." `dp` appears in `apply` and `offer` only. It does not appear in a `grant`. (Qualified 2026-10-15: true of `v1.1`; keripy's head at `c102367ea` validates a `dp` on `agree` and `grant` too, see [the empty-`dp` ruling](#empty-dp).) Getting this backwards is not a stylistic error: `a` is where `ax` and the origin `o` are proposed to live ([section 10](#anchoring)), so a `dp` in `a` would collide with a different field family and would put a request in the slot reserved for what is being conveyed. [spec-next]

#### The route table {#route-table}

The route table changed with it, and this supersedes what the standardizing line says. At `main` @`f0bd097de` the `apply` row reads "Schema or its SAID, Attribute field label list, Aggregate element label list, signature on `apply` or its SAID" and the `offer` row reads "Metadata ACDC or its SAID, Schema or its SAID, partial disclosure, Aggregate element label list, signature on `offer` or its SAID" (ACDC spec > IPEX Protocol Messages, L1811-1813 @f0bd097de) — that is [spec], and it is the model in which a disclosure is negotiated by naming attributes. At `v1.1` @`2362e48a1` both label lists are replaced by `dp`: the `apply` row becomes "Disclosure Paths, `dp`, signature on `apply` or its SAID" and the `offer` row "Metadata ACDC or its SAID, Disclosure Paths, `dp`, partial disclosure, signature on `offer` or its SAID" (ACDC spec > IPEX Protocol Messages, L2004-2006 @2362e48a1) — [spec-next]. The `spurn`, `agree`, `grant` and `admit` rows are identical on both branches. Both rows are live, on different branches, and this chapter records both rather than replacing one with the other: the label-list form is what a conformant implementation of today's standard does, and the path form is what the forward branch expects. Anyone reading a single row without its branch will mis-state the protocol.

#### Solicited response {#solicited-response}

An empty list `[]` in an answering message means "the answering path list is the same as the one it answers"; where the answering list differs, or where the message is unsolicited, the value "MUST NOT be an empty list" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Disclosure Paths, `dp`, Field > Solicited Response, L1917 @2362e48a1). The IPEX subsection carries the same rule through for an `offer` answering an `apply`. [spec-next]

#### Aggregate-section pathing {#aggregate-pathing}

Aggregate-section pathing is the one genuinely special case. Blinded blocks are array elements whose offsets a Disclosee does not know, so a path MAY name a block by its uniquely labeled field: `A/over21` and `A/1/over21` designate the same closure. The shorthand designates the block, not the field, and closes over all of it. It cannot reach into nested sub-blocks — `A/over21/issued` "MUST be rejected when expanded" — on the grounds that partial disclosure inside selective disclosure is an anti-pattern, and "a Schema that calls for it is better rewritten as a DAG of ACDCs" (ACDC spec > Disclosure Paths > Special Cases > Aggregate Section Pathing, L1923-1931 @2362e48a1). The two forms cannot be confused, because a field label MUST NOT begin with a numeral. [spec-next]

#### What landed: the wire shape {#dp-wire-shape}

What landed is the wire shape. `dp` lives in the `exn` query/modifier section — the placement the branch requires — and is required on `apply` and `offer`: stage 2 of `IpexHandler.verify` rejects a message whose `q` lacks `dp`, whose `dp` is not a one-element outer list, or whose single element fails the triple check (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L419-425 @b8f60166b). The outer one-element list is deliberate future-proofing for [section 11](#dag-soup)'s multi-DAG case: "We currently only support one DAG (until Multi DAG), so exactly one entry is required" (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, _validSingleDagList, L223 @b8f60166b). The grammar checks each entry is a triple of "[schema SAID, DAG path, ACDC paths]", that the DAG path is either the root `"/"` or a prefix that "terminates at the far-node hop `\"_/\"` such as `\"/e/holder/_/\"`", and that the third element is a list of non-empty path strings. The builders refuse to construct a message without one, raising rather than returning false. So keripy at this pin agrees with the branch about where `dp` goes, what shape it has, and that the hop is spelled `_`. [code]

#### What did not land: enforcement {#dp-not-enforced}

What did not land is enforcement, and this is the distinction the whole tier story turns on. No verb checks a disclosed DAG against its `dp` plan. `dp` is validated for `apply` and `offer` only; `grant` — the verb that actually discloses — never reads it. A grant's stage-5 check walks the disclosed graph for internal closure and evaluates edge semantics over it, both of which are about the DAG's own shape, not about whether it matches what was asked for. A grant disclosing more than the agreed paths, or less, is accepted by this code. Nor is the machinery that would produce a targeted partial wired up: `Compactor.compact(paths=…)` exists and is tested, but the `paths=` form has no caller anywhere in `src/`, and nothing in the tree translates a `dp` path into a `Compactor` path — they are not obviously the same language, and no code reconciles them. `dp` is negotiated, not enforced. A reader who takes "`dp` is implemented" to mean a verifier will catch a mismatched disclosure would be wrong, and would be wrong about the one property that would make the negotiation load-bearing. [code]

## 5. Edges, operators, and edge groups {#edges}

The presentation model leans on the edge layer in two places: `I2I` closes the bespoke recipe ([section 3](#bespoke-origin)), and every proposal to signal multiple endorsement in the ACDC rather than in the exchange message is a proposal for a new edge-group operator ([section 9](#multiple-endorsement)).

### Block discrimination [spec] {#block-discrimination}

"An Edge MUST contain a node, `n` field. An Edge-group MUST NOT have a node, `n` field" (ACDC spec > Edge Section > Block Types, L1058 @f0bd097de). The Edge Section is itself the top-level edge-group. Edge-groups nest to arbitrary depth. Reserved labels are `[d, u, o, w]` for a group and `[d, u, n, s, o, w]` for an edge.

#### Unary operators: four are [spec], the fifth is [spec-next] {#unary-operators}

On `main` @`f0bd097de` the table is `I2I` (near issuer MUST be far issuee — the delegation link, and the default for a targeted far node), `NI2I` (relaxes it; default for untargeted), `DI2I` (near issuer MUST be far issuee or a delegated AID of far issuee) and `NOT` (inverts far-node validity) (ACDC spec > Edge Section > Edge > Operator, `o` field, L1190-1195 @f0bd097de). [spec] `v1.1` @`2362e48a1` adds a fifth row, `E1E`: near issuee MUST equal far issuee, "an identity relation on the two ACDCs' Issuees" that "places no constraint on either ACDC's Issuer" (ACDC spec > Edge Section > Edge > Operator, `o` field, L1206 @2362e48a1). Both ends MUST be targeted. Its motivating case is a core identity credential and a separate entitlement credential issued to the same subject by different issuers — a relation `I2I` cannot express, since `I2I` would require the entitlement's issuer to be the core credential's issuee. `E1E` occurs zero times on `main`, so it is [spec-next], and it also joins the default-inference gate on that branch while never being a default itself. `o` may be a list, and "When multiple unary Operators appear in the list, and there is a conflict between Operators, the latest Operator among the conflicting Operators in the list takes precedence" — that rule is [spec], on both branches.

#### The empty-operator default is to change in v1.1 {#default-change}

Added 2026-10-15. Both branches append `I2I` to an operator-less edge whose far node is targeted and `NI2I` to one whose far node is untargeted (ACDC spec > Edge Section > Edge > Operator, `o` field, L1209-1213 @2362e48a1). That rule is decided to change. Sam proposed it in ACDC issue #205: "we could change the default in v1.1 to be 'N121` for an empty unary operator list", where "N121" is a slip for `NI2I` (kswg-acdc-specification issue #205, body of 2026-08-11, R-030, https://github.com/trustoverip/kswg-acdc-specification/issues/205). The next day he recorded the decision: "Based on the discussion in the maintainer call yesterday. The change of default is to land in v1.1 of the ACDC spec." Because vLEIs in production rely on the `I2I` default, "the v1.1 code must have a version switch that changes the default for v1.1 ACDCs but not for prior versions of the ACDC protocol." (issue #205, comment of 2026-08-12, R-030, https://github.com/trustoverip/kswg-acdc-specification/issues/205#issuecomment-5268897690). This is [proposal] with a maintainer-call decision behind it, and it is not carried: `v1.1` at `2362e48a1` keeps the `I2I`-if-targeted rule, and issue #205 is open. It changes the meaning of every operator-less edge in a v1.1 ACDC, and it reverses the position #1556 took in July against changing the global default ([section 9](#proposal-1556)). Once landed with the switch Sam describes, it would move the spec toward `acdc/ipexing.py`'s no-default behavior and away from `vdr/verifying.py`'s inferred `I2I` ([section 5](#three-axes)), for v1.1 ACDCs only (`raw/21` T-05).

#### M-ary operators {#mary-operators}

M-ary operators [spec] on an edge-group: `AND` (default), `OR`, `NAND`, `NOR`, `AVG`, `WAVG`. "When the Operator, `o`, field is missing in an Edge-group block, the default value for the Operator, `o`, field MUST be the `AND` Operator" (ACDC spec > Edge Section > Edge-group > Operator, `o` field, L1110 @f0bd097de; the m-ary table is unchanged between the branches).

#### No registry for operator tokens {#operator-namespace}

The operator-token namespace has no registry, and two specs are now writing into it. The Dossier spec @`6037adfb9` defines four further m-ary edge-group operators for joint issuance — `MxN`, `RMxN`, `MxQ`, `RMxQ` — "placed in the operator field (`o`) of an edge group within the dossier's edges block, following ACDC operator conventions" (Dossier spec > Joint Issuance > Threshold Operators, L364-372 @6037adfb9). They do not collide with the ACDC set or with the proposed `ME`, but nothing prevents a collision, and #1555 itself flags the risk in passing ("need to confirm no collisions"). Any argument that pivots away from edge operators toward exchange-message fields ([section 11](#dag-soup)) should weigh this: the operator namespace is a shared, unmanaged resource across at least two specifications.

#### keripy's operator enforcement {#keripy-operators}

[code] keripy recognizes five operators and enforces two of them, and the enforcement picture is mixed rather than empty. `Verifier.UnaryOps = ('I2I', 'NI2I', 'DI2I', 'E1E', 'NOT')` (keripy vdr/verifying.py, Verifier, L41 @b8f60166b). `I2I` and `E1E` are enforced — `I2I` as plain qb64 string equality of near-issuer against far-issuee, `E1E` as equality of the two issuees, with the code drawing the distinction explicitly: unlike `I2I`, `E1E` "says nothing about the issuer, so the common SEDI case -- both credentials issued by a third party to the same subject" is valid, which is exactly what `I2I` rejects. `NI2I` is a no-op binding check by construction — it requires the far node to exist and be in issued state, and constrains nothing about issuer or issuee. `DI2I` and `NOT` fail closed deliberately, raising `ValidationError` rather than escrowing, and the reason is written into the source: "DI2I and NOT are recognized but unimplemented: they are listed so they fail closed diagnosably instead of being dropped and silently defaulting" (keripy vdr/verifying.py, Verifier, L38-39 @b8f60166b), because "escrowing would promise a retry that can never succeed." A reviewer must not simplify either branch into a missing-chain error or into a silent skip. The edge `s` constraint is now enforced too, with "satisfy" rather than "equal" semantics: a direct SAID match short-circuits, otherwise the far node is validated against the edge's schema, and an uncached schema escrows and queries rather than failing.

#### A genuine inconsistency between two modules, which neither acknowledges {#module-inconsistency}

`vdr/verifying.py` drops an unrecognized operator token from a list-valued `o` and then applies the default rule; `acdc/ipexing.py` treats an unrecognized `o` as malformed and fails closed. The same edge can therefore pass credential processing and fail IPEX grant verification, or the reverse, depending only on which code path sees it. Nothing in either file mentions the other, and neither branch of the ACDC spec says what a validator should do with an operator token it does not recognize. This is the sharpest open code question in the chapter's area and is carried in [section 14](#open-questions).

#### Widened 2026-10-03: three axes of disagreement {#three-axes}

Widened 2026-10-03: at `80f77b73b` the two modules disagree on three axes, not one. [code] (raw/09 section 7e, section 9, section 11 item 1)

## 6. Two IPEX state machines, and only one of them runs {#two-ipex}

### New 2026-09-15 {#two-ipex-new}

Every [code] claim about IPEX in this chapter now needs to say which IPEX, because keripy at `b8f60166b` contains two implementations of the protocol and they have begun to diverge. This section exists so that the rest of the chapter can cite one or the other without re-explaining the split.

#### V1, the handler keripy wires {#ipex-v1}

V1, `src/keri/vc/protocoling.py`, is the one keripy actually wires. Its handler is what the CLI loads. Its state machine is a legal-predecessor table: `offer` ← `apply`, `agree` ← `offer`, `grant` ← `agree`, `admit` ← `grant`, and `spurn` ← any of the four. `apply` may only open an exchange; `admit`, `agree` and `spurn` may never open one; `offer` and `grant` may open one or must answer a legal predecessor; the prior message must exist; and a second response to the same prior is rejected outright. An unmatched route returns false, so the machine fails closed. All of these claims were re-read at `b8f60166b` and are still true — they are rescoped, not superseded. What has changed is their scope: they describe the V1 handler, which is now one of two. [code]

#### V2, library and tests only {#ipex-v2}

V2, `src/keri/acdc/ipexing.py`, is library and tests only. `loadHandlers` has no caller anywhere in `src/keri` at this pin. The module is exported and exercised by tests, and it more than doubled in size in the two weeks before this pass, but nothing any shipped keripy command does goes through it. Claims sourced from it are [code] about code that exists in the reference implementation, not about behavior the reference implementation performs. Do not upgrade them past that, and do not cite V2 behavior as what "keripy does" without the qualifier.

#### What IPEX preserves and what it rejects {#defer-vs-refuse}

"IPEX rejects what it preserves" needs qualifying rather than cutting. The chapter has used that phrase for the shape of the KRAM gap in [section 8](#freshness-kram), and at `b8f60166b` the accurate form is sharper and is a statement about the handler generally: IPEX preserves what it defers on, and drops what it refuses. The two outcomes are not variants of one rejection; they are a deferral and a refusal, and only the deferral preserves anything. ## 7. Authentication factors: the second problem {#authentication-factors}

The DAG solves data integrity and authority structure. It does not, by itself, establish that the party sending the presentation controls anything at the moment of sending. Discussion #1613, "Authentication Factors in IPEX," is the systematic treatment. All of it is [proposal]; none of it is in any spec.

### Two purposes, never conflated {#two-purposes}

Every ACDC in a granted DAG needs an Issuer authentication factor — proof it was authentically issued. The `grant` needs a Grantor authentication factor — fresh proof of control by whoever is presenting. "For a given ACDC, a Grantor may be the Issuee, Issuer, both, or neither."

#### Three factor types, strictly ranked {#factor-ranking}

Three factor types, strictly ranked: registry (TEL) anchor, KEL anchor, bare signature. The ranking is not stylistic — registry and KEL anchors "enable perpetual verifiability and detectability of impersonation fraud. Whereas a bare signature does not," and a registry anchor additionally carries lifecycle state. The composition rule is absorption: "when multiple authentication factors are either required or provided for the same purpose, the highest-preference factor is used, and the others are ignored."

#### Bare signatures are ephemeral {#bare-signatures}

Bare signatures are ephemeral in a strong sense. "as soon as the Issuer rotates its keystate, any ACDCs it issued using a bare signature as the Issuer authentication factor with a prior keystate become unverifiable and must be reissued. This is only useful for truly temporary use (ephemeral) ACDCs." This is worth flagging against [section 3](#bespoke-origin)'s claim that a bespoke origin "does not need to be anchored or use a registry": a registryless, signature-only bespoke origin is disposable by construction, which is fine for a one-time recipe and is not fine if anyone later needs to prove what was presented.

#### The issuer factor for every disclosed node {#issuer-factor-every-node}

New 2026-10-03: the issuer factor is now [code] for every disclosed node, in the V2 module. At `b8f60166b` the V2 handler checked issuer authentication only for registry-backed nodes, and a node without a top-level `rd` passed that stage unconditionally. At `80f77b73b` `_verifyIssuerAuthGraph` requires "one valid issuer authentication factor per node" (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyIssuerAuthGraph, L1132 @80f77b73b), and `_verifyIssuerAuthNode` (L1139) selects exactly one of three, in #1613's order and without falling through once one is chosen: the issuer registry when a top-level `rd` is present (L1142-1152); a seal anchor in the issuer's KEL (L1159-1201), where "An invalid anchor must not silently downgrade the ACDC to signature authentication." (L1199-1200); or signatures (L1203-1263), kept only if they "already name the current state" (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyIssuerAuthNode, L1237-1240 @80f77b73b). That last restriction is the code's version of the ephemerality #1613 describes: an ACDC signed under issuer keys that have since rotated, with no registry and no anchor, should no longer authenticate in a V2 grant. That consequence is raw/09's inference from the code, not behavior anyone has run, and the code states the filter without stating the reason ([section 14](#open-questions)). [code] (raw/09 section 7e; V2-only per [section 6](#two-ipex))

#### Vocabulary {#ipex-vocabulary}

The Discloser sends `offer` and `grant` (Offerer, Grantor); the Disclosee sends `apply`, `agree`, `admit` (Applicant, Agreent, Admittant). "In common parlance, a Grantor is a Presentor, and the grant is a presentation." Where several AIDs endorse one grant, "there is a set of Grantors that each must be uniquely authenticated."

#### The receiver AID is never empty {#receiver-aid}

Added 2026-10-15. Every IPEX message names both parties. Asked why a draft left `ri` empty on several verbs, Sam answered: "The `ri` field is meant to carry the receiver AID." The negotiating AIDs are a correlation point by construction, and he argues that is the price of a binding contract rather than a leak: the verifier can correlate on the presenter's negotiating AID but "can't prove it is the same as the Issuee until the Grant", and "Proof of participation is not the same as correlation." (keripy discussion #1542, comment of 2026-07-23, R-054, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1542#discussioncomment-17755467). Contract law wants "a clear intent to engage in legal relations", which needs identity, so graduated disclosure governs the ACDC content and not the pair of AIDs negotiating over it. [proposal] keripy's V2 module already refuses a reply whose prior names no receiver (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyReplyChain, L766-768 @80f77b73b). [code]

## 8. Freshness: KRAM, and what rests on it {#freshness-kram}

Everything about multiply-endorsed presentation rests on KRAM, so its status matters more than any other single fact in this chapter.

> This section is an audit of KRAM's status as it bears on presentation, not an account of what KRAM is. For the mechanism itself — the problem it solves, simple vs full KRAM, the monotonic timeliness cache, the v0.7.6 redesign, and its relationship to BADA-RUN — see [chapter 09](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md), which is built from Sam's whitepaper rather than from #1613's paraphrase of it. Two claims below are corrected there and are marked in place.

### The problem {#replay-problem}

A signature is a bearer token: "Any holder of both the document and its signature can replay them, and the signature will verify" (#1613). That phrasing is #1613's, and [proposal]. The KERI spec states the general form and prefers non-interactive mitigation — "Because non-interactive mitigations are asynchronous, however, they do not have the latency and scalability limitations of interactive mitigations and are therefore preferred" (KERI spec > Annex A > BADA (Best-Available-Data-Acceptance) Policy > Security Issues, L2878 @be618e7dc). [spec] for the problem and the preference; the spec says it about data-at-rest under BADA, not about presentations, so applying it here is this chapter's step. (Retiered 2026-10-15: this heading carried an ad hoc marker, `[N-adjacent]`, now said in words; the locator previously named a "Replay attack" heading that does not exist at that pin.)

#### The proposed mechanism [proposal] {#kram-mechanism}

As #1613 describes it, KRAM (KERI Request Authentication Mechanism) uses "a message SAID `d` field, a sender AID `i` field, a receiver AID `ri` field, a salty nonce `u` field, and a datetime stamp relative to the receiver's clock `dt` field." Corrected — the nonce is not part of KRAM. The whitepaper builds uniqueness from monotonic ordering of receiver-clock datetimes, and argues at length that nonce-based mechanisms are what KRAM replaces; keripy reads `msg.stamp` and never touches `u`. See [chapter 09, section 4](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md#redesign) and [chapter 09, section 8](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md#load-bearing). Its properties are otherwise as stated: one play or none, within a window measured on the receiver's clock, so "The sender cannot therefore lie about the datetime in the message." A same-message replay inside the window is either ignored or, for a multisig sender, contributes its signature to an escrow — "This elegantly solves the multi-sig problem without requiring a pre-protocol to collect signatures. The receiver's KRAM escrow does the signature collection."

#### Three findings about KRAM's actual status, each verified {#kram-status-findings}

KRAM is not in the KERI specification. A search for "KRAM" across KERI spec `main` @`be618e7dc` returns nothing in `spec/spec-body.md`. The spec discusses replay attacks and BADA-RUN at length and never names this mechanism. So the freshness guarantee the entire multiply-endorsed design depends on is, at the specification layer, absent. (One near-miss, found later: the rendered v1 artifact carries a glossary cross-reference imported from `trustoverip/kerisuite-glossary`, defining simple KRAM in a single sentence. It is not spec text, and it describes the variant that cannot support multisig — see [chapter 09, section 5](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md#normative-status).)

The `u` field KRAM is described as using does not exist on an `exn`. This puzzle probably dissolves — `u` is #1613's paraphrase, not the whitepaper's mechanism, per the correction above. The observation about message shapes stands on its own terms and is worth keeping, so it is recorded unchanged below. The KERI spec fixes both message shapes exactly: `xip` is `[v, t, d, u, i, ri, dt, r, q, a]` and `exn` is `[v, t, d, i, ri, x, p, dt, r, q, a]`, and for both, "All are REQUIRED. No other top-level fields are allowed (MUST NOT appear)" (KERI spec > Routed Messages > Exchange Transaction Inception Message Body, L1154 @be618e7dc; KERI spec > Routed Messages > Exchange Message Body, L1197 @be618e7dc). `u` is present on `xip` and absent on `exn`; the spec says `u` "appears in exchange transaction inception messages to ensure that the associated transaction ID is also universally unique" (KERI spec > Routed Messages > Reserved field labels in routed messages > UUID Salty Nonce field, L963 @be618e7dc). #1613 describes KRAM as using `u` and as applying "to each `xip` or `exn` individually." For an `exn` the uniqueness comes from `x` ⧺ `p` chaining instead. This may be a slip in the prose rather than a design gap, but it is not currently reconcilable as written.

keripy is further along than the July reading suggested, and the gap has moved. [code] `Kramer` (`src/keri/core/kraming.py`) is real, exn-aware — its cache-type cascade is keyed by message ilk and route, `_fetchCacheType(msgType, route)` with "`exn`" named explicitly (keripy core/kraming.py, Kramer._fetchCacheType, L282 @4df8e4a86) — and it deliberately distinguishes sender from non-sender attachments. `_normalizeSenderSeals` "leaves only non-sender triples in ssts for non-auth forwarding / escrow" (keripy core/kraming.py, Kramer._normalizeSenderSeals, L386 @4df8e4a86), and `_normalizeCurrentSenderTsgs` notes that "downstream exn/rpy handling still needs the[m]" (keripy core/kraming.py, Kramer._normalizeCurrentSenderTsgs, L448 @4df8e4a86). #### Amended 2026-09-15: the evidence policy {#evidence-policy}

#### Tethering [proposal] {#tethering}

Given multi-endorsement-aware KRAM, #1613 defines a derived property. The `grant` carries the origin SAID in an origin `o` field, and because the origin commits to every ACDC in its DAG, "when any Grantor AID appears anywhere in the DAG of ACDCs originating at the origin ACDC, then we say that that ACDC is *tethered* to the `grant`." Tethering means only that: fresh proof of control over that AID, wherever it appears. "Tethering implies no other relationship besides fresh (timely) proof-of-control over an AID so tethered." Its motivating case is entitlement replay — Bob's movie coupon, where Cal must know Bob and not the thief Ian is presenting.

#### A vocabulary trap in the code, as of `80f77b73b` {#tethered-set}

The V2 IPEX module has a set called `tethered`, and it is not #1613's tethering. It holds the Issuees a presentation registry authenticated, and it is consumed only by the `ax` anchoring rule ([section 10](#anchoring)). Nothing in the code requires an Issuee appearing in the DAG to be a Grantor, which is what #1613's tethering would check (raw/16 section 7.3, section 7.7). So tethering as #1613 defines it remains unimplemented, and a reader grepping for the word will find something else. [code]

Note that the origin `o` field is [proposal] and appears in no spec. Note also that `o` is already the reserved label for Operator throughout the ACDC edge and edge-group blocks. The two live in different namespaces — an `exn` attribute section versus an ACDC edge block — so this is an overload rather than a collision, but it is an overload in a protocol family where `o` otherwise means exactly one thing.

## 9. Multiply endorsed presentation: the proposal that moved {#multiple-endorsement}

This is the clearest case in the corpus of a design that was superseded, and reading the superseded version as current is the most likely way to be wrong about it.

### #1555 (2026-07-29) [proposal] {#proposal-1555}

Superseded in large part by #1613 below, though not its `ME` operator. (Retiered 2026-10-15: the heading carried an ad hoc marker, `[P, superseded]`, now said in words.) Non-sender AIDs attach signature groups (`-X`/`-Y` for transferable, and seal-source equivalents) to a single `grant`; KRAM's timeliness extends to them. The signal that endorsement is required should live in the ACDC, not the `exn`, because "An exchange message is relatively simple in comparison to an ACDC. There is no normative cryptographic commitment in an exchange message to a given schema." The mechanism: a new M-ary edge-group operator, `ME`, on a bespoke origin, meaning each far ACDC in the group must be endorsed by its issuee via signatures attached to the grant. Multi-DAG presentation explicitly out of scope.

#### #1556 (2026-07-29) [proposal] {#proposal-1556}

The companion. The global `I2I` default is a backward-compatibility accommodation for pre-operator vLEIs, and it fits the new non-delegative operators badly — an `ME` group would need explicit `NI2I` on every edge. Proposal: make unary defaults a function of the enclosing m-ary group operator (`ME` group → `NI2I` default), and add an `IAND` group whose edges default to `E1E`. Not changing the global default, which "might break vLEIs in the wild." Superseded in part 2026-10-15: two weeks later Sam proposed changing the global default after all, and a maintainer call decided that it becomes `NI2I` for v1.1 ACDCs behind a version switch, which answers the vLEI concern by leaving older ACDCs on `I2I` ([section 5](#default-change)). The group-scoped defaults and `IAND` are not withdrawn, but the problem they were built around is now proposed to be solved globally.

#### #1613 (2026-08-12, revised to v1.5 on 2026-08-18) [proposal] {#proposal-1613}

The current proposal. (Retiered 2026-10-15: the heading carried an ad hoc marker, `[P, current]`, now said in words.) Opens by superseding its predecessor: "As of version 1.1, this discussion supersedes much of the 'Multiply Endorsed' discussion... This discussion solves for the proxy negotiator for another AID use case without needing a new edge operator." What it does not supersede is `ME` itself — "It does not supersede the proposal for a new edge operator" — and it explicitly does not solve "the more generic use cases of a proxy negotiator for multiple other AID nor the more generic case where multiple simultaneous AIDs are negotiating in concert."

#### The motivating case: correlation, not convenience {#proxy-motivation}

The motivating case is correlation, not convenience. "a given controller may use one AID it controls as a proxy to negotiate on behalf of another AID it controls so that this other AID is not disclosed until after contractual protection is in place... Only the first AID needs to participate in the failed negotiation and is the only one exposed by it." Sam (proxy negotiator) fronts for Bob (coupon issuee); Bob's AID appears for the first time in the `grant`.

#### The processing rule [proposal] {#endorsement-processing}

KRAM validates sender-AID groups and passes non-sender groups through; "to support multiply endorsed IPEX, the IPEX processor MUST validate all non-sender endorsements after KRAM." Signatures must verify against the endorser's current key state and satisfy its threshold; seal-source references must be found in the endorser's KEL against the message SAID; where both are supplied, "the seal source reference is preferred."

#### One limitation stated plainly, and it is the sharp edge {#multisig-endorser-limit}

A non-sender multisig endorser gets no help from KRAM's escrow-collects-signatures trick, "therefore, the Grantor must employ some pre-protocol to collect a threshold-satisfying set of signatures and then attach them to the grant." The offered mitigation is that a proxy and its principal are the same controller and can be given matching multisig infrastructure and threshold, in which case collection happens "serendipitously." That is a real constraint on deployment topology dressed as a coincidence.

#### The `grant` only {#grant-only}

Multiple endorsement attaches to the `grant` and to nothing else — "A valid IPEX could consist of only a `grant` message. This means that multiple endorsements cannot depend on some combined effect of multiple messages."

#### New 2026-10-03: what keripy does with a multiply-endorsed grant, and the gap in it [code] {#keripy-endorsement}

At `80f77b73b`, in the V2 module only ([section 6](#two-ipex)), the generator supports the design. Commit `790797bcd` ("add multiply endorse support, add presentation registry support") gave `grant` two new parameters: endorsers are "Additional local habitats that sign the same outer exchange as non-sender grantors (e.g proxy AIDs)." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, _sign, L291-292 @80f77b73b), and anchorers are "Additional local transferable habitats whose KELs seal the same outer exchange." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, _sign, L293-294 @80f77b73b), attached as explicit source triples naming the anchoring AID. On the receiving side, the processing rule above is followed as far as filtering goes: non-sender evidence is validated after KRAM, against the endorser's current establishment state, and a valid seal from an AID is preferred to that AID's signatures — the code's version of "the seal source reference is preferred" from the processing rule above. Accepted endorsements are persisted and indexed.

#### Endorsement signatures do not decide validity {#signatures-discarded}

But endorsement signatures do not decide whether a grant is valid. `IpexHandler.verify` unpacks the accepted evidence as `_, _, extraSeals = evidence` (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L605 @80f77b73b) and discards the signature groups. Only the source seals are used, and only toward the anchoring requirement of [section 10](#anchoring). Under the default policy, then, an endorsement signature on a grant is recorded and has no effect on acceptance; an endorsement seal can satisfy `ax`. The #1613 obligation that each Grantor's endorsement verify and satisfy its threshold therefore has no consumer in the verdict. Whether non-sender signature groups are threshold-checked at all is unverified (raw/16 section 7.3 did not read `verifyAttachments`), and whichever way that falls, the result is not used. The limitation stated above — that a non-sender multisig endorser needs a signature-collection pre-protocol — is moot in the code for the same reason, because the signatures such a pre-protocol would collect are not counted. Whether discarding them is the intended endpoint or a stage in progress is not said anywhere in the tree ([section 14](#open-questions)). (raw/09 section 7e, section 10 item 9)

#### Live co-presence versus durable consent {#copresence-vs-consent}

`ME`-style endorsement requires every issuee online and signing at presentation time. It does not cover presentation on behalf of an offline party — a guardian for a dependent, an agent for a traveller. That case closes under plain `I2I` with a consent ACDC in the middle, and does so today at v2 with no new operator: `bespoke(issuer=A, issuee=V) --I2I--> consent(issuer=B, issuee=A) --I2I--> credential(issuer=HA, issuee=B)`. Both links satisfy `I2I`, and A cannot manufacture the bridge because the middle link demands B be the issuer of the consent. These are complementary halves — live freshness versus durable delegation bounded by revocation — and conflating them is easy. (Established by measurement, keripy v2, 2026-07-29; see #1555's comment stream.)

## 10. Anchoring: the `ax` field, presentation registries, origin-AID anchoring {#anchoring}

### Mostly [proposal], from #1613, but no longer all of it {#anchoring-tiers}

The section was written when every construct in it was a proposal. At keripy `b8f60166b` one of the three legs is [code], and saying which one is the point: direct KEL anchoring of a truthy `ax` is implemented, and presentation-registry anchoring is not. The sub-claims are marked individually below.

#### Superseded 2026-10-03 {#anchoring-superseded}

At keripy `80f77b73b` all three legs are [code] in the V2 module: direct KEL anchoring, presentation-registry anchoring, and #1613's origin-AID fallback rule, under which the origin's Issuee or Issuer may anchor in place of the sender. The 2026-09-15 sentence above is kept as the record of where the code stood two weeks earlier. Every one of these remains absent from both ACDC spec branches, and every one is subject to [section 6](#two-ipex)'s qualifier: the module that implements them has no caller in `src/keri`.

#### The gap `ax` fills {#ax-gap}

KRAM proves timeliness but "gives neither party a way to signal to the IPEX that the relevant messages MUST be perpetually verifiable and hence anchored. Anchoring via KRAM is solely determined by the set of endorsers at the time the message is transmitted."

#### The field {#ax-field}

`ax` is a boolean in the attribute `a` section of `apply`, `offer`, and `grant`; truthy only if present and `True`. An IPEX may begin with any of those three, so any of them may carry it. Satisfying it means the Grantor anchors the `grant` and the Applicant anchors `agree` and `admit`, with an exception where a `grant` arrives without an enabling `agree`.

Note the placement asymmetry against [section 4](#disclosure-paths): `dp` is in `q` on the forward branch and never in a `grant`; `ax` and the origin `o` are proposed for `a` and do appear in a `grant`. Any proposal that amends all three uniformly has to account for this.

#### `ax` in keripy {#ax-keripy}

`ax` is now [code], as a negotiated bit with a real anchor check behind it. At `b8f60166b` the V2 IPEX handler treats anchoring as negotiated state that a reply may not silently change: for `agree`, `grant` and `admit`, "Binding replies must exactly preserve the negotiated state," and an `offer` "may initiate anchoring, but may not drop it." That one-sidedness is an invariant a reviewer must not relax into a symmetric check. Where anchoring is required, the handler demands a real seal in the sender's own KEL: a source-seal couple naming an event at or after the sender's last establishment event, matching that event's SAID at the same sequence number, required to be an interaction event beyond it, and containing a seal whose digest is this exn's SAID. Missing KEL evidence raises a retryable missing-key-state error rather than a rejection — a deferral, in [section 6](#two-ipex)'s terms, not a refusal. [code]

#### Narrowed 2026-10-03 {#ax-narrowed}

Narrowed 2026-10-03: at `80f77b73b` the sender-KEL requirement applies to `agree` and `admit` only. The negotiation invariants are unchanged (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L561-568 @80f77b73b), and the seal checks moved into `_verifySourceAnchor` (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifySourceAnchor, L656-703 @80f77b73b) unchanged in substance. But "Agree and Admit satisfy anchoring directly through their sender." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L573 @80f77b73b), and a `grant` no longer must: "A truthy ax requires one permitted Grant anchorer." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L620 @80f77b73b). The verifier collects every AID that anchored the grant — the sender if its own seal verifies, every non-sender whose source seal verifies against its own KEL, and every Issuee a presentation registry authenticated — and accepts if any of them is one of three: `candidates = (serder.pre, origin.iseaid, origin.israid)` (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L648 @80f77b73b), the sender and the Issuee and Issuer of the origin named by `a.o[0]`. The comment draws the boundary as policy: "Default IPEX policy deliberately stops at these three AIDs; credential-specific policy may impose a narrower requirement." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L645-646 @80f77b73b). So a grant with `ax` true can now be valid with no sender anchor at all, provided the origin's Issuee or Issuer sealed it. The generator side matches: `grant(..., anchorers=[])` suppresses the default sender anchor and "leaves anchoring to a presentation registry." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, grant, L1948-1950 @80f77b73b). [code] (raw/09 section 7e; raw/16 section 2.5)

#### One-item lists on the wire {#one-item-lists}

And `o` and `ax` are one-item lists on the wire, which settles a conflict raised on the discussion. `_validSingleDagList` rejects any non-list and requires exactly one entry, with the source saying why: "We currently only support one DAG (until Multi DAG), so exactly one entry is required." On 2026-09-01 dhh1128 flagged on #1627 that keripy had merged `o` as a scalar string while the validator rejected lists outright, which contradicted #1627's preferred list form ([section 11](#dag-soup)). The code went the list way within ten days. That is worth noticing as a fact about this design conversation's tempo: a conflict between a discussion's preferred design and the implementation was resolved, in the discussion's favour, by the implementation rather than by the discussion. [code]

#### An origin `o` on the `offer` too {#offer-origin}

Added 2026-10-15. Sam wants the `offer` to carry an origin as well: "The offer message also needs to have an origin `o` field to replace the ACDC field." (keripy PR #1629, review of 2026-08-20, R-053, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/pull/1629#pullrequestreview-4987948288). Its value is "just the SAID of the Metadata ACDC DAG origin. The actual metadata ACDCs are attached in a substream." (keripy PR #1629, review comment of 2026-08-21, R-053, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/pull/1629#discussion_r3832392219). The reason is the agreement step: the metadata ACDC discloses the rule section and withholds the attributes, so "the only way for the agreent to know what it is agreeing to is for it to see the metadata ACDC DAG". So an `offer` looks structurally like the `grant` it anticipates. Neither ACDC branch has an `o` on either message. [proposal] This bears on [question 4](#q-dag-ordering): if the `offer` carries an origin, one message before the `grant` names a DAG, though the `apply` still does not.

#### The third leg, as of 2026-09-15 {#third-leg}

The third leg did not move, and chapter 10 owns it. #1618 frames presentation authentication as three factors — a signed grant, a grant anchored in an Issuee-controlled presentation registry, and a grant anchored directly in a KEL without one. Leg 3 is the one that just became [code]; leg 2 is not implemented at all, and the merged PR that added the anchoring work says so of itself, stating that presentation-registry anchoring "will come in a follow up PR." The three legs have moved apart, and the one that moved is the one needing no registry, no Issuee cooperation and no new infrastructure — which is what cost predicts and is not evidence about the design's merits either way. See [chapter 10](10-presentation-registries.md) for the registry leg, its three-part signal, the self-issuance hole in its fraud protection, and a check in the registry layer that a presentation registry would fail by construction. This chapter does not restate that material.

#### Superseded 2026-10-03: the third leg moved {#third-leg-moved}

Superseded 2026-10-03 — the third leg moved within three weeks. The paragraph above describes `b8f60166b`. At `80f77b73b` the verifier side of presentation-registry authentication is built, on `grant` only, in the V2 module: "Verify every presentation registry declared across the DAG." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L609 @80f77b73b). Every registry an Issuer declares in a disclosed node must bind this grant's SAID, as of KRAM's acceptance time, with a KEL source reference from the Issuee; any failure rejects the grant, and the Issuees so authenticated count as anchorers for `ax` above. The registry-layer check that a presentation registry "would fail by construction" was scoped rather than removed, so the Issuer-equality test now applies only to an Issuer registry (raw/16 section 7.4). Discovery of a foreign registry is still not built: the verifier must already hold the Issuee's TEL (raw/16 section 7.8). The cost-predicts-order observation above still describes the order the legs arrived in, but it no longer describes a gap. All of this is [code] at `80f77b73b` and is recorded in full in `raw/16` section 7 and [chapter 10](10-presentation-registries.md); it is summarized here only because it changes what satisfies `ax`.

#### Who must act in a presentation {#who-must-act}

One part of it changes who must act in a presentation, and chapter 10 owns the argument. Between 2026-09-21 and 2026-09-25 the code required a presentation registry's Issuee also to be a sender, endorser or anchorer of the grant — "A registry supplements, but never replaces, a Grant endorsement." — and commit `b7c170f93` ("remove grantor requisite") deleted that check before the work merged. At `80f77b73b` a presentation-registry binding authenticates its Issuee on its own: "The declaration is the issuer's signal that this Issuee must authenticate the grant through this node-local registry." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyPresentationAuthGraph, L1516-1517 @80f77b73b). #1613 says the opposite for every presentation-registry-anchored grant (not only the proxy case — scope corrected 2026-10-03 (verification) against the #1613 snapshot), that the grant "must be endorsed by each Grantor" and is therefore "always double-authenticated". In effect the code lets a registry anchor make the Issuee a Grantor without an endorsement. raw/16 judges that coherent, since the anchor is a KEL-anchored commitment to the exact grant SAID, and a design change that #1613 does not record. [code] against [proposal] (raw/09 section 7e; raw/16 section 2.6)

#### Why anchor at all {#why-anchor}

Anchoring `agree` gives the Discloser perpetually verifiable proof of agreement before disclosure; anchoring `grant` gives the Disclosee proof of "what was granted and, as importantly, what was not granted"; anchoring `admit` "removes plausible deniability regarding the Disclosee's knowledge of the disclosed information and could trigger safe harbor protections."

#### The contractual logic: agree binds, admit removes the easy defense {#contract-logic}

Added 2026-10-15. Sam's July comments on the CLC worked example give the contract reasoning that #1613's anchoring rules rest on. The `agree` is the binding act, and a missing `admit` does not undo it: "An agree is legally binding not sending an admit does not convey backing out." The `admit` exists for enforcement: "The admit is a corner case that enables easy enforcement by removing plausible deniablity." To make a disclosee send one, the grantor's terms can carry an escape clause: "The grantor gets an escape clause unless the admitter verifiably admits." (keripy PR #1505, comment of 2026-07-17, R-049, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/pull/1505#issuecomment-4997904242). He ties it to the duty of data loyalty: "So CLC and DDL are mutually reinforcing." [proposal]

Multiple anchors in one exchange are contingencies, not redundancy. Because the messages of an exchange are hash-chained, "anchoring the tail also anchors the whole transation including the head", and "Multiple anchors are not meant to anchor multiple transactions but as continencies for the transaction to proceed." (keripy PR #1505, comment of 2026-07-16, R-050, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/pull/1505#issuecomment-4996894599; "continencies" means contingencies). The sequence of anchors is what lets two peers settle without a trusted third party: the discloser wants an anchored `agree` before it grants, and the disclosee wants an anchored `grant` before it admits. In his words, "So the Offer/Agree/Grant/Admit removes plausible deniability which is the bane of peer-to-peer interactions." [proposal]

#### Four tiers of IPEX {#four-tiers}

Added 2026-10-15. Before #1613, Sam laid out four tiers as a decision aid, in captions to four diagrams (keripy discussion #1512, comment of 2026-07-22, R-062, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1512#discussioncomment-17737728). The low-stakes, signed-only tier: "Only the granted ACDC is itself perpetually verifiable, Not the IPEX transaction". The same plus a presentation registry, which lets the presenter detect use of its compromised keys ([chapter 10](10-presentation-registries.md)). The high-stakes anchored tier: "The Agree, and Admit exchange messages are anchored by the Disclosee. The Grant is anchored by the Discloser." And the high-stakes tier with a presentation registry, in which the ACDC itself requires the grant to be anchored through the `rd` in its attribute section. The anchoring rules above are #1613's later and more detailed form of the third and fourth tiers; the taxonomy is the part #1613 does not restate. [proposal]

#### Presentation registries {#presentation-registries}

Summarized here because they bear on anchoring; treated in full in [chapter 10](10-presentation-registries.md), which is built from the concept's origin document — keripy discussion #1095, ACDC Issuee Usage Registry, ten months older than #1613 and filed under a different name, which is why earlier passes over this corpus missed it. A registry controlled by the Issuee, whose latest non-vacuous blinded state binds the `grant` SAID. Signalled by the issuer at issuance through a populated `rd` and `i` at the top level of the ACDC's attribute `a` section — note, not the ACDC's own top-level `rd`, which names the ACDC state registry.

Two benefits, and only one is a security property. Impersonation-fraud detection: because the registry's events anchor in the Grantor's KEL, the Grantor can see events it did not create, and "an imposter that merely compromises the Grantor's signing infrastructure can't avoid the requirement without also compromising the Issuer." This works only when the Issuer differs from the Grantor — for a self-issued ACDC an imposter with the signing keys simply issues one that requires nothing. Correlation resistance: an anchored grant whose SAID appears only inside a blinded registry event means "a correlator walking the Grantor's KEL would not be able to observe it," so the exchange can be perpetually verifiable without giving a KEL-scanning third party a join key between Grantor and Grantee.

#### Restricting `rd`-in-`a` to presentation registries {#rd-in-a}

#1613 floats restricting `rd`-in-`a` to presentation registries. The v1 spec also permits it as a hidden ACDC state registry, and #1613 judges that case "potentially dubious" and suggests forbidding it in v1.1. That question is live and touches the independent-registry bulk-issuance work directly enough to be worth watching.

#### Origin AID anchoring: the resolution {#origin-aid-anchoring}

With multiple Grantors, who must anchor? The complications are real: in a delegation chain, "Issuee AIDs that are not at the tail (leaf) of a delegation chain (tree) do not need a fresh proof-of-control"; and a Dossier-style evidence presentation may name an issuee wholly unrelated to the Discloser. Rather than enumerate, #1613 picks a rule: "either the sender must anchor the grant or the Issuee or Issuer of the origin ACDC of the grant must anchor the grant," checked in that order of priority. This is what lets the sender AID stay absent from the disclosed DAG entirely while the principal's anchor still satisfies the requirement. [proposal] Implemented 2026-10-03, with one difference. keripy at `80f77b73b` realizes this rule in the V2 module (see the `ax` paragraph above), but as membership in a set rather than a priority order — any one of the three suffices, so "in that order of priority" has no operational effect. raw/16 reads that as consistent with #1613's own next sentence. [code]

#### The layering {#anchoring-layering}

The layering is explicit and worth preserving in any critique: the anchoring rule "is a loose business logic requirement... enforced after the grant passes KRAM and IPEX multiple endorsement but before other business logic requirements." Cal's coupon rule — that Bob specifically must anchor — is EGF business logic riding above the protocol rule, not a substitute for it. A grant can pass KRAM, pass multiple endorsement, satisfy `ax`, and still be refused by the verifier's own policy, and #1613 treats that as correct.

## 11. DAG soup: the list form, and what remains open {#dag-soup}

#1627's only concrete proposal. [proposal]

### The argument for needing it at all {#why-dag-soup}

A bespoke origin converts a soup into a DAG, so why keep soup? "When the ingredients are sourced from different cupboards, i.e., each have different Issuee, then the presentation must be multiply endorsed." And the practical case: presenting several DAGs in parallel in one exchange beats a series of IPEXes, since "A series of IPEX presentations adds more failure modes, and if the business logic requires all to complete for processing to continue, now the parties to the exchange have to keep state across multiple IPEXes. This requires a meta IPEX protocol."

#### The pivot away from edge operators {#pivot-from-operators}

#1555 put the multi-endorsement signal in the ACDC. #1627 reconsiders, because anchoring joined the picture: "#1555 predates #1613 which largely superseded #1555... now a fully multiply endorsed exchange gets more complicated with respect to anchoring, as there would be more than one effective origin node to which the anchoring requirement... must be applied. Following down the path of using new edge operators would require defining yet another edge operator that signaled the anchoring requirement." Conclusion: "the complications of a combination of multiply-endorsed and multiply-anchored presentations may flip the trade-space away from using edge operators as the primary way to signal either or both."

#### The amendment, as it now stands {#list-form-amendment}

The body of #1627 was revised in place on 2026-08-25, after this chapter was first drafted, and now offers two options in this order. The field-map option — each of `dp`, `o`, `ax` may become "a field map, with a unique informative label for each DAG" — is presented as deferrable: "This proposal does not change the current definitions but allows a future option to replace the field definitions with a field map. This is possible because none of the current field values is a field map so the data type removes ambiguity." The list option makes `dp` "a list of a list of tuples," `o` "a list of SAIDs," and `ax` "a list of Booleans," with one element per DAG and exactly one element in the single-DAG case. It is presented as not deferrable — "The proposal requires the change now because it uses lists, and the `dp` field is already a list. So it would be ambiguous" — and it is the one marked "the preferred approach at the time of this writing." dhh1128 endorsed the list form on the discussion, 2026-08-25. [proposal] #1627's body has since been edited again, on 2026-09-11, and the list form remains its preferred option.

#### The list form in keripy {#list-form-keripy}

The list form is now also [code], which is the strongest evidence it has. keripy at `b8f60166b` carries `o` and `ax` as one-element lists on the wire and rejects anything that is not a list, with the single-DAG restriction stated as temporary "until Multi DAG" ([section 10](#anchoring)). `dp` is likewise an outer one-element list of path plans. So the wire shape the code enforces today is the list form's degenerate case, laid out so the multi-DAG generalization needs no type change. That does not make the proposal normative anywhere — it is absent from both spec branches — but it does mean the trade-space question below is no longer purely hypothetical: an implementation has committed to one side of it.

#### What the list form settles {#list-form-settles}

Identity by position rather than by a minted label. That removes the join-key problem the field-map form created, and it removes a graduated-disclosure leak that was specific to the word informative: a meaningful label (`employment`, `utah-sedi`) is semantic content about the evidence, written in the clear in an `apply` or `offer`, before terms are agreed, so a disclosee that walks away from the negotiation keeps a description of what it declined. Position discloses only a count. Note the symmetry that makes this a genuine trade rather than a rout: across disconnected DAGs there is no edge to walk, so an ordering is stipulated rather than derived, exactly as a label is. Position's advantage over labels here is that it leaks nothing, not that it is canonical; labels retain a real advantage on optionality, since a positional gap cannot say which requested DAG is missing.

#### What the list form does not settle {#list-form-open}

Cross-message correspondence. No message carries both `dp` and `o`. `dp` is in the query section `q` of `apply` and `offer` only, on the forward branch; `o` is proposed for the attribute section `a` of the `grant`. So the disclosee fixes an ordering in the apply and the discloser fixes one in the grant, and nothing binds them together. The concrete form of the question: what does the `ax` list index against in an `apply` or `offer`, when the DAG set is fixed by the discloser at grant time and no origin exists yet? Asked on the discussion 2026-09-01; answered in part on 2026-09-02, and the answer does not close it. Sam replied that the KEL anchor "only applies when their is not a presentation registry being used by DAG ACDCs" whose issuees match one of the three eligible anchoring AIDs — which says which anchoring obligation applies to which AID, and says nothing about what an index in an `apply` refers to before any origin exists. The reply was subsequently promoted almost verbatim into #1613's body, so it now reads as standing design text in a discussion it was never posted to. The indexing question stays open; see [section 14, question 4](#q-dag-ordering).

`ax` binds three messages and only one of them is per-DAG. "the Grantor must anchor its `grant` message and the Applicant must anchor both its `agree` and `admit` messages when either is provided" (#1613). The `grant` carries `o` and can be spoken of per DAG. There is one `agree` and one `admit` for the whole exchange. So a per-DAG `ax` has no reading for the disclosee's half of the obligation it creates.

Whether the break must happen now. The "it would be ambiguous" claim looks checkable and may not hold: `o` is a string or a list, `ax` is a Boolean or a list, and `dp`'s first element's first element is a string in the flat form and an array in the nested one — one type probe each, which is the same discrimination argument used to call the field-map option deferrable. If that is right, the preferred form need not break the `dp` text that just landed in `v1.1`, nor force rework of the worked examples that build the flat form. Overtaken in practice, though not answered. keripy resolved the question by fiat in the list form's favour, wrapping `dp`, `o` and `ax` each in a one-element outer list at `b8f60166b` — so the code now presumes the break, while the `v1.1` text it would break still says a `dp` value is a list of tuples rather than a list of lists of tuples. The two are already out of step, which is a smaller version of exactly the branch-divergence problem this chapter's markers exist to track, and it should be re-checked at the next pass on both sides.

What DAG soup earns. Unchanged by the move to lists, and worth keeping separate from it. All three fields must change in a multi-DAG exchange — `o` cannot carry N commitments in one SAID, and `dp`'s prefixes have no unambiguous root once there are N origins — but only `ax` earns a capability by it. The one thing a single bespoke origin cannot express is a negotiated anchoring obligation bound to a particular body of evidence. Issuer-signalled per-ACDC anchoring already works inside one DAG: "When multiple ACDCs in the granted DAG each have a different presentation registry ID field value in the `rd` field in their attribute `a` section, the verifier must verify all of the anchors" (#1613). So the prize is real but narrow, and `o` and `dp` change as plumbing.

#### Superseded, recorded for provenance {#field-map-superseded}

Against the earlier field-map-only text this chapter argued that the labels were an unowned join key spanning three fields, and that a map reinstated the shape `dp` rejects. The first objection is answered by the list form. The second was always the weaker one and should not be revived: the rejection — which is [spec-next], not [spec] — is of a map keyed by schema SAID, a value the ACDCs determine and which therefore collides, whereas a presenter-chosen label can always be made unique.

## 12. The two SEDI recipes, and the reissuance cascade {#sedi-recipes}

#1627's applied section. Both are [proposal]; the trade is genuine and is the most decision- ready material in the discussion.

### Bespoke origin as recipe {#recipe-bespoke}

See [section 3](#bespoke-origin). Maximum flexibility. Relationships form only at presentation time, so "Data changes in each isolated ACDC that cause reissuance do not trigger a cascade of reissuance of other ACDCs." Cost: a bespoke ACDC per presentation, and no memorialization — "A bespoke on-the-fly ACDC does not have any ability to memorialize the state of the graph at the time."

#### Chained ontology as recipe {#recipe-ontology}

Pre-build the DAG as a standing labeled property graph, so that "All presentations are just branches of this master recipe." Edges are non-delegative (`NI2I` or `E1E`) since the issuee is constant. Simplest tooling; strongest normative structure for interop. Cost: the cascade — "whenever data changes in the root ACDC or in ACDCs that lie in upper branches of the graph... then all dependent ACDCs in lower branches or leaves must be reissued," mitigated by pushing volatile data to the leaves, and by a transfer registry whose state change forward-references a replacement rather than revoking.

#### What is not tradeable {#not-tradeable}

"secure delegation requires a DAG that represents the authority structure. A bespoke ACDC can not replace it. The delegation chain is inescapable." So guardianship and every other genuinely delegative use case needs the delegative DAG regardless of which recipe style is chosen, and the realistic answer is a combination.

#### A tension this chapter has not seen addressed {#ontology-origin-tension}

Under the ontology recipe you present a branch, and a branch's local source node is not the ontology's origin. But [section 10](#anchoring)'s anchoring rule and [section 8](#freshness-kram)'s tethering are both stated in terms of "the origin ACDC" of the grant. If the origin is whichever node roots the presented branch, then which party can satisfy `ax` changes with the branch selected — and if instead the ontology's own source node must always be the origin, then every presentation drags the whole upper graph along, which is most of what the ontology approach was supposed to avoid. #1627 does not say which.

#### Question 22: Should the route table say a flow-starting grant may be spurned? {#q-spurn-grant}

Sam ruled on keripy #1628 that an unsolicited grant may be spurned, and keripy's V2 module enforces exactly that, refusing a spurn only of a grant with a prior. Neither ACDC branch's route table has a row for a spurn of a `grant`, and V1 lets a spurn answer any grant. An implementer reading only the spec, or only V1, will build a different rule. ([section 6](#spurn-grant).)
