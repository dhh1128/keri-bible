# 10 — Presentation Registries and Issuee-Side Detectability {#registries}

## Thesis {#thesis}

KERI's answer to key compromise is not prevention but detect and recover, and for the Issuer that answer is already built: because an ACDC's state must be anchored in a TEL that is anchored in the Issuer's KEL, an Issuer can see fraudulent issuances it did not create, without anyone's cooperation. The Issuee has no equivalent. A compromised Issuee's keys can present its credentials, and the real Issuee has no place to look. A presentation registry — Sam's original and better name for it is an Issuee usage registry — closes that asymmetry by giving the Issuee its own blindable state registry, requiring that a presentation be anchored in it, and thereby letting the Issuee detect presentations it did not make and rotate to invalidate them.

Two things distinguish this from the rest of the presentation design. First, it is deliberately optional, and the argument for optionality is quantitative: Issuer compromise is rampant and high-ROI, Issuee compromise is localized and low-ROI, so Issuee-side detectability is a measure an Issuee elects and pays for rather than a protocol requirement. Second, it does two unrelated jobs with one mechanism — impersonation-fraud detection and correlation resistance — and they have different preconditions, so a design that satisfies one may not deliver the other. Confusing them is the most likely error in this area.

## Status {#status}

Pre-normative, and partly built in keripy only. As of 2026-10-03 the tiers sit as follows, and they have to be held apart because code and specification now say different things.

- [K] at keripy `80f77b73b`, for `/ipex/grant` only — The verifier-side check exists and fails closed. An Issuee-controlled registry is recognized by the incepter check (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, vetBinds, L714 @80f77b73b). Every `(i, rd)` declaration across the disclosed DAG must bind the `grant` — "Verify every presentation registry declared across the DAG." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L609 @80f77b73b). The binding must be dated no later than the receiver's KRAM acceptance time `rdt`, and a `grant` without completed KRAM acceptance is refused. A compact `a`, an undisclosed `A` entry or an `rd` in `a` without a top-level `rd` is refused rather than skipped. The requirement is read from issuance metadata on every `grant`, independent of `ax`. The Issuee's write path (`present`/`vacate`) exists. One boundary carries over from last month unchanged: the V2 IPEX handler has no caller in `src/keri`, so this is code that exists and is tested in the reference implementation, not behavior any shipped keripy command performs ([section 7](#keripy-verifier)).
- Still [P] — Retrieval of a foreign registry (the verifier must already hold the Issuee's TEL), #1095's verifier-side acceptance delay, a presentation registry on an ACDC with no Issuer state registry, the correlation-protected self-issued bespoke origin (nothing forbids it, no test builds one), and every piece of the design's rationale.
- Nothing is [N] or [N1.1] — Both ACDC specification lines remain silent ([section 5](#normative)). And the code applies the requirement to every declaration whoever sends, where #1613 conditions it on the Issuee being the Grantor ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram), [section 7](#keripy-verifier), open questions 10 and 11). Superseded 2026-10-03: the previous status read "pre-normative, unbuilt, and now intended on the written record", resting on keripy `b8f60166b`, where no code read an `rd` from an attribute section and one landed check forbade the arrangement by construction. ### Two names, and searching one misses the other {#two-names}

A warning that is also a research finding: this concept has two names, and searching one misses the other. Sam calls it an Issuee usage registry in [#1095](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1095) (October 2025) and a presentation registry in [#1613](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1613) (August 2026). A search for "presentation registry" across keripy returns #1613, #1618, #1550 and — since 2026-09-02, in a comment only — #1627, and not #1095, which is the document that invents the idea and carries most of its security reasoning. Any survey of this topic has to search both terms plus "issuee registry". In speech Sam has used a third name, "user presentation registry" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 14:36). The code has picked its own vocabulary, and it is the narrow one (keripy `80f77b73b`, `raw/16` section 7.7). `ipexing.py` says "presentation registry" in comments and docstrings, with "issuee/presentation-registry declarations" (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1410 @80f77b73b) and "Issuee registry pairs" (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1434 @80f77b73b); the registry facade says "issuee-controlled registry" (keripy acdc/registraring.py, Registrar.present, L413 @80f77b73b); the SEDI schemas describe the field as "Issuee Presentation Registry SAID"; the binding kind is `'presentation'` (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, L60 @80f77b73b) with default TEL state `"presented"`; and the Issuees a registry authenticates are the `tethered` set (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L610 @80f77b73b). "Usage registry" occurs nowhere in keripy's `src/` or `tests/`. So a code search needs "presentation", "issuee-controlled" and "tethered" as well as the discussion terms. #1098 ([section 1](#asymmetry)) also says "presentation registry", in a body last edited 2026-09-26. This chapter uses "presentation registry" for the IPEX-anchoring application and "usage registry" for the general primitive, and [section 7](#keripy-verifier) and open question 2 argue that distinction is real rather than cosmetic.

## 1. The asymmetry that motivates it {#asymmetry}

### Issuer-side detectability {#issuer-detectability}

Issuer-side detectability is mandatory, and already exists [P]. #1095's argument: the catastrophic failure is Issuer key compromise, because "the worst form of fraud with the most widespread potential of harm would be rampant impersonation fraud… This could annihilate trust in the ecosystem fostered by any assurance derived from the Issuer. The ROI to an attacker for compromising the Issuer keys could be huge, especially if that Issuer is a root-of-trust for Identity assurance for an ecosystem." Anchoring answers it: "an impersonator cannot impersonate the Issuer of an ACDC merely by compromising the signing keys of the Issuer. The impersonator MUST anchor the ACDC in a TEL that is anchored in the Issuer's KEL. This anchor enables the Issuer to detect any fraudulent issuances without requiring the cooperation of any other party, such as the Verifier."

That last clause is the KERI-shaped part: detection with no dependency on a counterparty. Sam attaches a comparative claim — "AFAIK, only KERI/ACDC provides such protection in a decentralized identity system" — which is his assessment, not a surveyed result, and should be repeated as such.

#### Issuee-side detectability {#issuee-detectability}

Issuee-side detectability is optional, and the reasoning is explicit [P]. "If a given Issuee's keys are compromised, the impersonation fraud is localized to that specific Issuee. It does not have rampant potential." Three consequences, all from #1095:

1. Low susceptibility. "Because the fraud is localized to a single Issuee, the ROI to the attacker for compromising the Issuee's keys is low." And a cheap mitigation already exists: prophylactic rotation, effective "because the verifier always checks the presentation as being signed by the latest keys of the Issuee." Sam names this as distinctive — the Issuee's ability "to rotate signing keys without reissuing the ACDC is a unique super-power of KERI/ACDC as a decentralized identity system."
2. Localized harm licenses localized measures. A Verifier could simply require a rotation as part of the presentation. Sam concedes what that costs doctrinally: "This violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party, but for localized harm, the party most likely to be harmed is the Verifier (Validator), which provides incentives for them to protect themselves." Record this as a real exception rather than smoothing it over — it is one of the few places in the corpus where the no-trusted-third-party maxim is knowingly relaxed, and an adversarial reviewer will find it whether or not we flag it. It is also not the only relaxation in this design: [section 8](#adversarial) records a second and distinct one, attaching to the presentation registry itself.
3. The Issuee can elect its own detectability. That is the usage registry.

#### The design consequence of optionality [P] {#optionality-cost}

"This is an optional protection measure. Many Issuees will feel that their key management is sufficient to protect against key compromise and therefore would not want the extra protection and friction of a usage Registrar." A presentation registry means the Issuee runs infrastructure — "the Issuee must have its own Registrar to manage its usage registries." That cost is why the mechanism is elective, and why any argument that it should be mandatory has to answer it.

#### Rotate, do not revoke, and log rather than track state {#rotate-not-revoke}

Added 2026-10-15 from `raw/21`. Sam restated the asymmetry in August 2026 while correcting a misreading that conflated the two kinds of registry. A compromised Issuee key is answered by key state, not credential state: "then the solution is not to revoke the ACDC but to rotate the keys", and "if the keys get compromised then the keystate is changed not the ACDC state." And he drew the line between the registries in one sentence: "A presentation registry on the other hand is not tracking ACDC state, but is merely logging presentations" (keripy discussion #1574, comment of 2026-08-07, R-042, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1574#discussioncomment-17938568). That is the cleanest statement in the corpus of what a presentation registry is for: it makes presentations, not the ACDC, perpetually verifiable, so that impersonation of the Issuee at presentation time becomes detectable by the Issuee. [P]

#### Where it sits among the tiers of IPEX {#ipex-tiers}

Added 2026-10-15. Sam's four tiers of IPEX ([chapter 08, section 10](08-presentation-architectures-and-ipex.md#four-tiers)) place the presentation registry as an add-on at either stakes level, not as a stage every exchange passes through. His caption for the low-stakes form with a registry: "This enables the presenter to detect if anyone has compromised the Presenter's keys and is fraudulently using those keys" (keripy discussion #1512, comment of 2026-07-22, R-062, https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1512#discussioncomment-17737728). That fits the optionality argument above: the registry buys Issuee-side detectability, and an Issuee or EGF that does not want it still has signed-only and anchored tiers to choose from. [P]

#### Optional to use, but not to be foreclosed {#not-foreclosed}

Optional to use, but a credential format must not foreclose it [P]. Added 2026-10-03 from [#1098](https://github.com/WebOfTrust/keripy/discussions/1098) (ACDC with Static Age Predicate Proof SMT, SmithSamuelM, created 2025-10-27, last edited 2026-09-26), which supplies the clearest purpose statement in the corpus. It rejects unlinkable ZKP predicate proofs on detectability grounds. Issuer-side first: "the use of unlinkable ZKPs is therefore precluded to preserve the detectability of an Issuer signing infrastructure compromise". Then the Issuee side: "Likewise, the key compromise of an Issuee (Holder) can be made detectable using a presentation registry. Which would also be defeated by an unlinkable ZKP." What the chosen design buys is "strong security (detectability of key compromise for both the Issuer and the Issuee)". `raw/16` section 1a draws the consequence, and it is that note's reading rather than Sam's words: #1098 does not contradict #1095's optionality, since detectability "can be made" available rather than must be, but it treats the possibility of a presentation registry as something a cryptographic choice may not foreclose. The Issuee registry is offered as the presentation-time analogue of a property #1098 calls one of "the inviolable properties of KERI/ACDCs". One provenance caution: without the edit history it cannot be said whether the "presentation registry" sentence was in the October 2025 text or added in September 2026, so it is evidence of Sam's position at 2026-09-26 only.

## 2. The mechanism {#mechanism}

### The rule, in its original form [P] {#original-rule}

"The ACDC must include a requirement that the ACDC is only verifiable when the presentation of the ACDC by the Issuee is anchored as the latest state in an Issuee-controlled *usage registry*. The verifier won't accept the credential if it's also not anchored in the Issuee's (in addition to the Issuer's) registry" (#1095).

#### Developed into IPEX terms [P] {#ipex-terms}

In #1613 the anchored object is the `grant`: "the value of the registry `rd` field in the attribute `a` section MUST be the SAID of the `rip` event of the associated presentation Registry. The Issuee of the associated ACDC, who is also a Grantor of that IPEX, MUST control this registry. The value of the ACDC SAID field in the blinded attribute block of the latest non-vacuous event in that presentation Registry must be the SAID of the `grant` message." Note the field reuse: the slot that normally carries an ACDC SAID carries a `grant` SAID.

#### Three of four requirements in keripy {#requirements-in-keripy}

Three of that quotation's four requirements are [K] at `80f77b73b`, and one is not (`raw/16` section 2.2). The `rd` must be the registry's `rip` SAID: `_vetRegistry` raises `MisregistryError` when the verified chain's `regid` differs from the declared one. The Issuee must control the registry: `vetBinds` checks "if controller is not None and record.issuer != controller:" (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, vetBinds, L714 @80f77b73b), and the presentation path passes the `a`-section `i` as the controller. The latest non-vacuous event must bind the `grant` SAID: `vetBinds` walks back over vacuous updates and requires the first non-vacuous one to bind the target, which is the `grant` SAID (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyPresentationAuthGraph, L1537 @80f77b73b). The clause "who is also a Grantor of that IPEX" is not enforced. The Issuee need not sign or anchor the `grant`; the registry alone authenticates it ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram), open question 11). The Issuee's side is built too: `Registrar.present`, "Bind a grant message SAID in an issuee-controlled registry." (keripy acdc/registraring.py, Registrar.present, L413 @80f77b73b), builds a blinded update with state `"presented"`, and `vacate` builds a vacuous one. Nothing in `present` checks that the caller is anyone's Issuee; control is enforced only by the verifier's incepter check.

#### One registry for many credentials {#many-to-one}

One registry can serve many credentials without leaking which [P]. "A *usage registry* can be employed to track the usage of one or more ACDCs by the Issuee… This means that the registry does not leak specific usage of a given ACDC to third parties" (#1095). Blindable state is what makes many-to-one safe; without blinding, a shared registry would be a correlation engine rather than a defence.

#### Detection converted to prevention {#acceptance-delay}

Detection converted to prevention, at the price of latency [P]. #1095 offers a verifier-side trick that appears nowhere else in the corpus: "by adding a time delay from presentation anchor to acceptance, the verifier can assume that a compromised Issuee would do a recovery, invalidating the fraudulent presentation." Detect-and-recover normally means the damage lands and is then undone; a deliberate acceptance delay gives the real Issuee a window to rotate before the presentation is honored. This is worth more attention than it has had. It is the only proposal in the corpus that converts Issuee-side detectability into Issuee-side prevention, and its cost — every honest presentation waits — is exactly the kind of tradeoff an EGF should be setting.

#### Incentive alignment {#incentive-alignment}

Incentive alignment, stated as a rule of construction [P]. "The verifier has a vested interest in not accepting unanchored presentations as the presentation of an Issuee unanchored ACDC that declares it must be Issuee usage anchored, must be considered fraudulent" (#1095). #1613 sharpens it into a liability claim: a verifier accepting an unanchored grant "is presumptively in violation of any contractual or regulatory protection afforded to the real Grantor by colluding with a fraudulent grantor."

## 3. Two jobs, two different preconditions {#two-jobs}

This is the section to get right, because the mechanism is the same in both cases and the guarantees are not.

### Job one: impersonation-fraud detection {#job-fraud-detection}

Job one: impersonation-fraud detection — requires that the Issuer is not the Grantor [P]. The protection works because the requirement is baked in by someone the attacker has not compromised: "an imposter that merely compromises the Grantor's signing infrastructure can't avoid the requirement without also compromising the Issuer." The Grantee enforces it by refusing an unanchored grant; the real Issuee then sees registry events it did not create and rotates.

The hole is self-issuance, and #1613 states it plainly: "For ACDCs that the Grantor self-issues, this is only a vulnerability if the Issuance authentication factor is merely an unanchored signature. An imposter who controls the Grantor's signing infrastructure can create ACDCs whose authentication factor is an unanchored signature." An attacker holding your keys can simply issue itself a credential that demands nothing. So the fraud-detection property is a property of the three-party arrangement, not of the registry.

#### The verifier half of job one in keripy {#job-one-keripy}

The verifier half of job one is [K] at `80f77b73b`, including the property that makes it work: the Grantor cannot opt out (`raw/16` section 2.3). The requirement is read from issuance metadata on every `grant`; the test says "Presentation anchoring is mandatory from issuance metadata even" when a later `grant` omits `ax` (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_presentation_registry_binds_proxy_grant, L6232 @80f77b73b). A presenter cannot hide the declaration by compacting the attribute section, because a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` or any `A` entry is undisclosed is refused outright: "A verifier cannot distinguish an omitted requirement from an undisclosed one using only its digest." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1414-1415 @80f77b73b). Nothing checks that Issuer and Issuee differ, so the self-issued hole is present in code exactly as #1613 concedes it.

#### Job two: correlation resistance {#job-correlation}

Job two: correlation resistance — works even self-issued [P]. The problem: if a Grantor anchors a `grant` SAID directly in its public KEL and the Grantee anchors the same SAID in its own, "a third party… would be able to detect the same grant SAID in both the Grantor's KEL and the Grantee's KEL, thereby correlating the IPEX." Anchoring in a blinded presentation registry instead means "neither the grant SAID nor any other artifacts of the granted DAG appear in the KEL of the Grantor," so "a Grantee could anchor every message in the IPEX without providing a point of correlation between the KELs of the Grantor and Grantee."

For this job a Grantor needs no cooperating Issuer: it self-issues a bespoke origin ACDC carrying `i` and `rd` in its attribute section — "the Grantor is both Issuer and Issuee of that bespoke ACDC." One constraint rides along, and it is easy to violate: "the bespoke ACDC itself must use a blinded state Registry for its Issuer authentication so that the SAID of its ACDC is not correlatable. If it uses a direct KEL anchor, then that anchor itself would be correlatable. If it used a bare attached signature… then the ACDC itself would not be perpetually verifiable despite using a perpetually verifiable presentation registry."

Job two stays [P] at `80f77b73b`. Nothing in keripy forbids a self-issued bespoke origin carrying a presentation registry, but no test constructs one, so whether the pieces compose is untested (`raw/16` section 7.8).

#### The origin object, now specified [N1.1] {#origin-specified}

The object that construction reaches for is now specified, though not for this purpose [N1.1]. The ACDC specification's `v1.1` branch at `2362e48a1` makes both halves normative in its own line: "That DAG MUST have exactly one source node, called the origin node," and, where a single exchange conveys several unchained ACDCs, "a DAG MUST be formed by issuing a bespoke ACDC whose node is the origin." That earns the chapter two things it did not have. The single-origin rule is the premise underneath the claim below that one anchor can cover a whole DAG — commit to the origin and you have committed to what is reachable from it — and until this pass the corpus held that premise only on #1613's own say-so. And the bespoke origin #1613 asks a Grantor to self-issue is a specified object rather than an ad-hoc one. What `v1.1` does not say is that such an origin needs a blinded state registry, which is exactly the constraint quoted above and exactly what #1627's competing recipe drops; see open question 8.

#### Correlation resistance is itself conditional [P] {#correlation-conditional}

The registry is "bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable. A bulk-issued ACDC with independent AIDs can remove this linkage" (#1095). So full unlinkability needs blinded state and independent-registry bulk issuance. A presentation registry used naively hides which credential was presented while advertising that the Issuee presented something.

#### Multi-ACDC DAGs [P] {#multi-acdc}

"When multiple ACDCs in the granted DAG each have a different presentation registry ID field value… the verifier must verify all of the anchors." And a precise statement of what a single anchor buys, worth quoting whenever someone over-claims: because the `grant`'s `o` field names the DAG origin, anchoring in one registry "provides a perpetually verifiable proof of the presentation of all ACDCs in that DAG, but not necessarily a fresh proof of control for all Issuee AIDS in that DAG. Multiple anchors in different registries provide multiple vectors of detectability and multiple fresh proofs-of-control; the perpetual verifiability is redundant."

#### keripy implements the strict form [K] {#strict-form}

keripy implements the strict form of the multi-ACDC rule [K]. At `80f77b73b` `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` collects every declaration across the walked DAG and fails the whole `grant` if any one does not bind it: "Every declared node-local presentation registry must bind the grant." (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_verifies_presentation_registries_for_different_issuees, L6355 @80f77b73b). Because each registry must bind this `grant`'s SAID, each is also a fresh proof of control for its Issuee. The case of one anchor covering the DAG while other Issuees go unproven is therefore not selectable — there is no way to declare a registry and have it ignored (`raw/16` section 2.3). A declaration is honored on any node, not only the origin: "Validate Core a.rd when its Residence origin does not declare one." (keripy tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py, test_residence_sedi_uses_nested_core_presentation_registry, L2364 @80f77b73b). The SEDI examples (PRs #1689, #1697) use that to realize ~~"one anchor proves the whole DAG"~~ Cut 2026-10-03 (verification): not found in #1613 snapshot at 2026-10-03; #1613's wording is the "perpetually verifiable proof of the presentation of all ACDCs in that DAG" quoted above. one anchor covering the DAG by practice rather than rule: the root identity credential, Core, carries the registry, and dependent credentials such as Residence carry none — "Residence needs only issuer proof; Core owns the presentation proof." (keripy tests/sedi/test_ipex_sedi.py, test_residence_sedi_uses_nested_core_presentation_registry, L2529 @80f77b73b). That is [K] as example and test practice. Nothing stops an Issuer declaring registries on dependent credentials as well, and then all of them must bind.

One label caution, because it will mislead a careful reader rather than a careless one. The `o` in that sentence is a field of the IPEX exchange message, and the ACDC specification does not define it on either branch; there `o` is the Operator field of an Edge or Edge-group. The two never share a field map, so nothing is actually ambiguous, but the concept the `grant`'s `o` names — a DAG with a single origin — is [N1.1], while the field carrying it is [P] plus [K] and is standardized nowhere.

## 4. The trigger, and the ambiguity blocking it {#trigger}

### The signal is a three-part test [P] {#three-part-signal}

From #1613: the requirement fires when a non-empty `rd` and a valid Issuee `i` are at the top level of the ACDC's attribute `a` section, and the ACDC's own top-level `rd` is non-empty. Miss any one and the meaning changes: with no Issuee in `a`, an `rd` in `a` imposes no anchoring requirement at all; with the top-level `rd` empty or missing, the `rd` in `a` "refers to an ACDC state registry, not an IPEX presentation registry." The top-level `rd` is doing disambiguation work, which is the easiest part of this design to miss when reading #1613 quickly.

#### Why disambiguation is needed [P] {#why-disambiguate}

The v1 spec also permits `rd` in `a` as a hidden ACDC state registry, so one field slot carries two unrelated meanings. #1613 offers EGF-declared purpose with "The default, if not otherwise specified, should be for anchoring presentation exchanges," floats forbidding the hiding case in v1.1, and then argues that case down: its only benefit arises "when the ACDC in compact form is made public, independent of any given presentation, and the fact of it having a registry must remain undisclosed. It is not clear that this is a worthy use case… Given the potentially dubious value, we might want to forbid using the `rd` field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry."

The section ends unresolved — "Not sure if either is a worthy use case" — and that unresolved state is what blocks normative text. You cannot specify a trigger condition on a field whose meaning is still contested.

#### keripy has made the choice [K] {#keripy-choice}

keripy has made the choice the specifications have not [K]. At `80f77b73b` the code implements #1613's three-part test as the disambiguation rule, extended to disclosed `A` aggregate entries as well as `a` (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1467-1488 @80f77b73b), and it refuses the hiding case rather than supporting it. Without a top-level `rd`, an inner `rd` is classed as "hidden issuer registry, not a presentation registry." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1419 @80f77b73b) and the ACDC fails verification, with no fall-through to a signature: the test of that point is named `test_ipex_v2_rejects_hidden_issuer_registry_signature_fallback` (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_rejects_hidden_issuer_registry_signature_fallback, L3558 @80f77b73b). That is the closest of #1613's options — forbid the `rd` field in the attribute section for an ACDC state registry — minus the benefit #1613 said forbidding would bring, since keripy recognizes a presentation registry only when a top-level `rd` is present (`raw/16` section 2.4). It is an implementation choice, not a resolution. Its consequence for interoperability is that a v1-conformant ACDC using `a.rd` as a hidden state registry is rejected by keripy's V2 IPEX verifier today.

#### What the choice costs [P] {#choice-cost}

What the choice costs, which is more than a labelling nicety [P]. The two readings are mutually exclusive per ACDC, and #1613 says so flatly: "a presentation registry may not be used with an ACDC that uses the `rd` field in the attribute `a` section as an Issuer-controlled ACDC state." One slot, one meaning, fixed at issuance and not recoverable afterwards. So an Issuer choosing the hiding case forecloses Issuee-side detectability on that credential permanently. #1613 uses the same fact to talk itself out of the hiding case — hiding "is not useful with full independent Registry bulk issuance since each presentation could use a different member of the bulk issued set" — so the correlation problem the hiding case solves is already solved better elsewhere, while the price of keeping it, forfeiting presentation registries for that ACDC, is paid nowhere else.

#### `v1.1` did not settle it {#v11-unsettled}

And `v1.1` did not settle it. At `2362e48a1` the ACDC specification's Registry SAID Field section and its reserved-fields row are byte-identical to `main` at `f0bd097de`, typo included. None of the three resolutions #1613 floats — EGF-declared purpose, restriction to presentation registries, forbidding the state-registry reading — has been taken up in the branch most likely to carry it. That is a negative result at a named commit rather than a silence, and it means the contested-field material in this section and in [section 5](#normative) stands unchanged and is now dated.

#### Field-placement consequence nobody else raises [P] {#exchange-rd}

From #1618: "An exchange anchored in a presentation registry would also need an `rd` on the exchange." If true, this design implies a field addition to `exn`, not only to the ACDC — and that is not accounted for in #1613's `ax` treatment. Added 2026-10-03: it is not what keripy built. ## 5. What is normative: one word {#normative}

[N] In `trustoverip/kswg-acdc-specification` `spec/spec-body.md` on `main` @`f0bd097de` (2026-08-28), "presentation registry" does not appear at all and "usage registry" appears exactly once, in a gloss. What does exist:

- The nested-`rd` field's purpose list includes usage. Top-level: "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, or retraction registry for ACDC" (ACDC spec > ACDC Structure > Top-Level Fields, L23 @f0bd097de). Not-at-top-level: "Issuance and/or revocation, transfer, retraction, or usage registry for ACDC when not at top-level" (ACDC spec > ACDC Structure > Other Reserved Fields, L48 @f0bd097de). That word "usage" is the only trace of this design in any specification.
- Nested `rd` is open-ended, but the examples point elsewhere: it "MAY be used for some other registry, such as an application-specific or Issuer-specific registry" (ACDC spec > ACDC Structure > Registry SAID Field, L85 @f0bd097de). The spec does not contemplate an Issuee-controlled registry.
- The spec's rationale for nesting is bulk issuance and graduated disclosure — it "may better facilitate contractually protected disclosure of the bulk-issued registry" (ACDC spec > Attribute Section > Targeted Attribute Section, L326 @f0bd097de) — not fraud detection.
- Blindable state registries and `rip` are fully normative, with a worked lifecycle. A `rip` is "a vacuous placeholder that reveals nothing about what will later be issued," which is where #1613's "latest non-vacuous event" phrasing comes from.

### The worked lifecycle states the correlation boundary [N] {#correlation-boundary}

The worked lifecycle also states this chapter's correlation boundary, and states it better than the corpus had [N]. The Registry-Dependent Issuance Lifecycle section walks a registry from `rip` through issuance, blinded state, verification and revocation, and closes with two sentences that belong wherever anyone claims a blindable registry buys privacy. The first draws the line between the log and its contents: "The registry *events* remain public and chained (the `rd` and prior links are in the clear); it is the state *content* each event commits to that is protected." The second is the doctrine: "This is confidentiality of registry state, not anonymity of the credential — issuer, registry, and issuance chronology remain linkable, which is the intended, auditable trade-off." This is the normative version of what [section 3](#two-jobs) records from #1095 as a caveat — a usage registry is "bound to the AID of the Issuee, so it is linkable" — and it settles the register in which the whole correlation-resistance argument should be made. Linkability of the registry is not a defect the design failed to notice; it is the trade the design chose. Whoever writes spec text for presentation registries will have to say the same thing about an Issuee's registry that this section says about an Issuer's.

#### The lifecycle is Issuer-controlled end to end [N] {#issuer-controlled-lifecycle}

But the lifecycle is Issuer-controlled end to end, and that is a negative result, not an omission [N]. The whole worked example runs Issuer-side: the Issuer incepts the registry, issues, and appends every blindable update, while the Issuee's only role is holding the shared salt. Nothing in it, and nothing elsewhere on either branch, contemplates a registry incepted by the Issuee.

## 6. Where this sits against Registrar/Observer and KRAM {#registrar-and-kram}

### It extends the governance split to a third party {#governance-split}

[Chapter 05, section 9](05-acdc-and-verifiable-data.md#registries) records Sam's framing that the Registrar/Observer split deliberately replays witness/watcher one layer up: "we have witnesses that are controlled by controllers, watchers that are controlled by verifiers… We have registrars controlled by the issuers, and observers controlled by the verifiers" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 16:58). A presentation registry adds an Issuee-controlled Registrar to that picture — the Holder, previously the one party in the triangle running no state infrastructure, now runs some. This is the synthesis the corpus does not state anywhere, and it makes the design look less like a bolt-on: "We shouldn't have shared governance" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 16:54) applied consistently gives every party to a transaction its own state service.

Sam's KERIcon phrasing is the clean version: a presentation registry is something which "allows presenters to detect compromise of their proofs that are in their credentials that they're presenting" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 14:44), and "So they themselves will protect it from fraud, not just the issuer" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 14:52). (Edited auto-caption — read `raw/14` section 0 before quoting it to anyone. Corrected 2026-10-15: the quote previously began "presenters detect", dropping the caption's "allows" and "to"; it is now split in two to restore the caption text and stay within the quotation limit.)

#### It sits inside KRAM's window [P] {#kram-window}

"in order to pass KRAM, a `grant` message anchor must happen in a timely fashion relative to the IPEX exchange. The `grant` message… must be created, then anchored, and then pass the receiver's KRAM" (#1613). A presentation registry therefore inserts a registry write into the critical path of a freshness window — and #1095's verifier-side delay deliberately adds more latency on top. Nothing in the corpus connects the two numerically. See [chapter 09, section 3](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md#full-kram) on why full KRAM's windows can be long enough to absorb this, and [chapter 09, section 9](09-kram-and-request-authentication.md#open-questions) on the absence of any window-class policy guidance.

#### keripy couples the two differently [K] {#rdt-coupling}

keripy couples the two differently: the registry check depends on KRAM, rather than the registry write having to pass it [K]. At `80f77b73b` KRAM's transactioned-message cache records a receiver timestamp, `rdt`, "receiver datetime when KRAM accepted the complete message" (keripy recording.py, TxnMsgCacheRecord, L505 @80f77b73b). For a multi-key sender it is set only once the signing threshold is met (keripy core/kraming.py, Kramer.kramit, L1658-1661 @80f77b73b; `raw/15` section 4.8). The presentation-registry check consumes it and refuses without it: "A presentation requirement needs a completed KRAM acceptance." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyPresentationAuthGraph, L1524 @80f77b73b). It then cuts the registry history off at that time — "Require a valid binding anchored before the KRAM-accepted send." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._verifyPresentationAuthGraph, L1543 @80f77b73b) — by dropping updates whose `dt` is later and rejecting a registry whose `rip` is later (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, vetBinds, L686-705 @80f77b73b). Three consequences follow, recorded in `raw/16` section 2.2 and section 8 item 5. The timeliness test compares the TEL event's own `dt`, which the Issuee asserts, against the receiver's clock. The KEL event anchoring that TEL event may arrive afterwards, through escrow. And since the bound target is this `grant`'s SAID, the binding is necessarily created after the `grant` body is fixed; the tests build a draft `grant`, bind its SAID, then rebuild the identical `grant` with the proofs attached. `raw/15` section 4.8 adds an inference not tested in code: a deployment with KRAM disabled cannot accept a `grant` carrying a presentation-registry declaration, because no cache record will exist. Neither the whitepaper nor any spec line mentions a receiver acceptance time consumed by another protocol.

#### The registry anchor does not stand in for the ordinary one [P] {#double-authentication}

And the registry anchor does not stand in for the ordinary one [P]. #1613's 2026-09-02 edit makes the layering explicit: "KRAM does not recognize or process a presentation registry-based authentication factor for the grant message," so "a presentation registry-anchored grant message is always double-authenticated," and the duplication is "unavoidable because the processing of presentation registry validation happens higher up in the stack than grant message authentication." Two consequences. The registry write is added to the signature or direct KEL anchor rather than replacing either, which makes the latency question in open question 5 worse than it looked, not better. And the absence of any presentation-registry handling in keripy's `kraming.py` is conformance rather than a gap — ~~[section 7](#keripy-verifier)'s negative is about `ipexing.py`, where such a check would belong~~ (updated 2026-10-03: the check now exists in `ipexing.py`, above KRAM, as #1613 says it should; [section 7](#keripy-verifier)). A verifier that treats registry anchoring as replacing the signature check will reject valid grants or accept invalid ones, depending on which way it errs.

#### keripy confirms the layering for the sender only [K] {#sender-layering}

keripy confirms that layering for the sender and departs from #1613 for anyone else [K], and the departure is a divergence, not a settlement. When the Issuee whose registry is declared also sends the `grant` — every SEDI test — double authentication holds by construction: KRAM authenticates the sender's signature, and the registry check runs above it in `IpexHandler.verify`. `kraming.py` still contains no presentation-registry logic beyond recording `rdt`. When the Issuee is not the sender, keripy no longer requires the Issuee to endorse the `grant`. The proxy test is explicit that "Sender authentication plus the Issuee's presentation TEL is" sufficient and that "the Issuee does not need a redundant EXN endorsement." (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_presentation_registry_binds_proxy_grant, L6205-6206 @80f77b73b). #1613's ACDC Grantor Authentication Factor section says the opposite for this case: "To be accepted by KRAM, the grant message must be endorsed by each Grantor". The code did require it for four days. Commit `790797bcd` (2026-09-21) carried "A registry supplements, but never replaces, a Grant endorsement.", and `b7c170f93` "remove grantor requisite" (2026-09-25) deleted it, both inside PR #1693 before it merged, so no first-parent commit on `main` ever enforced it (`raw/16` section 2.6; `raw/09` section 7e). The commit message gives no reason. The two positions can be reconciled — a registry anchor is a KEL-anchored commitment to the exact `grant` SAID, at least as strong as a signature, so in effect it makes the Issuee a Grantor without an endorsement — but that is a design change #1613 does not record. A reader of #1613 will expect a check the code does not make. One side effect follows from the code, though no test asserts it: the new endorser index `eidx` (keripy db/basing.py, Baser, L591 @80f77b73b) is filled from accepted signatures and seals only, so an Issuee authenticated by registry alone is not indexed against the `grant`. See open question 11.

#### Independent of, and stronger than, `ax` [P] {#independent-of-ax}

It is independent of, and stronger than, `ax` [P]. "Notwithstanding the presence or absence of the `ax` field, when an ACDC in a granted DAG meets the requirements for a Issuer signaled presentation anchor registry… then that `grant` message MUST be anchored in that registry for the `grant` to be valid. Otherwise, the presumption is that the grant is fraudulent." `ax` lets a party request perpetual verifiability; a presentation registry lets an Issuer impose it at issuance time, and the Grantor cannot opt out later. See [chapter 08, section 10](08-presentation-architectures-and-ipex.md#anchoring). ~~The two have since come apart in implementation as well as in design — `ax` is built, the presentation registry is not — which [section 7](#keripy-verifier) states as its own finding.~~ Superseded 2026-10-03: both are built at `80f77b73b`, and the independence clause is [K]: the presentation-registry check runs on every `grant` whether or not `ax` is present ([section 3](#two-jobs)).

#### A registry anchor counts toward `ax` [K] {#counts-toward-ax}

And a registry anchor now counts toward `ax` [K]. #1613's Origin AID Anchoring section proposes that under a truthy `ax` "either the sender must anchor the grant or the Issuee or Issuer of the origin ACDC of the grant must anchor the grant". At `80f77b73b` all three are implemented, and an Issuee authenticated by a presentation registry is one of the anchorers: "Presentation registries authenticate their controlling Issuees." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L626 @80f77b73b), followed by "Restrict default qualification to sender, Issuee, or issuer." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L647 @80f77b73b). The code checks set membership, so #1613's "order of priority" has no operational effect, and it states the three-AID boundary as default policy that credential-specific policy may narrow (`raw/16` section 2.5). This is broader than the `b8f60166b` behavior this chapter recorded last month, where only the sender's own KEL anchor satisfied `ax`.

#### Scope judgment on which registry may be used [P] {#which-registry}

"There is no security advantage to using a presentation registry specified by the Grantor, as the main purpose… is to allow the Grantor to detect a compromise of its signing key infrastructure… Thus, the main use case for impersonation fraud detection of the Grantor requires a pre-specified presentation registry by an Issuer different from the Grantor". On tooling, #1613 declines to build anything special: "we should use the existing tooling for a bulk-issued ACDC to handle it." The first judgment has a [K] counterpart at `80f77b73b`: the code recognizes only registries declared in a DAG node, and nothing on the `grant` itself can name one (`raw/16` section 2.5). The tooling remark has only a partial one. The Issuee's write path reuses the `_blind` path of Issuer issuance (`raw/16` section 7.5), which is consistent with it, but whether bulk-issuance tooling serves presentation registries was not examined.

### One boundary bounds every [K] claim in this section {#v2-boundary}

One boundary bounds every [K] claim in this section, and it has not moved. `acdc/ipexing.py` is the V2 IPEX handler, and at `80f77b73b` its `loadHandlers` still has no caller anywhere in `src/keri`; keripy's own CLI wires up the V1 handler in `vc/protocoling.py`, and the v1 `vdr` verifier has no presentation registry (`raw/09` section 7b; `raw/16` section 7.8). So these are claims about code that exists and is tested in the reference implementation, not about behavior any shipped keripy command performs today.

### 7.1 What is implemented {#implemented}

On every `/ipex/grant`, after the DAG walk, the edge semantics and Issuer authentication of every node have passed, `IpexHandler.verify` runs `_verifyPresentationAuthGraph` — "Verify every presentation registry declared across the DAG." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L609 @80f77b73b). For each node it collects the `(i, rd)` pairs declared in a disclosed `a` section or disclosed `A` aggregate entries, provided the node also has a top-level `rd`. For each pair it requires that the registry named by `rd` is incepted by that `i`; that its latest non-vacuous update, as of the receiver's KRAM acceptance time, binds this `grant`'s SAID; and that the node carries a KEL source reference from the Issuee to the binding event. Any failure rejects the `grant`. The Issuees so authenticated form the `tethered` set and count as anchorers for a truthy `ax` ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram)). `apply`, `offer`, `agree`, `admit` and `spurn` never consult a presentation registry, which matches #1613's statement that the `grant` is "the presentation" (`raw/16` section 7.1).

### 7.2 Where it fails closed {#fails-closed}

Each step refuses rather than skipping (`raw/16` section 7.2). Declaration discovery fails the `grant` when a registry-backed node (one with a top-level `rd`) has a compact `a` or `A` or any undisclosed aggregate entry — "Every inspected aggregate entry must be a mapping." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._presentationRegistries, L1458 @80f77b73b) — and on an inner `rd` without a top-level one ([section 4](#trigger)). A `grant` without completed KRAM acceptance fails ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram)). The TEL must be held locally — "Registry verification requires an injected local Regery." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler._registryEvidence, L1290 @80f77b73b) — and a missing `rip`, head or intermediate update raises `MissingChainError`, which is retryable from escrow. Updates later than the KRAM acceptance time are ignored, so a later presentation in the same registry cannot displace an accepted one (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_presentation_registry_binds_proxy_grant, L6271 @80f77b73b). The incepter must be the Issuee (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, vetBinds, L714 @80f77b73b). Every update from the head back to the latest non-vacuous one must carry exactly one disclosure, and the non-vacuous one must bind the `grant` SAID. The binding event must be sealed in the Issuee's KEL: "Presentation timing requires the Grant to carry this bup's KEL source." (keripy acdc/regeventing.py, vetBinds, L750 @80f77b73b). And the vetted registry must be the declared one; a valid registry cannot be substituted (keripy tests/acdc/test_ipexing.py, test_ipex_v2_rejects_substituted_presentation_registry, L6301 @80f77b73b). Every permanent error ends in rejection: "Reject any malformed, missing, or invalid presentation factor." (keripy acdc/ipexing.py, IpexHandler.verify, L616 @80f77b73b). Nothing falls back to a weaker factor.

### 7.4 The three-factor framing, with all three legs now [K] {#three-factor-framing}

#1618 states symmetrically that three authentication factors apply to the presentation and not only to issuance: "Grant is signed (normal)", "Grant is anchored in a presentation registry (issuee-controlled; `rd` on the origin ACDC can force the check)", and "Grant is anchored in a KEL without a presentation registry (a case V1 did not really specify)". At `b8f60166b` the KEL-anchor leg was [K] and the presentation-registry leg was not ([section 7.5](#statement-2026-09-15)). At `80f77b73b` the presentation-registry leg is [K] too (`raw/16` section 3). #### Three: ~~nothing distinguishes an Issuee-controlled registry from an Issuer-controlled one~~, superseded 2026-09-15 {#retained-negative-three}

## 8. Reading this adversarially {#adversarial}

What a hostile reviewer will reach for, stated here so that none of it is a surprise.

### The origin document has had no engagement {#no-engagement}

The origin document has had no engagement — and the argument about what that silence meant has itself been corrected. #1095 is eleven months old with zero comments, and that part is unchanged. ~~What followed it here, written 2026-09-01: #1627 — Sam's newest architecture discussion — does not mention presentation or usage registries at all, so on the written corpus alone whether the idea was settled, parked or quietly superseded could not be determined, and the question was closed only by a first-hand account of a call.~~ Superseded 2026-09-15. #1627 does mention presentation registries, in a comment by SmithSamuelM dated 2026-09-02, answering a direct question about what an `ax` anchor applies to: "This anchor in the KEL only applies when their is not a presentation registry being used by DAG ACDCs" (the misspelling is his). PR #1674 then said in writing, on 2026-09-11, that presentation-registry anchoring "will come in a follow up PR". The conclusion did not change; its evidence class did. What this chapter asserted a fortnight ago on the strength of an unquotable conversation now rests on two written, attributable, dated sources, and the chapter no longer needs the call in order to say the design is alive.

#### The narrower statement survives {#narrower-statement}

The narrower statement survives, and is still informative. #1627's body, through its last edit of 2026-09-11, contains zero occurrences of "presentation registry", "usage registry" or "issuee registry". Every mention in that discussion is in the comment stream. So Sam's own survey of how to shape an ACDC presentation does not reach for a presentation registry when laying out the tradespace, and names one only when asked a direct question about anchoring. That is a weaker silence than the one recorded on 2026-09-01, not an absent one. The lesson the corpus drew stands, and is better supported than when it was drawn: a dead discussion thread is not evidence of a dead idea.

#### Provenance note on how this was known before it was citable {#first-hand-provenance}

The first-hand report is retained, and demoted to corroboration. On a call on 2026-09-01 Sam named observers, registrars and presentation registries together as things that need to be built (Daniel Hardman, first-hand, same day). It remains a class of source this corpus otherwise does not use and the weakest in form — no recording, no transcript, not even approximate wording, unlike `raw/14`'s edited captions — usable for status and intent only, never as a quotation and never as evidence about a design detail. What it was load-bearing for on 2026-09-01, two written sources now carry independently. Worth noticing in that direction: the corpus held a true claim on evidence it could not cite, until written sources caught up with it — which is the argument for recording a weak-provenance class explicitly rather than either using it silently or refusing it.

#### The novelty claim is unverified {#novelty-claim}

"nobody else does as far as I've never seen anybody do it" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 14:40) and "AFAIK, only KERI/ACDC provides such protection" (#1095) are both explicitly hedged by their author. Holder-side presentation logging is not an unheard-of idea in the wider VC world; what is distinctive here is that the log is cryptographically required by the credential and enforced by the verifier, rather than being a courtesy audit trail. That narrower claim is defensible; the broad one has not been checked.

#### Two trusted-third-party concessions {#ttp-concessions}

The trusted-third-party concession is real, and there are two of them, not one. The first is in [section 1](#asymmetry), in Sam's own words: Verifier-enforced protection against Issuee impersonation "violates the maxim that security not be dependent on a trusted third party," justified by localized harm and by the Verifier's own incentive to protect itself. The second is distinct, and it lands on the mechanism this chapter is about. #1613 describes what a presentation registry gives the Grantor as "an Issuer-Verifier-protected mechanism to detect compromise of its signing keys" — protected at both ends by other parties. It needs an Issuer that is a different party and does not collude, because that is the only reason an imposter holding the Grantor's keys cannot simply issue itself a credential demanding nothing ([section 3](#two-jobs)). And it needs a Verifier that enforces: "The verifier then enforces the protection by not accepting presentations (grant messages) that are not so anchored." So the mechanism offered to an Issuee as its own self-protection is two-party-dependent at both ends. Neither concession is an oversight — both are stated plainly by their author, which is why they belong here rather than in a critic's mouth — but a reviewer who quotes the no-trusted-third-party maxim against this design is right about the tension, and the corpus's residual on that maxim should name the second relaxation as well as the first.

#### The self-issuance hole {#self-issuance-hole}

Fraud protection has a self-issuance hole that its own author documents. See [section 3](#two-jobs). An attacker with the Grantor's keys can self-issue a credential that requires nothing. The mitigation is that high-stakes credentials come from third-party Issuers — which is true and is also an admission that the property lives in the deployment pattern, not the mechanism.

#### Refusals no specification states {#unspecified-refusals}

New 2026-10-03: the reference implementation refuses `grant`s for reasons no specification states. At `80f77b73b` keripy's V2 IPEX verifier rejects four kinds of `grant` (`raw/16` section 8 item 13): one whose ACDC declares an Issuee registry that does not bind it; one carrying an ACDC with an `a`-section `rd` and no top-level `rd`, which is v1-legal as a hidden state registry; one carrying a registry-backed ACDC whose `a` is compact; and one carrying a registry-backed ACDC with any undisclosed `A` entry. All four are [K], none is [N] or [N1.1], and the last two follow from the first, because a verifier cannot tell an omitted requirement from a hidden one. A peer implementation built from either specification line will produce `grant`s keripy rejects, with no normative text to explain why. A hostile reviewer will call that a conformance hazard, and is right to. The mitigating facts are that the handler is not yet in keripy's shipped path ([section 7](#keripy-verifier)) and that implementation-first is how `E1E` went as well (`raw/09` section 11). Neither changes what an interoperating implementer faces.

#### Code and design document disagree {#code-vs-design}

And the code and its design document disagree, in two places, about what a Grantor must do. #1613 conditions the requirement on the Issuee being the Grantor and requires every Grantor to endorse; keripy applies the requirement unconditionally and accepts a registry binding in place of an endorsement ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram), open questions 10 and 11). The code's position is defensible on its face. But a reviewer reading #1613 will expect checks the code does not make and will miss a refusal the code does make, and neither document says the other has moved.

## 9. Open questions {#open-questions}

### Question 1 (answered): ~~Is this live?~~ {#q-live}

Answered: yes, and as of 2026-09-15 answerable from written sources. ~~The answer rested on a first-hand report of a call on 2026-09-01, and on the observation that #1627 does not mention presentation registries at all.~~ Both halves of that basis are superseded ([section 8](#adversarial)): Sam wrote about presentation registries in a #1627 comment on 2026-09-02, and PR #1674 stated on 2026-09-11 that presentation-registry anchoring "will come in a follow up PR". The eleven-month silence on #1095 is real and still does not reflect the design's status. What remains open is everything below, none of which those sources address. 2026-10-03: past "live" and into "partly built". keripy `80f77b73b` implements the verifier side for `grant` ([section 7](#keripy-verifier)), and #1098 uses the possibility of a presentation registry as a premise in an unrelated design decision ([section 1](#asymmetry)).

#### Question 2: What is it called? {#q-name}

"Issuee usage registry" (#1095), "presentation registry" (#1613, #1618), "user presentation registry" (KERIConf26 caption, Samuel Smith, The Digital Identity Tradespace, 14:36). #1550's Guardianship for SEDI uses the same primitive for agent capability control, with a diagram legend reading `rd = issuee usage registry SAID` — so the general thing is not about presentation. The case for the usage name got stronger this pass: #1627 proposes a fourth job, a "transfer registry where a state change is not to revoke an ACDC but to provide a forward reference" to a replacement, and the ACDC spec's own `rd` gloss already lists four purposes. Four uses, one of which is presentation, and the name of the whole is the name of that one. The general name should probably be the usage one, with "presentation registry" reserved for the IPEX-anchoring application. 2026-10-03: still open, and the reference implementation has picked the narrow name ([Status](#two-names)): "presentation registry", "issuee-controlled registry", "Issuee Presentation Registry SAID", and nowhere "usage registry". The code also scopes the mechanism to its narrow use, since the only registry an Issuee writes through the new facade binds a `grant` SAID with state `"presented"`. Code names do not bind a specification, but they set the vocabulary most implementers will meet first, which makes settling the general name more urgent, not less (`raw/16` section 8 item 1). 2026-10-15: Sam's own August description fits the general reading, since it defines the thing by what it records, "merely logging presentations", rather than by the IPEX use ([section 1](#rotate-not-revoke)).

#### Question 3: Does `rd`-in-`a` get disambiguated, and how? {#q-disambiguation}

EGF-declared purpose, restriction to presentation registries in `v1.1`, or left ambiguous. Unresolved in #1613 and blocking any normative text. Still open, and now with a dated negative behind it: at `v1.1` @`2362e48a1` the relevant spec text is byte-identical to `main` @`f0bd097de`, so none of the three resolutions has been taken up in the branch most likely to have carried one ([section 4](#trigger)). What [section 4](#trigger) also newly records raises the stakes — the two readings are mutually exclusive per ACDC and the choice is permanent, so this is not a labelling question. Tracked as [chapter 08, section 14, question 7](08-presentation-architectures-and-ipex.md#q-rd-restriction). (Link corrected 2026-10-15: it pointed at chapter 08's question 21 under a wrong section number.) 2026-10-03: resolved in keripy, still open in the specifications. At `80f77b73b` an `a`-section `rd` with a top-level `rd` is a presentation registry, and one without is refused as an unsupported hidden Issuer registry ([section 4](#trigger), [K]). Interoperability now depends on the specifications adopting that rule or rejecting it explicitly.

#### Question 4: Does the exchange need its own `rd`? {#q-exchange-rd}

#1618 says an anchored exchange "would also need an rd on the exchange," which implies a field addition to `exn` that #1613 does not account for. 2026-10-03: answered "no" in keripy, still open in the specifications. No field on the `grant` names a registry; it is found from the DAG, as #1613 requires ([section 4](#trigger)).

#### Question 5: What is the latency budget? {#q-latency}

Registry write inside KRAM's window, plus a deliberate verifier delay. No number anywhere — and worse than it looked, since [section 6](#registrar-and-kram) records that the registry anchor is additional to the signature or KEL anchor rather than replacing either. 2026-10-03: keripy has chosen a timing rule, and it is looser than "must pass KRAM" ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram)). The registry check runs after KRAM and accepts a binding whose Issuee-asserted TEL `dt` is no later than the receiver's KRAM acceptance time; the KEL event anchoring it may arrive later through escrow. A compromised signer could backdate `dt`, but the backdated TEL event would then be anchored in the real Issuee's KEL and detectable, which is the point of the mechanism. So the looseness may be acceptable, but nobody has written that argument down (`raw/16` section 8 item 5). Still no number, and `raw/16`'s reading of the code reports nothing implementing #1095's deliberate delay.

#### Question 6: What would `rip` spec text say? {#q-rip-text}

The current wording binds a registry to "the Issuer AID" (ACDC spec > Registry SAID Field @f0bd097de); for a presentation registry the incepting AID is the Issuee. ~~No longer only a wording question: keripy at `b8f60166b` enforces that reading and refuses the arrangement outright ([section 7](#keripy-verifier)), so a spec fix now implies a code fix, and the two have to be scoped together.~~ 2026-10-03: the code problem is gone and the wording problem remains. keripy `80f77b73b` scopes the Issuer-equality check to the top-level `rd` and checks the Issuee as incepter on the presentation path ([section 5](#normative)). The spec still says "the Issuer AID".

#### Question 7: Who verifies, and against what? {#q-who-verifies}

The design says the Grantee must check the anchor. Against the Issuee's Registrar directly, or via an Observer? [Chapter 05, section 9](05-acdc-and-verifiable-data.md#registries) records that Observers are verifier-controlled and watch registrar state — which is exactly the shape this needs — but no source connects them. PR #1674 pairs the two absences without connecting them either, naming "presentation-registry anchoring or observer-based TEL retrieval" as the follow-up work. 2026-10-03: half answered. The Grantee checks it, against a TEL it must already hold locally; retrieval has not arrived, and the tests preload the foreign TEL to "Simulate the future observer retrieval layer" ([section 7.3](#still-absent)). The Observer connection is still unbuilt, and still no written source connects the two.

#### Question 8: Two incompatible bespoke origin ACDCs share a name {#q-two-bespoke-origins}

New: two incompatible bespoke origin ACDCs share a name. #1613 requires the self-issued bespoke origin to use a blinded state registry for its Issuer authentication, or the correlation resistance a presentation registry buys is handed back at the origin node ([section 3](#two-jobs)). #1627's SEDI "recipe" pattern specifies the opposite — no registry, no anchor, bare attached signatures, because it is one-time use. Both are Sam, three weeks apart, and both are coherent for their own purpose; they are two different bespoke ACDCs for two different jobs. But neither document says the other exists, and the shared name invites the wrong one. Any worked example that combines a presentation registry with a #1627-style recipe origin is wrong in a way neither source warns about — it yields a correlatable presentation and a `grant` whose perpetual verifiability the origin ACDC cannot carry. 2026-10-03: unchanged. keripy constructs neither kind of bespoke origin together with a presentation registry ([section 3](#two-jobs)).

#### Question 9: keripy refuses `grant`s for reasons no specification states {#q-unspecified-refusals}

New 2026-10-03 (`raw/16` section 8 item 13). Four [K] refusal grounds — an unbound declared registry, an `a.rd` with no top-level `rd`, a compact `a` on a registry-backed ACDC, an undisclosed `A` entry on one — have no [N] or [N1.1] counterpart ([section 8](#adversarial)). The open question is which side moves: the specifications adopting the rules, or keripy relaxing the ones a peer cannot be expected to know.

#### Question 10: The code's requirement is unconditional, and #1613's is not {#q-unconditional}

New 2026-10-03 (`raw/16` section 8 item 11). #1613 applies the anchoring requirement "when the Issuee is the Grantor of that IPEX" (#1613 > Registry Anchored Granted ACDCs). keripy applies it to every declaration in the granted DAG, whoever sends ([section 7](#keripy-verifier)). The two agree when a holder presents its own credential. They differ for #1613's Anchoring Requirement Complication section, where "an ACDC is presented as evidence, but the Issuee AID is unrelated to the Discloser's AID". Under keripy's rule nobody can present such an ACDC unless its Issuee writes this `grant`'s SAID into its registry. That may be intended — it is the strongest reading of "an imposter … can't avoid the requirement" — but it makes a presentation-registry ACDC unusable as third-party evidence, and neither #1613 nor the code says whether that was weighed. A scoping question, not a bug report.

#### Question 11: Single versus double authentication for a non-sender Issuee {#q-single-vs-double}

New 2026-10-03 (`raw/16` section 8 item 12). #1613 says every Grantor must endorse and that a registry-anchored `grant` is "always double-authenticated"; keripy since `b7c170f93` accepts a proxy-sent `grant` whose Issuee participates only through the registry binding ([section 6](#registrar-and-kram)). Deliberate, and defensible on its face, but the design document and the code now disagree about what a Grantor is, and the endorser index does not list registry-only Issuees, so business logic that finds a holder's presentations through `eidx` will miss them. Open: whether #1613's text should move to match the code, or the reverse.

#### Question 12: Fail-closed discovery may rule out selective aggregate disclosure {#q-aggregate-disclosure}

New 2026-10-03, unverified: fail-closed discovery may rule out selective aggregate disclosure of a registry-backed ACDC (`raw/16` section 8 item 14). For `a` the cost is small, since tests compact nested blocks and keep the top level expanded. For `A`, rejecting any undisclosed aggregate entry appears to rule out selective disclosure altogether. The SEDI Age credential has both a top-level `rd` and an `A`, and no IPEX test at `80f77b73b` presents it. `raw/16` did not check how keripy represents an undisclosed aggregate element. If it is not a mapping, an Age credential cannot be selectively presented through keripy IPEX at this pin, which would collide with the age-proof design in `raw/11` and #1098.
